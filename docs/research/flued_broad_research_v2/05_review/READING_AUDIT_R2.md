## 3. Requested high-risk adjudications and safe citations

### 3.1 V18: cancellation of weights is not identification of retrieval treatment

[Original 2610.02070v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.02070v1), Eq.5/§3.1 and App.C.2; card L7–11. The card's principal warning is correct:

1. Assignment chooses `E_t`, but Eq.5 retrieves `top-(B-k) union E_t` (source L345–368). An item in the deterministic part may be retrieved when not randomly assigned. Distinct context size may be below B.
2. Eq.6 and C.2 define `Z_m(t)=1[m in R_t]`, the **realized retrieval** indicator (L380, 1248). Balanced assignment fixes assigned appearances; it does not establish the printed equality between assigned count and sum of realized indicators (L1250).
3. For any constant `e` strictly between zero and one, each self-normalized nonempty-arm ratio algebraically reduces to its **realized** sample mean. That cancellation does not require balancing and does not make assigned denominators correct. It also does not make the realized indicator independent of query/background or identify a desired causal contrast.
4. H.1 explicitly reports 90 inclusions/zero exclusions and an undefined estimate (L2200–2206). This is a concrete counterexample to a blanket both-arm guarantee **for this union design**. It is not a refutation of randomized exposure in designs that genuinely randomize the relevant treatment with suitable backgrounds and support.
5. C.2's exact variance additionally invokes independent draws with a fixed store (L1282–1302). A fixed store alone is not a general independence theorem. For dependent draws, covariance must be considered; do not automatically inherit the source's assertion that its expression is always a lower bound for evolving stores without checking covariance signs.

**Safe citation:** “The paper proposes randomized retrieval exposure and reports better required-memory discrimination in its tested oracle-enriched pools, while its union design leaves some realized treatments without an exclusion arm; unbiasedness/exact-variance claims require reconciled assignment, realized support, background and independence assumptions.” Do not write “all CMP estimates are invalid,” “a Hájek estimator is inherently biased,” or “query utility establishes an eviction/retention policy.” `k=B` removes deterministic slots but still does not remove interaction/background and target-estimand obligations.

## 4. Mediation, supermodularity and utility assumptions

### 4.1 Four cells do not establish a universal structural law

V19's interaction is an exact finite difference for four specified entry interventions. A positive value means super-additivity on **that cell/background/metric**; a negative one can reflect substitution, saturation, changed downstream trajectories, or their combination. It does not uniquely identify redundancy. It also does not establish global supermodularity (increasing marginal gains over every relevant nested set/background), adaptive increasing returns over every observed history, or unique causal mediation shares. Reversing a score or changing the background can change the sign. The already-read scope/R1 restrictions on hybrid-state validity and oracle/probe permissions remain applicable.

For SAGE, [original v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.00668v2) Supp.D L862–872 explicitly says practical **marginal** missing-feature sampling changes the game from the **conditional** theoretical game. The convergence theorem L363–368 requires the correct conditional distribution and asymptotic data/inner/outer samples. This is not a reader-imposed defect or a reason to call Shapley theory false. Additivity of allocated scores is an allocation identity; it does not make a nonlinear coalition value or adaptive action sequence additive. Visual p.8 confirms SAGE does not win every reported cell: BRCA **.8611 < .8718** univariate; credit **.9565 < .9571** permutation.

### 4.2 Preserved legacy sign restriction; independent algebra versus unavailable original

The reused W0483 card L7–10 identifies weak/pointwise substitute assumptions and reports a sign problem in *Informational Substitutes* [1703.08636v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.08636v1), Theorem 5.3.1 proof. The original page itself was **not available to this audit**. Do not describe the printed-sign allegation as newly confirmed here.

The elementary correction is independently checkable: `I(E;B|A) = H(B|A) - H(B|E,A)`, not its negative. For finite variables with `B independent of A conditional on E`, this becomes `H(B|A)-H(B|E)`. Adding another conditionally independent observation C reduces the first term on average while leaving `H(B|E,A,C)=H(B|E)`; this supports **substitute/diminishing information gains under these premises**, not a universal supermodularity claim. Thus a reported reversed entropy sign need not make the theorem's intended conclusion false. Exact quantifiers involving degraded signals and the complete original proof are not independently certified by this argument.

A separate, reader-constructed applicability counterexample: independent fair A,B, target `E=A XOR B`, 0/1 decision reward. Baseline/one-read optimal expected reward is **1/2**, two-read reward **1**; single gains are zero and joint gain **1/2**. For logarithmic information value, the corresponding joint gain is **1 bit**, not 1/2. The signals are independent unconditionally but not conditionally on E; this does not contradict a conditional-independence substitute theorem. Neither this one complementary pair nor a positive mediation cell proves a global increasing-returns property.

Reuse W0303 at [1003.3967v5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1003.3967v5), actual card L7–13: adaptive submodularity is conditional diminishing returns at each positive-probability partial history, not ordinary set submodularity or unconditional feature independence. Keep its revised squared-log coverage bound and cost/truncation qualifications; these proof/version details are inherited, not freshly verified. W0313's actual card L7/15–17 at [2610.21919v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.21919v1) reports a claimed zero-gap limit from an `exp(-gamma)` bound. ### 4.3 Local contribution, continuation and supported policy value

- **W0301, simulated metareasoning:** source L88–140 explicitly distinguishes stop-after-one myopic value, information value without computation cost, and optimal continuation; L506–555 charges online selection and amortizes offline fit. Correct nuisance models under support/consistency conditions can remove bias; model-based predictions off support are extrapolations, not recovered counterfactual truth. Do not impose the variance theorem's stationary-logger simplification on every bias result, or infer a future-retention policy from one-step contextual rewards.
- **W0317, clipped counterfactual risk:** source L125–224 uses a stationary stochastic logger, transformed losses in **[-1,0]**, covering-number and sample conditions; the clipping upper-bound argument relies on the sign. Variance control is not identification of unsupported actions. OPT is a distribution-aware dynamic policy, not merely the best fixed arm. This is a useful stronger benchmark but does not model fact-pair payoff depending on prior selected content without additional state/assumptions.

**Safe common citation:** “These works distinguish local scores, coalition attribution and continuation-aware policy outcomes under different support, loss, distribution and budget assumptions.” No confidence interval, inverse propensity formula or arm ledger by itself makes unchosen actions observed, a myopic label terminal utility, or a current-query signal future retention value.
