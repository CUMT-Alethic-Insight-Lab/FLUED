### P1-01 — disclose the actual split-law limitation, not only nominal versus realized expansion

**Affected final locations:** R L968–970 and L978; O L56–74 artifact table; V L33. R names “Dirichlet–multinomial split allocation” among the techniques and correctly says unsplittable choices do not change the tokens, but never connects that fact to the source's claim that the two methods share the same split-count law. O does not list the resulting source/implementation clarification. This is an omitted material qualification, **not** an explicit assertion in R that the laws are equal.

**Evidence:** [latest claim rules](RECENT_ORIGINAL_CLAIM_RULES.md), L32–41; [T02 card](../03_reading/V17/BR-R2-T02.md), L11–13; actual [2604.16037v1 PDF](source_staging/R3_BR-R2-T02__2604.16037v1.pdf), Algorithm1 p.18 versus Appendix F p.21. The latter says the correspondence is exact and constructs STOCHASTOK-UNI to follow STOCHASTOK's split-count distribution. The printed algorithm permits skipped attempts.

For canonical `[a,bc]`, vocabulary containing `a,b,c,bc`, and one attempt, the printed algorithm yields realized counts `(0,0)` or `(0,1)`, each with probability 1/2. `DirMult(1,(1,1))` instead yields `(1,0)` or `(0,1)`, each with probability 1/2. Renormalizing that latter law onto feasible split counts yields `(0,1)` with probability one, still not the attempt algorithm. I independently checked the finite arithmetic and the two printed passages; no authors' code was inspected.

**Minimum author fix:** add after R L970, with a link to the latest claim rules:

> The claimed split-count correspondence is idealized: Algorithm1 can skip unsplittable selections, so Appendix F's exact Pólya/Dirichlet–multinomial law and matching realized split distribution require successful-split/feasibility assumptions or implementation clarification. This does not overturn the reported task cells.

Add one O artifact row naming skip/cap/feasible-count handling and realized distributions. No need to repeat the toy example in E or reread the whole public paper. V should not imply all latest source restrictions were integrated before this repair. The empirical support-versus-uniformity controls remain valuable; the omission matters if their mechanism is attributed to matched allocation rather than actual support/exposure.
