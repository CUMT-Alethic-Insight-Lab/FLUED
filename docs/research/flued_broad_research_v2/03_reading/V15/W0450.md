# W0450 — DRAGIN: Dynamic Retrieval Augmented Generation based on the Information Needs of Large Language Models

**Status: FULL, assigned arXiv:2403.10081v3, 21 September 2024**, [original](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.10081v3); local `sources/W0450__2403.10081v3.pdf`, SHA-256 `02807e9427f7e24093b1ce3e9f3be63b671cc4e218ff04e682c86b6475e63bf3`. Main and Apps. A–F read; pp.7/15 Tables 2/3/8/9 checked visually. The work is cited as ACL 2024, but this card reports the pinned preprint and does not assume identical proceedings bytes.

**Problem → solution.** Fixed-cadence retrieval wastes calls or misses a changing information need. Borrowing model entropy, self-attention and lexical BM25, DRAGIN triggers a search when the product of vocabulary entropy, maximum later-token attention to an earlier token, and a non-stopword indicator exceeds a threshold; a query comprises highest-attended earlier tokens in original order. The LLM generates a provisional continuation first, then truncates at the detected point, inserts three retrieved passages and regenerates (§3). It needs logits and last-layer attention; no extra model fitting or gold answer at runtime. 'Later' attention is available only after speculative generation, not an illegal future ground-truth token; online rollback and regeneration cost must be charged. Query-token address, 100-token Wikipedia passage content and generated reasoning are different units. Later retrieval replaces earlier passages; there is no persistent-source update/deletion contract.

**Comparators and failures (Tables 2–7; App. D).** 1,000 2Wiki/Hotpot/StrategyQA and 954 answerable IIRC samples, three Llama/Vicuna hosts, greedy decoding, top-3 BM25. For Llama-13B Hotpot, EM/F1 .314/.4238 vs single-round .263/.3706 and FLARE .180/.2756. **Negative cells:** 13B IIRC single-round .196/.2303 beats DRAGIN .185/.2221; 7B StrategyQA no-retrieval .659 beats DRAGIN .641. Thus abstract's all-task superiority and prose's 'single-round consistently boosts' overstate Table 2. Under matched query, trigger comparison on IIRC favors DRAGIN among dynamic baselines, but is not equal-cost oracle control. Table 3 DRAGIN Llama-13B uses 2.631/3.505/4.786/2.829 retrievals per question on 2Wiki/Hotpot/StrategyQA/IIRC, while FLARE uses 1.592/3.378/.625/5.521; not always fewer. App. D gives an example where relevant retrieved passage is present but Llama-7B answers incorrectly: source hit is not deployed use.

**Reproducibility concern.** Visually checked Appendix Table 9 gives Llama-13B Hotpot θ=1.2, but Table 5 labels the same model/dataset and lists θ=.6 result .304/.3931, matching main Table 2's **2Wiki** values, rather than Hotpot .314/.4238. It appears a source table/label inconsistency, not enough to identify the true threshold; do not silently repair. Search/index construction, retrieval latency, speculative generation, repeated prompting, hard call-cap failures, self-confident wrong answers and irrelevant extra-read denominators are not fully reported. No theoretical claim of calibrated omission detection follows.

**Independent tests.** Source-free state retention or query/readout improvements remain competitors, not excluded by this paper. At same full call/latency budget, compare always/no retrieval, strong flat retrieval, entropy and confidence policies, and explicit evidence replacement. Stratify confident misses, false alarms, absent/corrected evidence, and count failures plus search/generation/index costs. Reversal: confident misses make its signal uninformative, equal-cost flat search matches it, or swaps show answer insensitivity. Directions R.06–R.08, R.13–R.16, C.18–C.20, D.03–D.04.
