## Problem and solution
Learning a policy from partial logged feedback needs more than an unbiased value estimate: badly explored actions have large importance-weight variance. **Solution:** minimize clipped inverse-propensity empirical risk plus an empirical standard-deviation penalty and train a stochastic exponential-family policy (POEM), using an iterative majorization decomposition (§§3–5). Borrowed tools: importance sampling, empirical Bernstein bounds, structural-risk regularization and stochastic gradient optimization.

The log has `(x_i,y_i,δ_i,p_i)`, drawn under stationary stochastic logger h₀ with `p_i=h₀(y_i|x_i)`; only the chosen structured output has a loss. Its estimator is the sample mean of `δ_i min(M,h(y_i|x_i)/p_i)` and penalizes `sqrt(sample variance/n)`. Loss is shifted/scaled to [−1,0], so clipped expectation upper-bounds true risk under the relevant support; arbitrary nonnegative losses can make avoiding observed actions a degenerate solution (§4.1). Theorem 1 assumes iid contexts/actions, n≥16, bounded transformed losses and complexity via covering numbers; proof invokes empirical Bernstein and the clipping upper-bound sign. M and penalty are selected using held-out data: clipping trades bias against variance, too-small M or too-large penalty degenerates. No support on target actions means counterfactual learning can remain suboptimal even with infinite data (§4.3). Fixed logged loss is not a rerun under a changed continuation policy or a sequential credit/retention estimator.

Here `x` is a document's feature vector, `y` a multilabel bitvector and feedback Hamming loss, not a source address, memory capacity or byte-causal channel. The stochastic logger is learned from a 5% fully labeled subset; four passes generate logged actions; 25% of log is validation using the *unclipped* counterfactual estimator, followed by fully labeled test evaluation, ten runs (§6). This privileged source supervision and replay cost belong in the ledger.

## Findings and limits
Visually checked Table 3: stochastic POEM vs stochastic IPS Yeast loss **4.517 vs 4.614**, LYRL **0.996 vs 1.118**, but supervised fully labeled CRF is better still (2.822 and 0.222 respectively); batch and stochastic optimization yield different absolute values. Authors report significant per-dataset improvements against corresponding IPS variant. Table 4 shows POEM stochastic faster than batch POEM on their CPU validation runs, but extra variance regularization remains costly versus stochastic IPS (e.g. TMC 276.13 vs 49.12 s per validation run). Fig. 3's near-deterministic logging loses improvement and approaches logger's MAP predictor, not proof that lack of exploration can be cured statistically. Expected stochastic-policy loss and MAP loss differ (Table 5).

**Directions:** R.14–R.16, D.11. Independent non-revisit alternatives: better fixed state and native query; fixed budget direct computation; prospectively randomized interventions. A target hypothesis changes if a held-out, supported policy improves **actual rollout** utility after all collection, teacher, scoring, selection and deployment costs; compare tuned IPS, doubly robust and the logger. Trace off-support actions as unknown and measure continuation policy changes rather than assuming logged action rewards transfer unchanged.
