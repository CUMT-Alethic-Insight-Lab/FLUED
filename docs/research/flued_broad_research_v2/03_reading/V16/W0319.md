## Problem and technique
Online selection can exhaust money, time or inventory before horizon, and the best fixed arm can be much worse than an adaptive budget-aware policy. **Solution:** learn reward and multidimensional consumption while allocating scarce resources with confidence bounds and either balanced exploration of feasible mixtures or multiplicative-weight primal–dual prices. Borrowed methods include stochastic multiarmed bandits, linear-program relaxation, upper/lower confidence bounds, dual optimization and information-theoretic lower bounds (§§2–6).

In the main model a finite set of arms has unknown, fixed per-arm joint outcome distribution, iid over pulls, scalar reward and d consumptions bounded [0,1]; only chosen arm's outcome is revealed. Stop on first budget violation; the violating round's reward is excluded. Finite time is another resource. Comparator OPT is an optimal dynamic policy knowing the latent distributions, not just the best fixed arm. Each action/pull and its observed reward/consumption is the unit: no retrieved fact, latent-state address, causal source intervention or generated-token cost is built in. Reward accumulates additively by assumption; complementary content whose payoff depends on earlier reads requires an augmented state/model and does not automatically satisfy these results.

## Theorem and main proof ingredients
Theorem 5.3 gives, up to stated logarithmic and additive terms, regret scaling `sqrt(m·OPT) + OPT·sqrt(m/B)`, with m arms and minimum resource budget B. §3 constructs a fractional packing LP whose value upper-bounds even the distribution-aware dynamic OPT via a dual-cost supermartingale and optional stopping. The primal–dual algorithm selects the highest optimistic reward per lower-confidence estimated resource cost, then multiplicatively updates resource prices; the proof combines the LP inequality, expert-update bound and accumulated estimation-error bounds (§5, App. C). The lower bound (§6) adds the standard multiarmed-bandit difficulty to a construction where arms have identical reward but one has slightly lower Bernoulli resource consumption; a KL change-of-measure and stopping-time argument limits how fast the better arm can be found. The theorem's iid, bounded rewards/consumptions, fixed action set and budget conditions matter; it is not a general guarantee for adversarial/nonstationary workloads.

The paper's counterexamples are important: a round-robin mix across d resource-specific arms can earn d times the reward of the best fixed arm (§1.1), and App. B gives a domain-aware case where the guaranteed bound for BalancedExploration can be stronger. Conversely BalancedExploration's approximate optimization over plausible mixtures has no specific efficient implementation (Remark 4.2); PrimalDualBwK is reported O(md) per round under its computational model. Section 7 handles some continuous-price problems with domain-specific discretization assumptions, not arbitrary infinite actions or arbitrary cost structures. These are theoretical examples and regret guarantees, not measured deployed speedups or empirical LLM results.
