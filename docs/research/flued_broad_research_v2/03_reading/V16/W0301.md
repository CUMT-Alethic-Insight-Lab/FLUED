## Original problem and one-sentence solution
Optimal sequential choice of costly computations is itself intractable; Bayesian metalevel policy search (BMPS) uses Bayesian optimization to learn a policy mixing myopic and perfect-information features against actual simulated episode return (§§2–3). It borrows reinforcement learning, Bayesian optimization, dynamic programming and value-of-information calculations. A metalevel state is a probability distribution over latent environment parameters, actions are belief-updating computations or stop, and each nonterminal computation incurs negative cost; stopping executes the object-level policy best under the current belief. The optimal value of computation includes the optimal continuation, whereas the one-step myopic value assumes stop immediately; nonmyopic complementarity can make two observations jointly useful when either alone is not (§2.2). The learned surrogate is not the exact optimal continuation.

Content is the belief over task outcomes; computation identity/which parameter is sampled is its action address; no persistent relation instances or byte interface are involved. Only observations produced by selected simulations are revealed, though the model assumes known transition/reward structure. Each test is simulated, not a deployed language-model read or causal demonstration of source-content consumption.

## Controls, results, budget and restrictions
Binary stopping uses beta–Bernoulli evidence, horizon 30, 500 Bayesian-optimization iterations × 2,500 episodes and 3,000 test episodes: BMPS at most 5.19% below the computed optimal and 6.78–35.8% above myopic where more than one observation is worthwhile (§4.1). For 2–5 candidate actions and horizon 25, mean return is 0.6535 BMPS, 0.6559 blinkered, 0.6596 optimal, 0.60 myopic, 0.58 DQN, 0.20 full deliberation (§4.2); blinkered is better at the lowest tested costs. BMPS uses 10×1,000 training episodes plus model-selection rollouts; DQN uses 5M steps, hence these are algorithm comparisons, not identical optimization-budget factorials. Tree planning uses a recursively blinkered baseline because independent-action assumption fails; exact optimum is computed only at heights 2–3 (§4.3, supplement).

Tornado application is hypothetical, not field deployment: 24 hours, 10/30 cities, simulation costs varied, 5,000 rollouts; online metareasoning time is charged and offline policy learning excluded/amortized (§5). Supplement explicitly finds that when selection itself is much slower than the computation, metareasoning ceases to pay. Its perfect-information feature can have exponential exact integration cost; known metalevel dynamics and a designed relevance map are material assumptions. The paper's sample-domain near-optimality does not establish calibration under unknown models or utility of unseen actions.

**Directions:** R.13–R.16, E, C/F. Independent no-revisit alternatives: improve closed-state writer/native readout, fixed-budget computation, or learned stopping without a source index. A decisive test logs all selected/unchosen action support, uses matched observed information and an explicit continuation policy, and charges offline fitting, selection, generation and failed calls. Show actual net episode return versus best fixed, myopic and nonmyopic policies under changing cost/workload; nonmyopic advantage only in specific complementary pairs would support the hypothesis without generalizing it to all content access.
