## Problem and mechanism
Logged contextual-bandit feedback only reveals the chosen action's reward, while a new policy chooses different actions. **Solution:** combine direct reward prediction with an inverse-propensity-weighted residual, so policy-value bias depends on the product of reward-model and propensity-model errors (§§2–4). For deterministic target π, per context the estimator is predicted reward for π(x) plus `1[a=π(x)](r−r̂_a(x))/p̂(a|x,h)`. This borrows inverse-probability weighting, outcome regression and supervised cost-sensitive learning. Training can optimize a policy over completed estimated losses (§5.1.3); the analytical theorem concerns evaluation, not a guarantee that optimization recovers the best policy.

The paper assumes iid context/reward vectors from a fixed distribution; the logging action may be randomized and history-adaptive. Its bias analysis fixes nuisance estimates independently of the evaluation sample, e.g. via splitting; the stated variance result assumes stationary logger/propensity estimate (§§3–4). Correct either nuisance gives unbiasedness under those assumptions; near-zero propensity leads to variance, and a model predicting rewards for unsupported target actions requires untestable extrapolation. Double robustness does not create action support or identify future trajectory/retention effects. Units are one context, one discrete action and the one observed scalar reward, not token or relation-instance states; causal policy interpretation also requires consistency and support.

## Positive and negative controls
Nine UCI classification datasets simulate one uniformly sampled label per example with known propensity `1/k`; fully labeled tests supply comparison truth. Table 2 gives letter evaluation RMSE 0.030 DR vs 0.049 IPS, but pendigits **0.016 DR vs 0.015 IPS** and optdigits both 0.023, contradicting a universal per-cell variance improvement. Direct method is strongly biased in this particular linear-outcome-model setup. For policy learning, 70/30 splits repeated 30 times: DR improves DLM/Filter Tree over IPS in displayed Table 3, but Offset Tree beats DR Filter Tree on page-blocks (0.04483 vs 0.05283) and yeast (0.5895 vs 0.59053). Direct loss minimization uses 20 random starts selected by training loss (App. A); not an equal-compute policy search certificate. A separate recorded user-visit dataset has 3,854,689 cookies, artificially subsampled under known sampling rules; authors report mean 13.6% RMSE reduction. It is not a live randomized deployment or a multi-step retention study.

**Directions:** R.14–R.16, D.11. Non-revisit alternatives: explore state updates/native read rather than add a source index, or use directly randomized matched-action trials. A useful decision test logs actual action probabilities and missing/failed outcomes, holds policy and observation regime fixed, fits nuisances on independent data, checks effective sample size/overlap and validates against randomized continuations. A counterexample with absent support or changed downstream continuation makes the old estimator insufficient, not evidence the alternative action has zero utility.
