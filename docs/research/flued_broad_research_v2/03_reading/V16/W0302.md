## Problem and proposed method
Reasoning chains can be expensive even when not useful. **Solution:** score sampled chains by the known-answer log-likelihood gain conditioned on the chain minus a per-generated-token charge, select above-mean examples, then fine-tune via expert iteration (§§2–3). Borrowed techniques are value-of-computation reasoning, rejection sampling and self-training. For problem `x`, gold `y`, chain `z`, reward is `log π(y|x,z) − log π(y|x) − γ len(z)`; target answers label the reward in training and are not an online deployable observation. Algorithm 1 keeps chains with advantage strictly above the question's mean, whereas the introduction says best chain; the algorithm permits multiple above-mean chains. It samples four candidates per problem, γ=0.1, then trains five iterations with growing 1,024-example increments and batch size 16. Direct answer generation and candidate-token reasoning are computations on the same input, not new source information or a tested memory write/read mechanism.

## Evidence, reversals and budget
Section 4 uses 4,096 training questions balanced across ARC, CommonsenseQA, GSM8K and ProofWriter. Baselines include direct and chain-of-thought few-shot prompts, STaR and instruction-tuned models; MMLU-CF is a separate out-of-domain evaluation. Tables 1–5 and Figs. 2–3 were read on the PDF, not inferred from the former HTML extraction. Numbers below are author-reported means and 95% intervals, not independent replications.

- **Table 1 / Fig. 2 (p.6):** across the four datasets, Llama-3.2-3B RaM versus STaR shows accuracy **50.1±1.8 vs 47.4±2.0** and output tokens **170.8±2.9 vs 222.7±3.5**; Llama-3.1-8B gives **63.5±1.7 vs 61.9±1.6**, **146.2±3.3 vs 215.1±3.3**. These means favor RaM on both measures, not a claim of statistical significance for each comparison. Instruction-tuned 3B accuracy is higher at **53.2±1.8** with **259.1±3.4** output tokens; it has extra instruction-tuning permissions.
- **Tables 4–5 (pp.15–16), negative cells:** at 3B, ProofWriter accuracy is **41.3±4.4 RaM vs 43.6±4.5 STaR**, despite shorter output **330.4±7.4 vs 380.0±7.2**. At the 8B label in Table 4, ARC is **80.3±1.5 vs 82.0±1.6** and ProofWriter **52.7±4.0 vs 55.8±4.1**, with shorter outputs in Table 5. Confidence intervals overlap; these are observed numerical reversals, not established significant deficits. Table 4 also lists a **Direct SFT** 3B row with ARC **82.8**, CommonsenseQA **66.0** and GSM8K **41.2**, each higher than RaM's **71.2**, **55.8** and **32.3**; the paper does not describe this comparator's method or report its Table 5 output lengths, so do not infer equal cost or use it as a fully specified matched baseline.
- **Table 2 (p.8):** the easy/hard split is made using a separate direct-few-shot correctness outcome, not actual individual benefit from deliberation. For 3B the hard/easy lengths are **145/72** for RaM and **195/128** for STaR; relative easy-versus-hard gap is **50.3% vs 34.4%**. For 8B it is **36.0% vs 35.9%**, effectively the same printed gap, despite shorter RaM output overall. This group association is not calibrated gold-free routing or a causal individual-necessity test.
- **Table 3 (p.8):** MMLU-CF 3B RaM vs STaR: **29.5±2.5 vs 27.0±2.4** accuracy, **96.3 vs 150.6** tokens. At 8B: **36.4±2.4 vs 40.2±2.4**, **110.6 vs 153.6** tokens. Thus the 8B quality–cost tradeoff is a numerical accuracy decrease of 3.8 percentage points, despite the prose calling performance similar; the intervals overlap and no paired significance test is supplied. Fig. 3 (p.9) plots 3B MMLU-CF subject-level output-length ratios, with variation by subject; it does not show subject-level quality preservation or prove why a subject uses more reasoning.

Source inconsistencies remain: the abstract says 23–45% and three models, but §4.3 and displayed Table 1 identify two base model sizes and §5.1 says 23–32%; Table 5 labels its 8B base block **Meta-Llama-3-8B**, while Tables 1 and 4 label it **Llama-3.1-8B**. Preserve this label mismatch; no separate third base model or correction is established. Inference output-token counts are not full FLOPs, latency or total system cost. Training incurs four sampled trajectories plus gold-label likelihood scoring per question per iteration, fine-tuning and selection overhead; complete cost and realized samples are unknown. No sequential policy-optimality theorem is demonstrated by this surrogate.

**Directions:** R.13–R.16, E/F. Independent no-revisit routes: improve native state/readout or train efficient direct prediction and internal computation; compare against a properly specified direct-SFT control, matched-quality fixed token schedules, STaR and stronger search. A test must compare paired actual continuation outcomes, including erroneous short answers, a gold-free online trigger, equal-source access, all scoring/training costs and workload shifts. Net positive return after charging all costs and an observable calibrated trigger would strengthen the adaptive-computation hypothesis; a matched-budget fixed/direct policy beating it would weaken it. These results do not establish either outcome universally.
