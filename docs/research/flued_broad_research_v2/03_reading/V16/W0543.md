## Problem, solution, mechanism
Ordinary attention uses query–key matches to route object values, while the authors hypothesize that separately emitting learned pairwise relations can improve relational generalization. **Solution:** dual attention concatenates conventional sensory heads and relational heads whose outputs are attended learned pair-comparison vectors plus sender symbols; an MLP then integrates them (§§2–3, Alg. 1). The borrowed techniques are multi-head attention, learned feature comparisons and symbol/pointer-like embeddings; training uses ordinary task supervision or causal next-token prediction, not an explicit relation-object lifecycle objective.

The pair relation `r(x_i,x_j)` is itself content, calculated by separate relation Q/K projections; attention Q/K chooses which sender is weighted. `s_j` tags sender identity via positional, relative-position, or feature-equivalence symbol assignment (§2.3); the last is a learned soft assignment, not a persistent database identifier. Relation vectors and symbols are weighted and summed, not retained as independently addressable relation instances. The next layer can compare representations already containing prior relational outputs (App. B.1), a compositional **relation-between-relations** mechanism; this is not proof of durable relation IDs, roles, provenance, deletion or revocation. Causal decoder masks apply to attention (§3.1); experiments use GPT-2 token units and 1,024-token windows for LM, character units for math and image patches for vision. This is not a fixed-size recurrent-memory evaluation.

## Evidence and countercontrols
- Relational Games: 3×3 image grid with 12×12 object-aligned patches, two-layer models, five seeds, 50 epochs; four tasks use 250–2,500 training examples and match-pattern uses 5,000–25,000 (App. C.1). Standard Transformers at several heads and parameter sizes are controls; DAT is more sample-efficient in the reported curves. Match-pattern compares two triplet patterns, a concrete nested-relation task, not a separate systematic sweep of relation arity, nesting depth, derivation steps and state size. CoRelNet is reported marginally better than DAT, PrediNet/Abstractor generally worse (Fig. 7); symmetry helps these tasks (Fig. 8). App. D reports DAT with relational cross-attention versus the new relational attention largely within error on relational games, but the latter improves the reported TinyStories comparison.
- Math sequence-to-sequence: 2M generated examples per task, 50 epochs, five trials, 85-character alphabet; Table 2 visually checked. Linear-1 at two layers: 783K DAT 66.5% versus 871K Transformer 64.0%; differentiation at two layers: 783K DAT 100.0% versus 871K Transformer 99.9%. The table includes missing entries and near-saturation; these are character accuracy, not exact derivation reliability. Size changes and head composition are not a full mechanism factorial.
- Causal LM: 10B FineWeb-Edu GPT-2 tokens, 24 layers, matched nominal token exposure; App. C.4/Table 5 reports validation perplexity 16.09 versus 16.94 at 343M/353M and 13.43 versus 13.63 at 1.27B/1.31B parameters. DAT also uses grouped-query attention and symbolic attention, so no isolated relation-head causal coefficient or matched total training FLOPs follows. Authors explicitly note current implementation slower without hardware-aware optimization (§5). CIFAR improvement (Table 1) likewise uses grouped-query attention. No end-to-end serving/net-state budget is supplied.

## Theoretical conditions and synthesis
App. A states approximation of a continuous relation to a query-selected element on compact spaces with continuous preference family and near-sure unique separation. Proof selects an almost-one softmax weight, approximates relation by pairwise feature inner products, and bounds the weighted error. The formal proof sets symbol weight to zero and conditions on the well-separated event; it is not identity preservation, finite-precision learnability or arbitrary relation storage. We do not independently certify every invoked approximation theorem.

**Directions:** R.11, A.11–A.14, C.20, F cost. Independent no-revisit candidates include ordinary flat binary relation networks, task-sufficient state/write and native-reader improvements, or supervised relation composition without an external source. Discriminator: same fact pool, strong flat binary baseline, equal parameters/training/physical-state budget, separately vary arity, relation-result nesting, inference depth and retained objects; include identity/role collision and revocation. A matched gain isolated to nesting would favor objectification; comparable gains from ordinary compositional processing would weaken its necessity. No claim of universal attention failure or byte-interface exoneration follows.
