## Problem and operational estimand

**Problem:** an individual component's patching score changes with the background and with other components, so a greedy ranking can miss complementary or backup mechanisms. **One-sentence solution:** decompose clean/corrupt counterfactual effects into component-background and cross-component finite differences and examine groups rather than assuming additivity. Borrowed technique is causal mediation / factorial interaction, used to diagnose a fixed pretrained model, not a training rule for memory. Text input is a causal token prefix; intervention address is head, group, and position; content is cached activations; query is task prompt; output is logit difference. Actual native write/retention is not measured, and activation substitution is not a native reader.

For clean input `a`, corrupt input `b`, mediator `M`, and scalar outcome `Y`, the four observable model evaluations in the paper's structural intervention setup are `Y(a)`, `Y(b)`, `Y(b,M(a))`, and `Y(a,M(b))`. Denoising effect `PIE=Y(b,M(a))-Y(b)`; noising `NIE=Y(a)-Y(a,M(b))`; interaction `INT=Y(a)-Y(b,M(a))-Y(a,M(b))+Y(b)`. Thus `NIE=PIE+INT` and `TE=PIE+PDE+INT` (§3 Prop. 3.1; App. A). The algebra is exact for specified interventions, independent of differentiability; neither outcome class automatically measures exclusive natural information flow. Calling PIE a path-specific effect uses the paper's *particular* residual graph and bypass-edge freezing/subgraph convention, including the relevant cross-world interventions; it is not general identification from ordinary observational text.

§3 Thm. 3.2 and App. A give a local mixed-Hessian approximation with mediator and bypass perturbations. Twice differentiability supports a second-order local expansion; their displayed cubic-remainder derivation additionally invokes stronger smoothness/analyticity. Zero Hessian **at one point** zeros the leading approximation, not automatically the exact finite difference across distant points; exact zero follows if the required region is affine or an intervention perturbation is zero. The paper's broader discussion of piecewise-affine compiled models likewise needs all four hybrid states to stay in an appropriate common affine region. No independent proof verification is claimed.

## Findings, counterexamples and cost

- §3/Fig. 1: mean head-ranking correlations are 0.919 for symmetric IOI swaps and 0.509 for pABC IOI; similar prompts can suppress the very conditional effect one wants to detect. Corruption quality and sensitivity must both be assessed.
- §4/Thm. 4.1 gives a set-function inclusion–exclusion decomposition of joint noising into single-head and higher-order cross-interactions. It is exact on specified interventions, not evidence every subset was searched. On pABC, reported `sum PIE=5.775`, group faithfulness `1.523`, and `sum xINT=-4.045` logit-difference units; sampling and outcome distribution matter.
- §5 uses GPT-2 small, N=1,000 IOI pairs and pABC mean ablation. L9H9 PIE +2.575, INT −2.338, NIE +0.237; its individual pair interactions with any backup reach only 7.1% of its INT, while one backup's group interaction is 3.8× its pair value. These are distinct denominators, not proof all backups interact 3.8×. Ranking by PIE instead misses duplicate/previous-token/induction heads that require clean context. App. B.2 notes backup-group exceptions L11H2 and L9H7. §5.2 proposes, but does not establish, competition of negative name movers and S-inhibition; App. B.3 and the visually inspected Table 3 show all eight measured pair interactions are negative.
- Frozen pretrained host; no new optimizer or training loss in the assay. Evaluation budget is many exact patching forwards and potentially exponential group combinations; full inference/training compute not reported. Input sentence has names/roles and predictions are next-token logits; this is not a long-delay byte memory task.

**Prior directions:** D.07–D.15, D.19; F.08–F.09 for interaction design, not a transfer result. **Rival explanations / minimal discriminator:** stable redundancy, conditional computation, abnormal hybrid state, and generic scale changes can all suppress a single-head score. On fixed matched pairs, report four cells and joint A/B/both interventions with output and non-target checks, contrast several donors and relevant groups, and separately measure downstream response. No finite pair battery proves exhaustive mediation or the architecture's natural mechanism.
