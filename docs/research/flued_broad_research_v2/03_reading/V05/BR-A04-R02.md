**Problem:** a small output drop after an ablation can mask an important component if later computation reacts to the missing contribution. **One-sentence solution:** track direct per-layer logit contributions before and after fixed-weight layer intervention and decompose downstream compensatory response. Borrowed techniques: causal-graph interventions, logit lens and resample ablation, serving mechanistic measurement rather than an extra memory component. The Chinchilla-family 7B autoregressive decoder is pretrained; no new fitting of weights occurs during intervention. Its training excluded dropout, stochastic depth, and layer dropout (§2.5.2).

## Inputs, effect definitions and observations

Input is a Counterfact subject+relation prefix; unit of intervention is a whole attention or MLP layer output at the final token, and donor content comes from 15 alternative dataset prompts (§2.2–2.5, App. A). The measured target is the **intact model's maximum-likelihood token**, not necessarily a verified gold fact; output is centered token logit. Query is the visible prefix, native latent state is the residual stream. No long-term write, time-delayed retention, learned address, or independent ground-truth fact recovery is measured.

- §3.1 defines **total effect** as the output change with the intervention allowed to propagate; **direct effect** holds other graph variables at their original values. With RMSNorm scale fixed, unembedding a layer approximates its direct logit contribution, subject to the donor contribution and the paper's sign convention; it is not a generic raw activation contribution under changing normalization.
- §4.1 defines compensatory effect as the sum of changes in downstream attention and MLP direct contributions, with indices reflecting their ordering. The authors use 1,209 Counterfact contexts, every layer, 15 resampled donors per intervention; output is an intervention-conditioned model response at fixed weights, not parameter adaptation or independent duplicated storage.
- §4.2/Figs. 7–8: effect varies by depth; at layer 23 a regression reportedly explains 92% of downstream direct-effect variance, and the balance shifts from attention changes toward MLP response. This is **variance explained**, not 92% correct recall or 92% recovery. §6 says roughly 70% of the reduction in token logits is restored at middle layers by attention compensation plus reduced MLP erasure. Slopes below one past layer 13 indicate incomplete restoration. Late MLP attenuation could be output recalibration rather than replacement of original features; the paper explicitly calls for feature-level and multi-ablation tests.
- App. A distinguishes average effect across donor resamples from using the mean activation; a naturally occurring donor marginal does not guarantee a natural hybrid joint state. §3.3 gives stylized erasure (`f=-x`) and self-repair cases with indistinguishable output under a knockout, illustrating ambiguity rather than proving an actual learned microcircuit. 'Natural dropout' as a training explanation is explicitly conjectural (§6).

**Prior directions:** D.11–D.14, D.19; F.08–F.09, E.15. **Minimal discriminator:** hold the initial state, prompt and donor pairing fixed; compare intact, single and joint route ablations and record downstream activations and target versus non-target logit changes. A coordinated return of the correct identity after a swap is stronger evidence of content compensation than a generic increase in all logits. Include positive assay and varied donor controls; static duplication and off-distribution response remain rivals.

**Visual completion:** Fig. 7b, p.13, reports `r²=0.92`, slope `0.69` for layer 23, compatible with substantial but incomplete compensation; this is not 92% restored accuracy. Fig. 8, p.14, separates layer-23 attention-response r²=.13 from MLP-response r²=.87; direct/total definitions (pp.9–10) and erasure versus self-repair toy construction (p.12) were visually checked against the original despite glyph extraction corruption. The figure and authors' conjectures do not establish preserved semantic features or broad model generalization. No independent code or proof verification.
