**Problem:** optimization of explanation metrics can be gamed without a corresponding improvement in explanation faithfulness. **One-sentence solution:** exhibit an input-case detector wrapper and label-encoding counterexamples for ERASER and EVAL-X and test them against ordinary saliency methods. Borrowed techniques: BERT classifiers to detect intervention type; likelihood-ratio token selection and surrogate evaluators to encode predictions. These are counterexamples for an evaluator, not a trained recurrent memory or a proposed answer system.

## Mechanism and learning permissions

For label `ŷ`, original `x`, explanation-token subset `e`, and predicted-class confidence `f_ŷ`, the paper defines sufficiency `f_ŷ(e)-f_ŷ(x)` (opposite sign to original ERASER) and comprehensiveness `f_ŷ(x)-f_ŷ(x\\e)` (§3). Higher is better for both here. A new detector per classifier/saliency pair selects among `2|Y|+1` cases: original, explanation-only by predicted label, or removed-explanation by predicted label. It routes inputs it classifies as original to the unchanged classifier/explainer, sets explanation-only predicted-class confidence to 1, and removed-explanation confidence to 0. **Perfect preservation is conditional on correct case detection:** authors say typical inputs are preserved and detector is not always correct (§3.3). Perfect detector yields score sum 1; overlapping supports and detection mistakes limit that construction. Do not state that all inputs or all deletion metrics are proven useless.

The unit is the selected 10% of input tokens for rationale explanations; target is a class label; wrapper queries the class and identifies the input case, not a native memory address or retrieval process. BERT prediction and case detectors are trained for 10 and 3 epochs respectively, batch 32, learning rate 2e−5; LIME, integrated gradients, attention and random selection are compared (§3.2). The model's ordinary input path is untouched only when correctly detected; altered explanation-only/removed inputs are explicitly adversarial interventions.

## Results and counterexamples

- §3.3: Movies mean comprehensiveness gain +0.59 and sufficiency +0.05; BoolQ +0.63 and +0.20, across their model/saliency combinations. On Movies attention comprehensiveness changes 0.18→0.89; BoolQ integrated gradients 0.03→0.73. Optimized random rationales can exceed the unoptimized comparator. Appendix detector accuracy aggregates all cases rather than reporting the untouched-input-specific error; do not infer exact preservation rates or per-method uncertainty.
- §4: independently trained EVAL-X sees randomly masked training examples and labels. With original model predicted label, the attacker selects input tokens with the highest class likelihood ratios, or queries the evaluator / approximates it by four differently seeded models and majority voting. On Movies, likelihood-ratio explanation lengths 10–100 tokens evade the authors' chosen 10% detection gap; 10 tokens already give 83.7% eACC, increasing to 92.5% at larger length (§4.3/Fig. 4 description). Surrogate majority voting crosses both cutoffs from 50 tokens; direct one-token success is conditional on a suitable token being available, not an unconditional existence proof. A single surrogate is lower and more variable (App. B prose).
- The detector, evaluator models, intervention construction, and optimizer steps introduce extra train/query budgets, not a zero-cost modification. The measured label/metric is not native semantic evidence use; a trained evaluator is a different system and cannot by itself certify the original classifier's process.

**Prior directions:** D.07–D.09, D.14–D.17, D.19; D.03–D.04 for content-versus-label distinction. **Minimal discrimination (proposed):** evaluate on untouched inputs and truly held-out intervention families; use random rationales, case-detector error/confusion, arbitrary-target or reversed-label tests and non-target consistency. An independently hidden evaluator reduces access to one exploit but is not a universal proof against correlated-token encoding.
