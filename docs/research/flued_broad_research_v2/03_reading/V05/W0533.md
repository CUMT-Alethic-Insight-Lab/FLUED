## Problem, method and permissions

**Problem:** localization changes with corruption, output metric, and intervention granularity. **One-sentence solution:** compare the same paired input and model under multiple activation-patching operators before interpreting the resulting localization (§§2–6). Borrowed technique: counterfactual activation patching as a causal measurement instrument, not a proposed memory architecture or a new language-model training objective. Models are pretrained and fixed in these analyses; no model optimization or training schedule for this study is specified in the observed methods.

A causal decoder receives an input prefix; content is token identity in factual subjects or IOI names, address is a specified token position and layer/head, readout is next-token probability, logit difference, or KL, and donor activation comes from a second forward pass. These are *inference-time replacements*, not learned writing/retention or a native read request. Clean and corrupt runs are individually valid examples for symmetric token replacement (STR), but their patched hybrid internal state need not be on the natural joint manifold. Gaussian noise (GN) alters subject-token embeddings, with scale 3 times embedding standard deviation; STR swaps equal-length related subjects/names and preserves template (§2.1; App. B). They use 145 paired factual prompts, 500 IOI prompts, and GPT-2 XL/small respectively (§3; App. B). The same STR incorrect answer is used to define the GN logit difference, a necessary fairness detail. PairedFacts is used in both directions. App. B calls 49.0% the average *correct-token probability*, whereas §3 calls it accuracy; retain that source inconsistency rather than converting one to the other.

## Results and limits

- §3/Fig. 2: for factual recall, GN yields a much sharper early/middle-layer peak than STR; across windows the GN peak is 2–5× higher. GN is not always sharper: App. C reports up to 4× sharper STR on addition/subtraction, but similar multiplication. This is sensitivity, not a settled diagnosis that either result is false.
- §3.2/Fig. 3: clean IOI name-mover attention on IO averages 0.58; GN spreads it over IO/S1 0.26/0.21. Restoring S-inhibition values yields normalized logit differences 1.04 STR versus 0.49 GN. The authors regard distribution-shift explanation as tentative. Corruption of S2 vs S1/IO traces different information; §6 explicitly says the latter exposes name movers missed by the former.
- §4: probability saturates near zero and can miss negative contributors. With pABC corruption the original IO probability is about 0.0005 and neither negative mover is detected by that metric; logit difference detects them (§4.2). In §4.1 subject-last/middle effect ratios under STR are 4.33 with probability versus 1.22 with logit difference. Different metrics estimate different behaviors, not interchangeable truth.
- §5/Fig. 5: jointly patching 3/5/10 layers produces peak 1.40/1.75/1.59 times the summed single-layer effects in the specified GN/probability comparison. A window is a **joint** intervention, not localization to its central layer; nonlinear interaction is suggested rather than experimentally isolated. App. D/E add docstring and greater-than observations; the locked PDF text supplies the table cells, although several graphical plots have not been visually inspected.

**Theory versus learned use:** The procedural interpretation depends on intervention scope and output scale; no theorem guarantees natural hybrid activations, comprehensive circuit discovery, or transferred use in other hosts. Head detections depend on a 2-standard-deviation threshold (§3); time/FLOPs and all training resources are not matched or reported here.

**Prior questions:** D.07–D.10, D.14–D.17, C.18; R.13 only as a measurement caution. **Minimal discrimination (proposed):** same held-out paired inputs; clean/corrupt, natural donor, sham, and positive-sensitive interventions, explicit answer changes and non-target integrity; compare raw logit/probability effects and single versus joint patches, not merely two normalized scores. Test one target host before attributing a failure to missing information.
