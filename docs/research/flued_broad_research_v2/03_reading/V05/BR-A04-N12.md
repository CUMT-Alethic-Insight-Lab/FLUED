**Problem:** finite-sample intervention scores can be monitored and sampled adaptively, producing misleading confidence in mechanistic claims. **One-sentence solution:** define the input/intervention population and bounded per-intervention score, then estimate its fixed mean using confidence sequences and bounded-weight adaptive sampling (§§2–3). Borrowed AI/statistical techniques are causal interventions, importance sampling, and test-martingale betting, used as an evaluation layer; CIF does not itself train a language model or construct its causal explanation.

## Estimand, visibility and assumptions

For fixed input law `D`, fixed target intervention law `Π` and score `Y(X,I)∈[0,1]`, the estimand is `θ=E_{D×Π}Y`. For abstraction, bounded disagreement `Z` gives risk `R=E Z` and fidelity `F=1−R`; for patching, `Δ=clip[0,1]((g_patch−g_corrupt)/(g_clean−g_corrupt))`, and `μ=E Δ` (p.212, §2.4). Clipping changes the estimand, hides over-recovery/negative effects, and denominator-zero/near-zero handling is not specified in the displayed definition: report raw gaps and handling, not just the clipped estimate. Inputs may be image pairs plus latent-coordinate masks or clean/corrupt language prompts plus component sets; interventions supply donor activations. This is not ordinary native writing, persistent retention or self-query behavior. A fixed component set corresponds to a point-mass intervention distribution; changing mask probability or target donor distribution changes the claim.

For fixed `n` sampled i.i.d. from `D×Π`, §3.1, p.213 gives Hoeffding half-width `sqrt(log(2/δ)/(2n))`; Bernoulli disagreement also permits exact binomial intervals. This does **not** license repeated unadjusted peeking. A time-uniform alternative uses `δ_n=6δ/(π²n²)` and `b_n=sqrt(log(2/δ_n)/(2n))`, with an all-times union bound; use upper bound on risk for fidelity or lower bound directly on recovery (§3.2, App. C.1–C.2).

For adaptive sampling, choose proposal `q_t=(1−α)Π+α q̃_t` based on history **before** current outcome; `X_t` remains i.i.d. from `D`, `q_t` must cover the support of `Π`, and `w_t=Π(I_t)/q_t(I_t)≤Wmax=1/(1−α)`. The average `w_tY_t` targets the original population; the time-uniform Hoeffding-style radius is `Wmax sqrt(log(2/δ_n)/(2n))` (pp.213–214, Thm. 2 and App. C.3–C.4). The conditional-mean martingale and correctly recorded propensities are essential; a sampler conditional on the *current* input would require the appropriate joint/conditional propensity and support argument, not merely the displayed intervention-only weight. A betting confidence sequence can use bounded `w_tY_t/Wmax` and scale back (§3.4), with predictable bets.

**Selection is separate:** §3.6 p.214 says the basic guarantees apply to a **single prespecified estimand**, not an arbitrary candidate discovered on the same data. For a prespecified finite set, allocate error `δ/K` for simultaneous coverage (or independent held-out evaluation); other sequential selection/FDR schemes require their own valid guarantees. The paper suggests peeling/e-values but does not prove arbitrary adaptive hypothesis selection in its main theorem. Paired comparisons use common samples and range-adjusted differences (§3.5).

## Experiments and restrictions

- E1 (§4.1, pp.215–216; Tables 1–2): MNIST 784→512→512→10 MLP, >98% test accuracy; four pruning/affine approaches, k=64/128/256 and donor-coordinate masks. At p=0.5, none certifies F≥0.90 (5,000 draws). At p=0.1/k=256, soft replacement F̂=.984 certifies F≥.90 in 93 betting versus 1,219 Hoeffding samples; F≥.95 in 342 versus 9,849. These are reported scenario-specific stopping counts, not universal 10–30×. The precise training budget for the underlying MNIST model and affine replacement was not fully disclosed in the inspected text.
- E2 (§4.2, p.217/Table 3): GPT-2 Small IOI circuits of 3–13 heads; clipped μ̂ .959–.972. For 13 heads, μ≥.90 certifies at 102 betting versus 1,875 Hoeffding samples; μ≥.95 at 357 betting, while Hoeffding does not certify within 2,000. These are bounded patching-recovery estimates, not proof of complete native mechanism or model accuracy.
- Negative/qualification: App. F, p.227/Table 6, five matched seeds, low-fidelity exclusion medians i.i.d./static/history = 88/201/118; borderline certification = 1,217/1,708/3,800. Adaptive sampling does **not** uniformly save samples. App. G estimates the 'true' reference with 100,000 Monte Carlo samples; reported coverage is descriptive, not independent proof. Severity and metric choice change the target (Fig. 5, p.217).

**Prior directions:** D.14–D.17, D.19–D.20 and E.21. **Rival explanations / minimal discriminator:** a narrow, precisely estimated interval can coexist with invalid cross-world hybrid, unrepresentative donors or post-selection bias. Prespecify score and input/intervention laws; report raw effects, clipped scores, paired held-out identities, uncertainty at each checkpoint and a sensitivity-positive donor, with explicit family-wise correction where needed. This is a proposed evaluation, not an implemented training or architecture recommendation. Visual completion additionally covers Table 1–2 and Figs. 2,6–7 (pp.216,225): the five-seed failure-finding median is 88 for i.i.d. versus 118 for history-adaptive despite history beating static; at the borderline certification condition the i.i.d. median 1,217 is lower than both adaptive medians.
