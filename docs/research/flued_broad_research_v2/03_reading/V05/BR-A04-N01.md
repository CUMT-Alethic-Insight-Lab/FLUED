**Problem:** circuit 'faithfulness' varies with ostensibly small assay decisions and can reward a different task. **One-sentence solution:** specify and test the intervention's granularity, unit, value, position, direction, circuit/complement, output metric, and input distribution before interpreting a score (§3). Borrowed AI techniques are activation resampling, compiled toy transformers, and automated circuit search, used as an evaluation laboratory rather than a language-model learning method. Base models remain fixed; Subnetwork Probing fits gradient-based node/edge masks for minimal ablation plus KL preservation; ACDC and attribution-based HISP are different search procedures (§5). The text does not give equal optimization/training cost for them.

## Interface and findings

Inputs are tokenized task prefixes (IOI names, Python docstrings, factual sports) or synthetic sequences; intervention addresses are heads/MLPs/edges and optionally position, contents are clean/corrupt activations; prediction is output logit difference, correct-answer fraction, probability, or KL (§§3–4; App. C). Resampling retains all features constant over its donor distribution and alters variable features. Zeroing can remove invariant features too. This is measurement of already learned or compiled computation, not a native state-write or retention comparison.

- §3.1.5/Fig. 2: restoring clean circuit edges in corrupted background may let clean information continue along non-circuit edges, while corrupting the complement on the clean background restricts clean signal to the circuit. Restoration and destruction do not automatically measure the same sufficiency or necessity. Node/edge/treeified-path operators additionally act on distinct computation graphs.
- §4/Figs. 3–4: GPT-2 IOI circuit faithfulness changes with mean versus resample, edges versus nodes, token position scope, prompt template (ABBA/BABA), and number of ABC prompts for the mean. The original roughly seven-example-per-template mean versus their 100 ABC-prompt comparator is not equal sampling. The paper reports original 87% recovery and instances above 100% and below zero. Report raw full/ablated logit differences beside both `mean(F)/mean(M)` and `mean(F/M)`; near-zero denominators can produce extreme individual ratios, so these are genuinely different estimands (§4.1–4.2).
- §5/App. D.3: in compiled X-Proportion, Input→Attn1 carries positional encoding invariant across clean and corrupt sequences: resampling keeps it, zeroing removes it. The zero-ground-truth and resample-ground-truth circuits differ. With matched resample-defined ground truth the three tested methods perfectly recover both Reverse and X-Proportion circuits in their reported ROC comparison; this conditional result does not imply perfect discovery on learned language models. Node-to-edge adaptations alter the methods; not an unchanged-method comparison.
- App. D: docstring and Pythia-2.8B sports are further cases. For the three-sport task mean-ablate whole model is about 33%, but resample donors always carry a different sport and produce 0%—an assay property, not universal better/worse circuit.

**Theory/transfer:** the toy algorithms are compiled, so their specified ground truth is known under a chosen intervention, unlike a learned model. The paper does not guarantee activation swaps are natural joint states. Direct task performance, raw effects, and distributional sensitivity should accompany normalized recovery.

**Prior directions:** D.07–D.09, D.11–D.17, D.19; C.18. **Minimal discriminator:** test actual positive signal and invariant nuisance across donor distributions, zero versus resample, edge versus node, and restore-circuit versus corrupt-complement; record prompt-level distributions and all four unnormalized outcomes. A null resampling effect without a positive assay cannot establish absence of information or use.
