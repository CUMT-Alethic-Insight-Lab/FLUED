# BIW-0095 — Improved Variational Autoencoders for Text Modeling using Dilated Convolutions

**Status: FULL**, [arXiv 1702.08139v2, 18 June 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1702.08139v2); first page identifies ICML 2017, PMLR 70, but formal edition not independently compared. [PDF](sources/BIW-0095__1702.08139v2.pdf), [text](sources/BIW-0095__1702.08139v2.txt); §§1–5 and qualitative appendix read; Table 2 visually checked on PDF p.5.

## Problem and approach

LSTM decoders may make sentence/document Gaussian latents unnecessary. **Solution:** use a causal dilated-convolution decoder and tune its receptive field to balance local word modeling with dependence on the global latent (§2). Borrowed techniques include WaveNet/PixelCNN-style masked dilations, residual CNNs, Gaussian reparameterization and KL annealing; semi-supervised variants also learn label variables using Gumbel-softmax. LSTM encoder observes the whole document; z is concatenated at each decoder word input. Outputs are stochastic word-level likelihoods with local prior-word context, not deterministic byte memory. Receptive field changes depth, parameterization and capacity together, not context alone.

## Results, budgets and negative controls

Yahoo/Yelp each use 100k training documents, 10k validation and test; vocabulary 20k, batch 32, 40 epochs, KL annealing to 1 over selected 10k/40k/80k iterations; latent dimension selected 32 or 64. Only LSTM decoders receive word dropout; CNNs use layer dropout (§3.2). Encoder initialization uses a separately pretrained LSTM LM; its extra training is not matched by the 40-epoch count. Models change compute/depth as field size changes.

Table 2 (Yahoo test): uninitialized SCNN field 15 has KL **13.3**, NLL **337.8**, PPL **68.7**; LCNN field 125 has KL **6.7**, NLL **333.9**, PPL **65.4**; VLCNN field 187 has KL **0.7**, NLL **336.5**, same as its LM. LSTM LM **334.9/66.2**; initialized LCNN **332.1/63.9**, KL **10.0**. Thus most latent KL is not best likelihood, and increased context eventually nearly erases latent improvement. Yelp LM PPL **42.6**, initialized LCNN **41.1**; initialized VLCNN **43.5**, showing non-monotonicity and an outright loss. These NLLs include the KL term and are variational quantities; do not treat them as separately verified exact marginals.

Semi-supervised Yahoo with 500 labels: SCNN accuracy **65.5%** versus LCNN **57.2%**, although LCNN NLL **331.3** beats SCNN **335.7** (Table 3); clustering reports the **best of ten** initializations and requires validation label assignment (Table 5). At 25k labels Yahoo **70.4 versus 70.5** LM-LSTM; uninitialized Yelp SCNN underperforms LM-LSTM above 100 labels (§3.4). Label generation outputs are qualitative.

## Interpretation and alternative routes

**Directions:** A.03–A.05, I.12. Author evidence supports a context-capacity tradeoff under these word-based stochastic recipes, not a causal diagnosis of a byte interface or a universal local-decoder prescription. Measure task-relevant latent content and native use independently of KL or perplexity; match parameter/compute, pretraining, decoder dropout and prefix visibility. A context-limited decoder that raises KL without improving exact identifiers, native answer dependence or matched-budget quality would weaken the proposed mechanism. Alternatives independent of retrieval include changing learning schedules/objectives, writer initialization or native reader design, and protecting exact content explicitly rather than merely reducing context.
