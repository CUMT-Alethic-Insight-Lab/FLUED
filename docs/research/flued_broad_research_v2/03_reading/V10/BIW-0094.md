## Problem and proposed method

An autoregressive language model does not explicitly provide a global sentence code, but an LSTM VAE often learns a posterior equal to its Gaussian prior. **Solution:** train a Gaussian sentence VAE using KL annealing and randomly replace decoder history words by UNK, making a global latent more useful (§§2–3). Borrowed variational inference uses a reparameterized diagonal Gaussian and an LSTM encoder/decoder; the ELBO combines word reconstruction likelihood with posterior/prior KL. The decoder has previous ground-truth words during teacher forcing; the stochastic full-sentence code is not a legal prefix-only online state. KL is annealed from zero to one; word dropout changes decoder information availability during training, not merely the KL weight. At test time full word history is supplied except in the inputless case (§6.1).

Nonzero expected per-example KL is not an information-content certificate: it includes both mutual information and aggregate-posterior/prior mismatch. Global sentence features, an address for a particular stored item, and use by a deployed query reader are different objects. Nothing here establishes byte exactness, dynamic chunk causality, or memory blindness.

## Evidence and limits

- PTB Table 2: standard RNNLM test NLL **100**, perplexity **116**; VAE bound **101** including KL **2**, perplexity **119**. Inputless RNNLM test perplexity **>600** versus VAE **380**, with VAE KL **15**; this compares two severely weakened decoders and does not make inputless VAE beat the standard RNNLM. VAE likelihood is a bound, baseline likelihood exact. The authors report ordinary training without both annealing and word dropout reliably yields zero KL (§4).
- BooksCorpus, approximately 80M sentences after pruning: word dropout 75%, LSTM 512; right-to-left generation and imputation of the sentence-final 20%. RNN beam 15 versus VAE three iterative conditional mode steps × beam 5 are the authors' same-count computational comparison, not demonstrated equal FLOPs. Table 4 adversarial distinguishability-gap (lower better) unigram **28.32→22.39**, LSTM **38.92→35.59**, while an independent RNNLM NLL worsens **46.01→46.14**. This restricted directional/beam setup limits the baseline to 15 candidate first-generated words (§5); this is not exact-fill accuracy or a general retrieval assay.
- Dropout increases KL and degrades overall likelihood; no-retuning across keep rates and inputless ungrammatical examples are explicitly acknowledged (§6.1, Table 5). In appendix, VAE TREC **87.0%**, CBOW **87.3%**, skip-thought **91.4%**, so representation benefits are not universal. PTB hyperparameter search reports 200 runs, 10 hours per set, 12 parallel; Books imputation and training hardware/compute are not established as equal-cost.

## Implications and reversals

**Directions:** A.03–A.05, E.01–E.03. Non-retrieval alternatives: alter objective/schedule, train writer and reader at separately controlled rates, or use a limited-context decoder; each needs its own baseline. Match training/teacher exposure, online causal visibility and bits, measure conditional content identity plus native output changes and aggregate-prior mismatch. A higher KL with unchanged native content sensitivity, or a positive result disappearing when full decoder history returns, weakens the proposed content-use explanation; a strong decoder that continues to use content refutes a blanket shortcut story.
