## Problem and one-sentence solution

The scalarized information bottleneck (IB) objective is often taken to trace a compression–prediction frontier and identify useful representations. **Solution:** show specific degeneracies when labels are deterministic functions of inputs and propose a squared information penalty to expose a non-strictly-concave frontier. The work borrows information theory (data processing, Fano, mutual information), multi-objective scalarization and a neural nonlinear-IB estimator; it does not claim compressed representations cannot be useful.

## Theory and proof boundaries

For discrete finite-label Y=f(X), Markov Y–X–T and unrestricted stochastic bottlenecks, DPI yields I(Y;T)≤I(X;T) and I(Y;T)≤H(Y). A stochastic mixture of Y and a constant achieves I(Y;T)=I(X;T) from 0 to H(Y); above H(Y) the optimum is flat (§3). Thus for β in (0,1), maximizing I(Y;T)−βI(X;T) selects the corner (H(Y),H(Y)); at β=1 all points on the rising segment tie. This is failure of one-to-one scalarization, not an impossibility of other useful compressed representations. The mixture is an 'uninteresting' optimal example, not uniqueness. For a Markov chain of layers ending in a perfect predictor, Fano and DPI give every layer I(Y;Ti)=H(Y), although compression I(X;Ti) may differ (§6); approximate low-error claims require the same evaluated distribution and the corresponding error bound, and skip paths must be included in the separator.

The proposed I(Y;T)−β I(X;T)^2 traces the theoretical IB curve under its global optimization/concavity assumptions; §4 explicitly simplifies to differentiable points, with superderivatives needed at corners. Appendix B distinguishes discrete-entropy deterministic IB: its curve may be nonconcave and the squared objective recovers suitable hard-cluster/envelope points, not automatically every point of a nonconcave curve. Appendix C assumes finite Y, same X marginal and L1 joint-distance ε≤1/2 from a deterministic reference, with perturbation bounds of order ε log(1/ε) and β-dependent constants that degrade near endpoints. Its layer bound additionally assumes the predictor implements the reference f; do not treat small label noise alone as the entire premise.

## Empirical controls and interpretation

MNIST 60k train/10k test, two-dimensional T=aθ(X)+standard Gaussian noise; 2×800 ReLU encoder plus output and 800-unit decoder (§7/App.A). 200 epochs, batch 128, Adam 1e−4, **20 runs per β with best objective selected**. The ordinary objective finds three regimes, with empirical transition β≈.45 versus theoretical 1; squared objective finds varied tradeoff points (Fig.4). Empirical I(X;T) uses a kernel upper bound, I(Y;T) a cross-entropy lower bound, not exact estimator-free information. Class clusters change across runs and can combine semantically dissimilar digits. The specific trivial stochastic-mixture family is outside this Gaussian-noise model class, so the plotted groupings are a different illustrative failure. App.A shows train and held-out curves; no equal-compute architecture-selection study or source memory evaluation.

**Directions:** A.06–A.08, A.16–A.17. Content is digit class/image information, not native address or online causal readout; KL, information estimates and physical bit rate are different units. Independent alternatives to retrieval include task-specific distortion, fixed-bit supervised content preservation and direct native-reader intervention. A compressed code successful on held-out identifiers, relation changes and deployment queries can remain useful despite this scalarization caveat; conversely matching estimated IB coordinates while differing on these endpoints defeats the assumption that IB-optimality alone selects the right representation.
