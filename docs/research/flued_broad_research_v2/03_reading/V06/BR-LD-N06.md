## Problem and method

**Problem:** final task accuracy conflates prompt control, preference, free construction and transfer. **Solution:** make copying a nearby demonstration conflict with compositional rules, track token interventions and follow each capability across saved checkpoints. Techniques borrowed: few-shot language modeling, controlled nonce morphology, attention blocking, activation patching and low-rank adaptation. Every prompt defines two binary features with fresh suffix mappings and examples, asks for a missing pair on a new stem; the causal decoder sees the prompt, not any future answer. Training targets next tokens or supervised rewritten exercises. Prompt token positions serve as addresses; requested feature and endings are content; model output is candidate scoring or unrestricted generated text. The paper does not measure persistent memory across separate documents.

## Measured behavior and bounds

- §§3–4/Figs.2–5: 17 models split 6 copy-preferring, 8 composition-preferring, 3 intermediate on probability conditioned on two candidate forms; this is not full-vocabulary probability. Four-choice ranking and free generation are separately tested. Masking the closest suffix versus requested value changes their log-probability margin. Patching a requested-value activation can redirect output toward a **shown** combination but Pythia rarely constructs a never-shown one (Appendix G); fixed layer 4 is not the best layer for every model (Appendix N).
- §5 and Appendices B/E/J: OLMo-2-7B control-sign change bracket 336B–630B tokens precedes fitted four-choice chance crossing ~1.46T original / 1.79T retest, and fitted exact-generation crossing ~3.59T. These are sparse-checkpoint and fitted times, not exact emergence instants. OLMo-7B-0424 reverses sign repeatedly; Marin already exceeds thresholds at first checkpoint, so its ordering cannot be dated. In direct task fine-tuning accuracy and control can cross together or in reverse order (Appendix I). Thresholds belong to their study, not to a new proposal.
- §§6.1–6.3, L–N: matched 1B-token SmolLM continuation replaces ~3% with synthetic definition/query documents; mere mined-definition exposure fails. At 360M, held-out-combination and all-shown training both improve, so absent-cell rehearsal is not isolated as necessary. Penalizing selected copy-favoring heads nearly eliminates their attention but not copy dominance/free-generation failure. At 7–8B, adapters improve the diagnostic but do not meet joint external SCAN/Elemental transfer, and some SCAN/general-text measures worsen. Definition exercises synthesized from real documents improve made-up/exchanged labels on some unseen classification tasks but leave the morphology diagnostic unchanged and can hurt relation benchmarks; model rewriting and a second-model verification are teacher costs.
- Appendix D/P reports float32 rescoring changes magnitudes, particularly Pythia bfloat16, while preserving main signs; a Quechua scoring fix changes one planned verdict and attention controls were split post hoc. Standard-spelling Quechua scores can be much lower than segmented suffix scores (F). Exact supplementary evidence and all plan departures are unavailable, so main-text reports remain author-reported, not fully audited.

## Questions and minimal test

Prior directions **D.01–D.05, D.07–D.09, D.20, E.05–E.08, E.15–E.17, E.21–E.22, I.01, I.22–I.23**. Rival accounts include failure to read the query, inability to construct a novel form, or candidate/scoring/tokenization artifact. Use independent prompts with conflicting correct/copy values, evaluate source-specific input interventions, candidate margins, unrestricted answers and transferred tasks separately; use same data and compute for direct skill practice, passive exposure and random/selected-head attenuation, and report precision/scoring. A controlled local counterfactual is not a proof about all historical mechanisms or long-range natural content use.
