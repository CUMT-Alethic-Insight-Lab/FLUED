## Problem, solution and units

**Problem:** a reconstruction-trained latent can be difficult for a generative prior, while naïve joint optimization may collapse the latent posterior. **One-sentence solution:** preserve posterior entropy and give generation high relative weight during joint latent formation, then lower prior and entropy weights to let reconstruction refine details (§§3–4). Borrowed techniques: variational Gaussian reparameterization, negative log-likelihood and Gaussian-mixture heads, flow matching, classifier-free guidance and staged multiobjective optimization; REPA representation alignment and pretrained visual/text encoders supply additional teacher information in particular variants.

Image encoder sees the full training image and writes a spatial grid of stochastic continuous latent vectors; image decoder reads them to reconstruct. In the continuous autoregressive (AR) prior, class/text condition precedes the sequence and position i sees only earlier latent tokens, predicting a mixture distribution for zi (§4.2, p.7); no separate indexed memory address or future query is involved. The flow prior sees a noised latent and time; the original full image is available to the encoder during training but not as source access during sampling. In the unified text–image variant, text is compressed to **16 continuous dimensions per text token**, not bytes, and text uses block-causal attention while image tokens are bidirectional; each direction conditions on the clean other modality, with noised target blocks and clean text prefix (Appendix C.4 pp.23–24). Reconstruction, sampling and native downstream use must not be conflated: FID and probe accuracy do not demonstrate deployed long-range content consumption.

## Objective and staged training

- §3.1 Eq.4–6 p.5: `KL(q(z|x)||p0)=E_q[-log p0(z)]-H(q)`; the proposed objective keeps image reconstruction, LPIPS, GAN, prior loss and **subtracts** λent H(q). This balances strong learned-prior pressure against entropy; it is not a theorem that all collapse has this cause. Appendix B p.19 describes the specific constant-mean/vanishing-variance shortcut, including near-constant latents making flow matching artificially easy while tiny mean differences may remain decoder-readable.
- §4.1 Eqs.7–11 p.7: AR models `p(z|c)=product_i p(zi|z<i,c)` and a diagonal Gaussian mixture; SiT uses `zt=(1-t)epsilon+t z`, velocity target `z-epsilon`, plus a distinct 10⁻⁶ Gaussian KL term for latent scale. The flow loss is not an exact likelihood. §4.3 Eq.12 p.8 keeps reconstruction/LPIPS/GAN weights **fixed**, ties λent to λprior, and decreases both together. Reconstruction is never literally off; the scheduling changes its relative influence.
- §5.1/C.1: AR EAR uses 500 epochs at prior/entropy 1 then 140 at .25, joint encoder/decoder/prior, batch 512. SiT EiT uses 80 epochs at .1 then 40 at .01 **plus a new frozen-VAE SiT trained up to 800 epochs**. For Table 6 ImageNet-256, an extra 10k decoder-only fine-tune improves the 80-epoch unguided gFID from 3.00 to 2.79 (C.1). Text-to-image includes the end-to-end phase, then 200k/80k/40k frozen-VAE prior training steps at successive resolutions/data. Comparisons and headline FID must include these stages and teacher costs, not merely the final prior-only count.

## Controls, positive and negative evidence

- §5.2 Table 2 p.10: GenFirst guided gFID 2.10, rFID 1.26; constant 3.04/3.27; cosine 5.45/.66; PI adaptive collapsed; frozen VAE 6.97/.53. Thus cosine/frozen reconstruction is better on rFID, not a uniform improvement. Appendix C.2 pp.20–21 states fixed and cosine controls run **320 epochs**; default GenFirst runs **500+140**, so Table 2 is not equal-exposure or equal-compute evidence. The paper reports simple hyperparameter tuning, not an exhaustive scheduler search. A matched **reverse-order** schedule is not found in these reported controls; absence of a found result is not a claim the authors never ran it.
- §3.2 Fig.4: more latent dimensions and VAE/prior capacity shift but did not remove the observed trade-off. §5.2 Fig.5 tests 200–560 first-stage epochs and second-stage weights, but the paper does not state a same-total-epoch matching rule for that sweep. Table 1’s frozen E2E-trained VAE plus new prior tests distribution modelability but includes additional training and differs from joint performance. Table 4’s REPA-E comparison is evaluated after 80 prior-only epochs for different VAE initializations; associated latent-learning costs and alignment teachers travel with the comparison.
- Appendix C.1 p.20 labels text-to-image variants A/B in the **opposite** way to §5.1 p.8 and Table 17 p.34: main text/Table 17 say A = ImageNet SiT, B = text-image MMDiT; C.1 reverses them. Cite each location and avoid assigning a specific method/number to a variant without author clarification. §5.2/Table 1 also calls EAR naïve training collapsed while its displayed EAR row has finite gFID 294.90; SiT is explicitly marked collapsed. Preserve this distinction rather than calling the paper wrong.
- §7 and Appendix A/Table 12 show remaining reconstruction losses: e.g. FLUX.1-dev PSNR 31.59 versus end-to-end 29.43/29.37; a good generation score is not perfect information preservation. Seed count and per-seed uncertainty were not found in the paper; do not imply multiple seeds. Visual sample claims remain illustrative, not validated by this read.

## Mapping and rival explanations

Prior directions **A.01, A.03–A.05, A.15, A.19–A.20, E.01–E.03, E.09, E.17–E.20, F.11, I.01–I.03, I.24–I.27**. Rival mechanisms include posterior entropy, changed total training, stage order, prior quality, reconstruction teacher, initialization and subsequent frozen-prior fitting. Proposed minimal contrast: same final architecture, output/rate, training FLOPs and exposure; factorial entropy on/off and high→low versus low→high versus constant prior, plus fixed reconstruction, equal stage durations and a separate frozen-prior evaluation budget. Track latent posterior, reconstruction fidelity and native generative performance independently. The image/text generative setting is not evidence that delayed quantization or a particular FLUED architecture is preferred.
