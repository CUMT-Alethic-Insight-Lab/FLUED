## Scientific contract

**Problem:** vector-quantized autoencoders can use many codes yet transmit information in very few directions. **Solution:** train the encoder/decoder continuously before switching on vector quantization, permitting weaker modes to form. Borrowed techniques: principal-component analysis (PCA), rate–distortion water filling, a straight-through gradient estimator, codebook exponential moving averages and dead-code respawn. Input is an image or audio clip; encoder writes continuous spatial/frame vectors, nearest-code address chooses one of K entries, discrete payloads are read by the decoder to reconstruct the same input. No future unknown query, separately trained language reader or natural content-use intervention is tested. Image uses pixel/perceptual and sometimes adversarial losses; audio uses waveform/mel, feature-matching and adversarial losses (§§2, 4, B). This is **not** delayed autoregressive generation or a learning-rate warm-up.

## Theory and observed results

- §§3.1–3.3, Eq.5–17 and Appendix A pp.12–17: linear encoder/decoder, independent Gaussian input and a rate-distortion-optimal channel continuously adapted to the latent distribution replace the actual finite quantizer. On the balanced, diagonal β=1 manifold, low-variance modes below the reverse-water-filling level become inactive; Theorem 1 upper-bounds the surviving active count and lower-bounds reconstruction loss after switching. It does **not** guarantee that every above-water mode survives or prove permanent collapse of a nonlinear trained encoder. The proof is read, not certified; neural predictions are empirical.
- The practical switch criterion is monitor continuous-latent effective dimension and stop near its plateau; compute reverse water filling on warm-up PCA at R=log2(K) to estimate an empirical upper envelope (§4, Fig.5, C.1). The 99%-variance PCA dimension of *all codebook entries* is not necessarily utilized payload dimension. Appendix C.2/Table 4's β=0 vanilla experiment has 248–254 apparent codebook dimensions but 3.7%, 0.3%, 0.0% utilization: unused random vectors inflate rank. Respawn at β=0 raises dimension to 22 vs 3 at K=1024 yet gives worse L1/rFID there; at larger K its reconstruction versus β=.25 respawn is mixed. Avoid flattening this into 'β=0 always worse'.
- Table 1 p.8: ImageNet-100, 100k total steps including 1k–40k continuous warm-up, compares vanilla and respawn+k-means. For K=16384 respawn gets L1 .140/rFID 69.7, 10k warm-up .128/52.0; at 40k .131/50.6. Longer warm-up is not uniformly best for every metric. 100k steps are **not verified equal FLOPs**, especially with k-means and different operations.
- Table 2 p.9 and B.2 pp.18–19: WavTokenizer 100 epochs total, 43 continuous+57 discrete, one common random seed; 2 H200 GPUs for about seven days per run, warm-up reused across K. At K=4096 test-clean PESQ is 2.336 cold versus 2.275 warm, a real negative; at K=8192 it is 2.179 versus 2.425. No cold K=65536 was run. Different cold/warm respawn thresholds and random instead of k-means initialization at the largest K complicate isolation. Appendix D records temporary degradation on switching. ImageNet-1K 500k steps, Table 3, is separate from ImageNet-100.

## Mapping and rival tests

Prior directions **A.15, A.19–A.20, E.01–E.02, E.17–E.20**. Plausible rivals include codebook staleness, initialization/respawn and altered compute rather than permanent mode death. Proposed minimal contrast: fixed final discrete rate and decoder, identical encoder/decoder, matched update/FLOP budget and codebook initialization/respawn factorial; measure variance on assigned codes and reconstruction on held-out rare details, not all-codebook PCA alone. Include multiple seeds and change-point trajectories. Results concern image/audio reconstruction, not proof that a particular continuous text-memory representation lost content.
