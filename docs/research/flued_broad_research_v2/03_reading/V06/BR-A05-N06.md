## Problem, model and training

**Problem:** why does increasing diversity sharply switch synthetic models from memorizing fixed labels to using contextual examples, and why may the latter fade? **Solution:** model competing learning rates, not simply capacity, using an analytically simplified attention and memorization circuit. Borrowed techniques: a one-layer attention classifier, residual MLP, binary cross-entropy and SGD, reduced-order dynamics and independent-circuit ansatz.

§2/Fig.2: K fixed random D-dimensional vectors receive random binary labels; a sequence contains N labeled examples and a query item whose label is withheld, selected from its context. Query item similarity addresses exemplars; label tokens are the value content. The attention output is read by an MLP classifier, while its residual query representation supports memorization in weights. Training labels are synthetic; no external source revisit or natural-document state is involved. The contextual test uses novel item-label pairs; the in-weight test excludes the query exemplar. Neither is a generic deployment-content intervention.

## Evidence and assumptions

- §§3.2–3.6, Appendix A pp.12–15: the minimal model assumes an additive independent MLP memorization logit plus attention context logit, and reduces query-key/value maps to β and w. The derivation further takes K≫N≫1, D→∞, small initialization and label symmetry; experiment uses D=63, N=100. Appendix A explicitly says the minimal model is phenomenological, **not derived** from the full trained transformer. Independent MLP experiments estimate an integrated memorization quantity with empirical exponent ν≈0.7; predicted context-length threshold exponent 1/ν≈1.43 versus observed ≈1.41 (Fig.6). Finite training curves are empirical evidence for this regime, not a universal capacity exclusion.
- §3.7 and A.4: after ICL has formed, an attention-specific L2 penalty plus slow memorization yields declining readout strength in the simplified model. Stronger regularization also changes acquisition. This differs from BR-LD-N05's shared L2 subcircuits; the papers do not establish the same replacement mechanism.
- §4/Fig.6/A.6: roughly 100+ seeds per context length in the acquisition distribution, ≈250 near the threshold, and ≥20 for each balanced-label setting; finite stopping time ≈450k in the bimodality experiment. Balanced labels remove a weak helpful statistical cue and can produce suboptimal solutions, not just delayed generalization. Fig. A.6 distinguishes **never acquired** (<99% in observed run), **acquired but no observed transience** (right-censored), and **observed transient** (≥99% followed by 90%). A yellow censored run must not be described as permanently persistent.

## Discrimination and applicability

Prior directions **E.04–E.08, E.15–E.18, E.21–E.22; B.20–B.21**. Rival explanation is finite memorization capacity versus accessible but slower memorization, with distribution and regularization further factors. A minimal test independently fits memorization at matched architecture and training budget, varies diversity and path-specific regularization, logs all seed trajectories and censoring, and uses balanced/unbalanced context controls. Add content-specific native interventions for target use; probe recovery or label accuracy alone is not that test. No natural language or architecture choice follows from the toy scaling exponent.
