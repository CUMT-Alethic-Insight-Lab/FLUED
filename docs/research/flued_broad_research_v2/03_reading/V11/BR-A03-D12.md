## Problem and method

Multi-query attention reduces decoding KV bandwidth but can compromise quality and stability; training an independent inference model is expensive. **Solution:** mean-pool pretrained MHA key/value heads within groups, retain separate query heads, and continue pretraining to recover quality at an intermediate grouped-query tradeoff. Borrowed ideas: shared KV cache, checkpoint conversion, continued pretraining, task fine-tuning, head grouping. The uptraining uses original T5.1.1 pretraining data/objective and recipe, not an external memory-label objective; task fine-tuning follows.

With H query heads and G KV groups, each group shares one key and value projection across H/G separately projected queries. MQA is G=1, MHA G=H. Keys/values are token-level contextual representations; distinct per-head q provide different address scores but queries in a group share the same stored KV content. Decoder self-attention is causal, cross-attention accesses the complete encoder input, and encoder self-attention stays unchanged (§2–3). Shared KV is not equivalent to independent memories or proof of fixed-state recurrent compression.

## Controls, dose and limits

Checkpoint initialization alternatives at T5-Large after 5% uptraining: mean-pooling > first original head > random new head (Fig.4, p.4898); figure is a comparison of **post-uptraining** quality, not zero-dose initialization alone. T5-XXL Fig.5 compares 0, 5%, 10%: GQA is already reasonable immediately after conversion; MQA is substantially worse at zero and both improve after 5%, with diminishing returns at 10%. Five percent costs approximately **600 TPUv3 chip-days**, in addition to original checkpoint pretraining and downstream task fine-tuning; no equal-total-compute scratch GQA comparator is provided. It is a real recoverable conversion counterexample, not a guarantee arbitrary architectures or hosts are cheaply transplantable.

Table 1 visually verified T5-XXL MHA/GQA-8/MQA average development score 47.2/47.1/46.6 and time 1.51/0.28/0.24 seconds per sample per TPUv4 chip. Tasks combine ROUGE-1, BLEU, and F1, so the mixed average is not a natural-unit single metric; GQA is below MHA on CNN 43.5 vs 43.8 and TriviaQA 81.6 vs 81.9, but above on PubMed 47.7 vs 47.5. Timings use 8 TPUs, per-model parallelization optimized separately, batch up to 32/chip and task-dependent lengths, not isolated KV latency or universal deployment throughput. Figure 3 labels its x-axis ms while Table 1 and Figure 6 label seconds: report the Table 1 seconds and retain this source unit conflict. Appendix A: from-scratch MQA suffered loss spikes and diverged during long-input fine-tuning; uptrained MQA still had variance and some tasks average three runs; uptrained GQA appeared stable. No decoder-only evaluation; authors explicitly state unknown comparison with a from-scratch XXL GQA.

## Direction and independent alternative

Directions **C.15, C.17, F.01–F.06**. A no-revisit alternative is to keep the existing recurrent state and diversify native read queries, with adaptation rather than an external bank. Disentangle query-head count, KV grouping, cache/state bytes, initialization, and uptraining dose; use matched self-transplant and same-compute alternatives. A no-conversion hypothesis is weakened by this measured example; an arbitrary-transfer hypothesis is weakened if its quality disappears under zero/5/10% dose curves, independent-seed validation, or matched host adaptation. This work alone does not establish natural final-output dependence on a selected distant fact.
