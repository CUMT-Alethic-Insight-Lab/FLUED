# W0373 — Naturalistic Causal Probing for Morpho-Syntax

**Status: FULL**, formal TACL 11 (2023), pp.384–403, [PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2023.tacl-1.23.pdf), local [PDF](sources/W0373.pdf), [text](sources/W0373.txt). Main paper and Apps.A–C read; Fig.7 PDF p.11 visually checked. Formal edition is not claimed byte-identical to arXiv:2205.07043.

**Problem → solution.** Correlational probes conflate recoverability with confounded semantics. Apply a dependency-tree-based inflection intervention to sampled Spanish sentences, estimate paired effects on language-model representations and compare with unpaired and template-based controls. Borrowed techniques: structural causal graphs, backdoor adjustment, paired potential outcomes, tree traversal/reinflection, principal components and supervised logistic/SVM probes. No model is trained to retain long-range content; probes are separately trained and supervised.

**Intervention and assumptions (§3–5, Apps.B–C).** A focus noun is gender/number-swapped; determiners, adjectives, subjects, verbs/copulas and other dependent morphology must be reinflected as appropriate; contractions also repaired (§3.2; detailed depth-first Algorithm 1). Not a one-token intervention. Gender analysis restricts to animate nouns, because lexicalized inanimate gender violates positivity for fixed lemma; even some animate lexicalized pairs require hypothetical shared lemmas. The paper expressly leaves semantically incompatible contexts unchanged: 'woman gave birth' → 'man gave birth' can be grammatical but semantically odd. Thus lexical surface, gendered referents and plausibility change, not a guaranteed semantic-only minimal pair. A natural sentence-level source does not make a patched hybrid hidden state natural; causal and bidirectional hosts expose different context at the target position.

The modeled graph has meaning U causing lemma L and morphology M, then forms F and representations R; Appendix C Proposition 1 blocks two specified backdoor paths by conditioning on `{L*, Z}` (lemma and remaining context), assuming the stated graph, valid interventions, positivity and representative sampling. It applies the backdoor criterion and marginalizes over L*,Z; paired within-context Monte Carlo estimates are unbiased only under those assumptions. In practice transformation is heuristic: one native speaker checked 100 sampled sentences, 73% accurate overall, 76% gender and 70% number (95% Wald interval overall 64–82%). Claims of perfect interventions or distribution-free unbiasedness are unsupported.

**Results/limits (§§5–7).** Two Spanish treebanks AnCora and GSD; 2,740 translated/filtered Winogender/WinoBias template pairs; BERT multilingual, XLM-R and Spanish GPT-2. Paired mean-effect directions agree more across treebanks than naive and template-based estimators (Fig.4); Fig.7 estimates first principal component about 20% of gender/number variance for BERT/RoBERTa, more concentrated in GPT-2. Probes on original focus nouns are near-perfect but sentence-level probes deteriorate markedly under counterfactual testing; retraining on augmented data improves them without closing the gap. Tested adjective mask probability is a real downstream-style outcome, but is not an autoregressive distant-answer task or native retention test. Template translation, WordNet animacy, labels, human checking and augmented probe supervision add resources; no equal-compute or latency claim.

**Directions:** D.07–D.10/D.18 and D.01. For independent non-revisit state reform, use matched grammatical repairs and human-checked semantic labels, compare direct natural input replacement with representation swaps, and separately assess unmodified causal-host next-token outputs. A hidden-state hypothesis changes only if effects replicate across contexts, valid interventions, hosts and native output, not merely if a supervised probe finds a direction.
