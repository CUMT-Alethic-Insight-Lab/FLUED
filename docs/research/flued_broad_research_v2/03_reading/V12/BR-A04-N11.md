## Problem, method and units

**Problem → one-sentence solution.** A small subgraph with a good reconstruction/faithfulness score need not be unique, sufficient, necessary or minimally explanatory; the paper frames preservation, localization and minimality as explicit statistical null hypotheses against input or randomly sampled circuit reference distributions. Borrowed techniques: score-based activation patching, binomial/tail tests, kernel independence and permutation tests, multiple-testing correction, and hard-coded program-to-Transformer positive controls. The experiments evaluate existing fixed circuits; they do not train a new memory mechanism or a native reader.

The model is a token-sequence-to-logit Transformer; graph nodes are attention heads, MLPs, input/output and edges are residual connections split into query/key/value inputs. `C(x)` runs only circuit edges, with excluded edges supplied from zero ablation or a corrupted-input activation (symmetric token replacement, STR). Thus input content, address and computation paths are separately specified: donor activations and hybrids are additional experimental permissions, not evidence that the unmodified system naturally reads the same content. A task has dataset `D` and score `s`; lower faithfulness loss `Fτ(M,C)` means model/circuit behavior is closer under that score. In experiments faithfulness is based on an L2 score difference; IOI and docstring scores are logit differences, induction is target-token log probability, greater-than is a probability contrast, and compiled tasks use token-level L2 error (§§2,4.1, App.D). A matching scalar is not whole-output semantic entailment.

## Exact tests and assumptions (§3; App.B)

Let `Δ=s(C*(x),y)−s(M(x),y)` and `q*=0.9`, `α=0.05` in the paper's experiment. Reference probabilities depend on a fixed chosen data distribution, edge graph, ablation and circuit-sampling procedure.

| Test | Printed null and comparison | What the outcome does **not** establish |
|---|---|---|
| Idealized equivalence | `H0: |P_D(Δ>0)−1/2| < ε`, with ε=.1. Count circuit wins and use a binomial tail (§3.1, App.B.1). Rejection is evidence against this idealized sign-balance null. | Retaining H0 is not positive proof of equality; the sign test measures win frequency, not closeness of score magnitude. Strict `>0` makes exact ties important: App.E.1 explicitly says identical Tracr outputs make the sign test inapplicable. Their Table 1 nevertheless marks Tracr equivalence as passing; do not claim an applied equivalence p-value. |
| Idealized localization/independence | `H0: s(C̄*(X),Y) ⟂ s(M(X),Y)` where C̄* removes candidate edges, using HSIC and permutation (§3.1, App.B.3). Rejection indicates dependent scores under the chosen setup, not necessarily a named causal path. | A retained null does not prove independence or absence of task information. Kernel, permutations, exchangeability, score choice and power matter; authors explicitly did **not** study Type II error (§5). |
| Idealized minimality | For each edge `e*`, `δ(e,C)=E_D |s(C)−s(C−e)|`. Inflate C* with one random input-to-output path and choose a uniformly sampled newly added edge eI; `H0: P_RI[δ(e*,C*)>δ(eI,CI)]>q*`. Rejecting after Bonferroni `α/m` identifies an edge as unnecessary by this operational reference (§3.1). | The construction assumes the added paths/edges are irrelevant. A redundant or synergistic edge can have a small single-edge effect yet be conditionally necessary after other edges are removed; this is not the group-conditioned minimality tested in N09. |
| Flexible sufficiency | `H0: P_R[Fτ(M,C*) < Fτ(M,Cr)] ≤ q*`; rejection indicates candidate is more faithful than the stated proportion of random reference circuits. | Better than a particular random-walk distribution is not equal to the original model, semantic correctness, or a uniquely identified mechanism. Reference size affects strength. |
| Flexible partial necessity | `H0: P_R[Fτ(M,C̄*) > Fτ(M,C̄r)] ≤ q*`; rejection means candidate knockout damages fidelity more than reference knockouts at the selected quantile. | Necessity is relative to R and ablation; complement-only vs full-model sampling can change conclusions. The overbar denotes the knockout complement, as shown in the printed Eq.7. |

Random reference circuits are built by random walks from input to output until at least k edges; for minimality an added path and added edge form the reference. The empirical suite samples 100 reference circuits for each flexible test, and 10,000 reference edges for IOI and greater-than, 1,000 for the other circuits (§4.1). Binomial validity requires appropriate independent reference draws and fixed target; permutation inference requires exchangeability; finite evaluation-data uncertainty, multiple comparisons across tested configurations, and tuning/selection on the same data need separate attention. Appendix B.1 sketches the boundary-binomial calculation; B.2 specifies the tail test and B.3 permutes the paired model scores for HSIC. We have not independently verified the proof or code.

## Evidence, contradictions and limits

**Controls.** Six circuits: two Tracr compiled known programs (reverse 1/2/3 and prefix proportion of x), plus four previously identified induction, IOI, greater-than and docstring circuits. Tracr reversal uses all permutations of 1/2/3; proportion uses 50 random length-four strings over `{x,a,c}`; both use zero ablation. Induction uses 40 filtered OpenWebText validation sequences of length 300 and zero ablation; IOI/greater-than use GPT-2 small and STR, while docstrings use a 4-layer attention-only model (App.D). These are legitimate known-positive functional checks, but neither reported construction deliberately establishes sensitivity to *nonlinear conditional redundancy*, nor is a dedicated such power curve presented; no claim that those positives prove the instrument can detect it. An explicit nonlinear OR-like parallel-path known-positive and a matched false/random circuit would test that gap. Paper reports hundreds of circuits in a few minutes on one A5000 GPU (App.D.2), not complete per-test, acquisition or selection compute.

**Reported results.** The authors report candidate circuits are much better than many random circuits: IOI and greater-than remain more faithful than random circuits at 90% of model size, docstring and induction at 30% (§4.2/Fig.2). The same comparison is not a proof that they reproduce the original. For knockout necessity, all four candidates beat equal-size complement-only random references, but docstring does not beat random full-model references (Table 2). Under STR, knocking out the entire model can damage IOI and greater-than less than knocking out only the candidate; the paper finds this artifact absent under zero ablation (§4.2). Progressive edge removal reportedly leaves faithfulness largely unchanged for about 50% of greater-than and 20% of IOI edges; IOI is nonmonotonic (Fig.3), so no blanket 'more deletion is always worse' conclusion.

**Source-level inconsistencies, visually checked.** Table 1's caption says ✓ means null rejected, but the authors' narrative and App.E.2 treat ✓ as retaining the idealized independence null; its idealized equivalence ✓ for both Tracr cases conflicts with App.E.1's explicit statement that exact output ties make the sign test inapplicable. Table 1 and §4.2 mark docstring as passing minimality; App.E.3 instead says only induction and Tracr pass and describes many docstring edges as insignificant. Figure 5 places the DS cutoff at the left edge, making the competing summaries particularly important. These are contradictions within v1, not grounds to invent corrected outcomes; code/author clarification is needed to reconcile the interpretation. Similarly, a non-rejection is not evidence of no alternative circuit when power is unmeasured.

**Selection and deployment.** Existing circuits, ACDC configurations and original task datasets are reused (§4.1, App.D); the paper does not demonstrate independent held-out circuit discovery, pruning/threshold selection, evaluation and all hypothesis choices for every task. This is an unresolved leakage risk, not proof a particular leak occurred. Neither input-level realism nor score agreement certifies the joint distribution of patched activations, and the study does not establish any target host's native content dependence.

**Directions D.14–D.17/D.20** (also D.11–D.13): preregister reference distributions and tests, include known positive and negative controls with group redundancy, reserve independent evaluation examples, compare native outputs and task-valid input interventions, and report non-rejection/power separately. Independent no-revisit alternatives include state-writing/retention reform, native query/readout improvement, or content-aware training objectives. A hypothesis changes if same-host, same-budget, legal positive/negative grouped interventions replicate on held-out tasks with calibrated power; a scalar gain or non-significant isolated knockout alone is insufficient.
