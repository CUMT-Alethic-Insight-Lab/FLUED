**Problem / solution / technique.** When pretraining must serve several future tasks at limited representation rate, which surrogate preserves the right information? Use a multitask information bottleneck and compare its optimal rate-dependent allocation with fixed Gaussian-prediction pretexts. Borrowed rate–distortion and Gaussian extremality provide population bounds, not an implemented language-model memory.

**Contract and learning.** §§2.1–2.3 define Gaussian input X, noisy linear task targets, weighted task identity known to an unrestricted probabilistic decoder but not to the encoder, Markov Z–X–Y, and log-loss regret versus full X. Z is the compressed content, not an addressed store; no sequence/query timing or native reader is modeled. Mutual information I(X;Z) is the rate in bits, not model size, FLOPs, or actual serialized bits. The encoder is reoptimized separately at every rate even when the surrogate is fixed. Proposition 1 and Appendix A reduce arbitrary encoders under the stated Gaussian assumptions to a unique residual-covariance optimum; Appendix A.1 cites an external extremality theorem, A.3–A.5 use geodesic convexity, existence and a supporting multiplier. This is paper reading, not independent proof verification.

**Reversal and positive control.** Proposition 2 and Appendix B require specialist subspace above-average relevance per dimension *and* positive omitted evaluation information. Then it wins at sufficiently low rate but loses at sufficiently high rate; existence, not uniqueness, of crossing is proved. §4.1 and Appendix C use two rank-one coordinate tasks with frequencies (1−1/M,1/M) and SNRs M²,M⁴: low rate favors common/moderate precision, high rate rare/high precision. For M=256, at least one of 8 and 32 bits has relative regret >4.67, denominator optimal weighted excess distortion, not total task loss; arbitrarily large worst-rate regret is an existence family with increasing SNR, not a typical-data claim. Appendix C explicitly limits its diagonal argument to these rank-one tasks, while handling oblique fixed Gaussian pretexts. Theorem 1/Appendix D constructs a task- and rate-dependent Gaussian corruption from the optimum, not a deployable task-blind universal pretext. Theorem 2/Appendix E: if every task information matrix is bounded by L I and weighted mean is positive definite on the relevant subspace, a fixed pretext based on that weighted mean has distortion ≤S(1+L) optimum for every finite rate; L=2 gives ~16% relative bound, and small-L regret bound is L²/8+O(L³). Knowledge of the task suite is required; singular common nullspace needs restriction, and the bound does not promise a generic reconstruction objective.

**Observed studies and cost.** §6.1/F.1 freeze ImageNet ResNet-18, PCA/whiten 512 features to ≤32 using training data, and train separate binary bottlenecks on five source attributes versus feature reconstruction; evaluate four disjoint CelebA attributes with fitted logistic probes. Five model seeds share splits; 2,000 paired bootstraps are pointwise, not simultaneous. Nominal Gaussian crossover ~16.8 bits and hard-code crossover ~21.2 bits are interpolations on different axes; 9/28 versus 13/28 endpoint rankings reverse. B∈{0,2,4,8,16,32} hard threshold bits, straight-through sigmoid gradient, 30 AdamW epochs, with actual fixed-length payload B bits if stored uncompressed. Empirical 2,000-test-sample code entropy cannot exceed log2(2000)=10.966 bits and cannot establish 16/32-bit population entropy or actual encoded length. NLL subtracts a fitted uncompressed probe, not Bayes risk; CelebA probe training reuses encoder-training images. §6.2/F.2 use three seeds, Gaussian variational KL upper-bound rate and independently trained probe MSE-derived indices on dSprites; a physical-axis role swap reverses priority too. Neither the KL upper bound nor summed recoverability indices is actual code bits. Authors report 168 main jobs plus 42 swap jobs, four H100 GPUs, without a total GPU-hour figure (F.2). End-to-end training is synthetic; CelebA only compresses frozen features.
