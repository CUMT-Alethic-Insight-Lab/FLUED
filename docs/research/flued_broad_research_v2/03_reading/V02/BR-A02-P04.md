**Problem / one-sentence solution / technique.** Correcting along a write key cannot explicitly clean a separately addressed stale association. Add a learned erase address before the conventional delta write, using fast-weight regression and a two-factor recurrent update (§3). The erase address is a projected representation learned under language-model loss, not a supervised label of obsolete facts.

**Causal operation.** For `S` key×value, use `S_t=(I−βkkᵀ)(I−γeeᵀ)D S_prev+βkvᵀ` (§3.3 eq.8). In order: channel decay D, normalized e directional erase, delta correction and write along normalized k, then read `S_tᵀq`. For `Ŝ=D S_prev`, erase is one gradient step on `½||Ŝᵀe||²`, so q orthogonal to e is unaffected *by that substep only*; correlated q is changed. The write and erase operators generally do not commute; γ=0 recovers baseline, e=k is same-address stronger correction, not a no-op. This is a separate learned projection, not GDN-2's channel reweighting of k. Current-prefix x generates all q/k/v/gates, with no future query at write time. §3.4 interleaves erase and delta as two recurrence substeps for chunk execution; source weights and buffers remain separate.

**Stability and cost.** Unit e and scalar γ∈(0,1) make erase factor a contraction; delta factor similarly when normalized k and β in range; product with diagonal D≤1 is nonexpansive under those assumptions, not a claim that the complete network or accumulated outputs are bounded. Safe log gate is bounded below by −5, differing from KDA's sigmoid parameterization (§3.3); separate projection/gate and doubled substeps increase cost. No whole-system latency benchmark/physical-byte ledger was recovered. Hybrid models retain a full-attention KV cache.

**Comparators / empirical results.** §4: dense 2.5B compares Transformer, GDN, GDN-2, KDA, EDA; MoE 25B total/2.8B active compares GDN/KDA/EDA. Non-Transformer models use three linear-attention layers per full-attention layer, so not pure fixed-state; 400B tokens at 4096, then MoE 80B at 32K, AdamW. Two evaluations of a checkpoint are not two training seeds. Dense EDA average beats KDA by 0.63 points; after midtraining RULER average is reported best, but §4.4 says advantage at 4–16K and near baselines at 32–128K, not necessarily best at each length. 64K/128K extrapolate past training: Table 3 gives EDA 71.90/44.22, KDA 72.00/42.70, GDN 67.62/45.16. Midtraining Table 2 also has EDA GSM8K 75.99 below KDA 76.21 and EvalPlus 51.78 below KDA 53.82. Appendix A Table 5 reports dense EDA 2.5295B versus KDA 2.5218B, and MoE EDA 24.6558B total/2.8119B active versus KDA 24.6324B/2.7885B.

**Real negative and limits.** §4.5/Fig.2 reports older raw write-key recall is *better for KDA*. Same-gate replay of actual/random/shuffled/same-key erasure yields mean local readout perturbation 0.064/0.143/0.115/0.223; this measures current readout disturbance, not removal of stale information or task correctness. Mean |cos(e,k)|≈0.105 for 128-dimensional heads, near independent random-direction reference. Gate-strength allocation is not removed energy; replay does not prove which facts are useful. No clear gold-defined first-binding versus latest-binding stale/valuable counterfactual is established by these probes.

**Rivals and discrimination.** The extra erase factor, extra projection, altered safe gate, compute or full-attention path may explain gains. With identical state/bits and q/k/v, cross no erase, learned erase, same-k, shuffled and targeted-wrong address, and same-gate variants; independently manipulate first/latest ground truth, related-key overlap and helpful older facts, measuring new and collateral old answers.
