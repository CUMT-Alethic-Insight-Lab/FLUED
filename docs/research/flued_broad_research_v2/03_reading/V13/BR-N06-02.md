## Problem and method

Representational similarity metrics need not tell whether one model's features can support a particular receiver's task. **One sentence:** freeze a source bottom and recipient top, optimize a restricted bridge on the recipient's labeled training task, and report held-out task penalty relative to the original recipient. This adapts model stitching into an operational, directional comparison; unlike an unsupervised geometric similarity score, it uses task labels and allows optimization. The precise unit is an image feature map or ViT token representation, not a memory address or naturally deployed source use.

§2 and App. A.4: conv connectors are BatchNorm → 1×1 convolution → BatchNorm; transformer connector is a tokenwise 768×768 linear map. Base weights frozen, bridge Adam with cosine schedule and initial LR .001; train labels are allowed and test error is measured only after fitting. Number of bridge training epochs/steps, initialization and BN running-statistic treatment are **not specified in the read v1 text**; do not invent them or infer them from the distinct X05 paper. Residual stitches occur between blocks, not inside them. Kernel-size 1/3/5/7/9 ablation reportedly makes minimal difference (B.1); optimizer/selection cost remains extra.

## Observations and strongest countercontrols

- §4/Fig. 2 captions report independently initialized CIFAR networks can be joined at tested layers with small errors, whereas random bottom networks incur high penalty especially deep. §5 and Table 1 describe ImageNet supervised versus self-supervised models with near-comparable quality and stitching penalties up to 5 percentage points. These observations are not a proof that all SGD minima are connected: the authors explicitly state this as a conjecture.
- 100% randomized training labels are an exception to early compatibility of weaker labels (§5). Appendix B.2 observes **random early layers can also stitch with low penalty**; a successful early bridge alone therefore is not evidence of trained, shared semantics. §6 reports stronger sources with more training data, width or time can improve a weaker receiver; inversion need not hold. Fig. 2C includes 5K/10K/25K source data; the downstream 10K receiver's labels train the bridge, so this is not zero-shot transfer. Appendix B.3 warns extensive receiver fine-tuning can conceal the difference between random and trained sources. Appendix B.4 reports approximately 3% versus §6's within 2% for the freeze-training result; the visually inspected curves do not supply exact endpoint values or resolve the verbal distinction.
- The authors' 'all roads' interpretation is **task-dependent compatibility**, not shared causal process. Reuse the independent Smith counterexamples as scoped in the [V07 Smith card](../V07/BR-A06-X04.md), and the IRI control in [X05](BR-A06-X05.md); neither proves failure for every transfer.
