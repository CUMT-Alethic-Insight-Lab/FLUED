## Task and one-sentence solution

Why do context-sensitive sequence behaviors emerge and sometimes disappear as data diversity, model size, history and context change? Train autoregressive Transformers on a finite mixture of randomly generated Markov chains, then model their next-symbol distribution as a convex mixture of four explicit candidate algorithms. Borrowed techniques are Bayesian posterior prediction, in-context sequence modeling, explicit n-gram statistics and post-hoc constrained regression—not a proposal to install external source retrieval. Here “retrieval” means Bayesian weighting of **training-set transition matrices**, not retrieval of document passages.

Default chain has 10 states; N=4…2048 chains, training sequence length 512; default test context 400; transition rows and task priors sampled from Dirichlet distributions (§2/A). Fresh sequences are drawn each update; task diversity and sequence diversity differ. Uni-Ret/Bi-Ret weight known training matrices using unigram/bigram likelihoods; Uni-Inf/Bi-Inf estimate unigram/transition frequencies from the current context. All use the observed causal prefix for next-state prediction; no future source tokens, external document index, byte boundary, or fixed recurrent state are measured.

## Findings, comparator permissions and budget

- §3, Fig. 5: shuffling context positions preserves unigram counts but destroys transition order, and proximity to trained versus fresh random transition sets is a second behavioral diagnostic. Both can have alternative explanations and are not unique circuit identification. §4/Fig. 6 fits nonnegative weights summing to one by squared probability error on 300 independent ID chains; Figs. 7 and I report low residuals and predict OOD behavior from those ID weights. Appendix I adds four arbitrary algorithms and observes near-zero weights for them; this is a useful negative control, **not** an identifiability proof against correlated or omitted plausible strategies. Appendices E.1–E.4 supply trained versus random/unseen-transition neuron comparisons and attention-map correlations; the authors explicitly propose future causal patching. Neuron reconstruction uses a selected best neuron for each ground-truth transition, not an unaided native decoder.
- At intermediate diversity the context-generalizing Bi-Inf-like phase can precede a stronger train-distribution Bi-Ret-like phase, with ID performance improving while OOD worsens (Fig. 7); at N=2048 the latter is not visibly dominant **within the budget** (Fig. 33). At lower complexity k=2, even 2048 chains do not yield the same Bi-Inf phase; wider embeddings shift the observed diversity threshold. App. C.2.3 compares estimated rather than measured FLOPs and explains a fixed-step versus estimated-FLOP scale reversal. These are configuration-dependent observations, not proof every model becomes context-blind.
- Transition-token encoding eliminates the observed Uni-Ret phase (Fig. 8c), but changes the input alphabet to k²+k and presents prior transitions as symbols; it is not a universal tokenizer or byte-interface verdict. App. H's Llama 3.1 8B/70B/405B digit experiment constrains output to digit tokens, uses comma separators and multiple prompt wordings; bigger models and explicit Markov instructions help in this narrow assay, not proof of the synthetic training mechanism in an LM.
- Two-layer softmax Transformer, AdamW 6e-4, batch 128, default 107,978 steps and estimated `6DN = 10^16` FLOPs for the main model; that estimate does not equal measured wall clock, attention compute, fitting/probing overhead or same FLOPs across every analysis. Their A100 note reports five jobs sharing an 80 GB device for 3.5 h, not a universal cost.

## Independent options and change of hypothesis

Rather than add source retrieval, vary data/task diversity, objective timing, representation of relations or history. At fixed compute and task visibility, construct target-host in-distribution and genuinely novel bindings, order-preserving versus shuffled controls, seed-matched checkpoints and content interventions. Include plausible rival mixture components and assess mixture collinearity/held-out fit; selectively perturb a candidate circuit where authorized. If content counterfactual use remains stable despite falling behavioral bigram fit, or recovered fit fails intervention, withdraw a literal competition-mechanism interpretation.
