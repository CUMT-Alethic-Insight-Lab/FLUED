## Problem and one-sentence solution

Equal initial input-output functions can follow different learning paths depending on how factor weights are allocated. **One sentence:** solve the continuous-time two-layer linear regression dynamics at λ-balanced initializations, linking relative encoder/decoder scale to function, internal feature and neural-tangent-kernel trajectories. Borrowed techniques are singular value decomposition, matrix Riccati differential equations, gradient flow and kernel analysis; this is a theoretical analysis, not an LM intervention.

Let output be W₂W₁x and loss be mean squared error. Their primary theorem assumes whitened input covariance, λ-balanced factors `W₂ᵀW₂ − W₁W₁ᵀ = λI`, and hidden dimension `min(input, output)`; task-aligned scalar-mode solutions need further alignment (§§3–5). Gradient flow preserves the balance quantity; initial product can be fixed while factors are rescaled. Appendix A.1 differentiates both Gram matrices and cancels terms; Appendix B.1 writes dynamics of the joint Gram in Riccati form; B.2 solves the initial-value equation by matrix exponential/inverse, B.3–B.4 separate modes; C.1 derives aligned singular-value trajectories. Invertibility of B enters the numerically stable form and the stated convergence argument, not every solution. Independent proof verification was not performed.

## What the analysis does and does not show

- Small λ yields balanced feature learning under the paper's settings, whereas large magnitude can make function dynamics kernel-like; if the task solution is outside the initially accessible span, rectangular architectures can show initial lazy behavior followed by delayed feature change (§5/C.4). Large fixed feature/NTK components can obscure a smaller task-structured factor; a nearly fixed kernel does **not** mean no useful feature is learned.
- §6 and D.1–D.4 discuss sequence-of-task forgetting, special exactly reversed tasks and transferring an extra feature after training on a toy semantic hierarchy. For the transfer comparison the additional output row is trained on **one labeled item while all other weights are frozen**; generalization depends on the trained input representation. Positive λ helps in that particular hierarchy, negative λ can fail, and rebalancing changes later fine-tuning dynamics. These are controlled linear models with specific parameter permissions, not demonstrated improvements in nonlinear Adam language models or trained LoRA.
- This describes vectors and matrix factors, not source spans, dynamic address selection, tokenization or deployed causal content access. Testing latent feature drift alone does not establish state retention or readout use. Cost is simulated small matrices/gradient steps; no large-model compute comparison is supplied.
- Source caution: exact-version HTML A.3 contains an apparent variance arithmetic discrepancy (derivation from `3σ⁴−σ⁴` prints `4σ⁴`), and D.2 contains adjacent contradictory wording about zeros in B for nonzero λ. C.3 also appears to swap factor norm signs relative to §5.2. These are directly present in the arXiv HTML, but no PDF cross-check or independent proof adjudication was possible; avoid relying on those exact steps or alleging the theorem false.
