## Problem, method, and permissions

Earlier hidden layers can receive weak gradients and need not be directly discriminative. **One sentence:** attach train-time companion classifiers to intermediate representations, optimize their supervised losses jointly with final classification, and eventually attenuate or zero these losses. The borrowed technique is supervised classification as an auxiliary/regularizing loss, not an autoencoder or an unsupervised objective. Sections 2.1–2.2 and eqs. 3–6 use a squared-hinge (or softmax) final loss plus weighted, thresholded hidden classification losses. The authors describe threshold γ or an epoch decay as ways to zero companions; the exact decay typography in the extracted text is not safely reconstructable. Each head receives class labels and gradients update its prefix; this is **extra supervision and computation**, not a free probe. Heads need not be present in final deployment.

Inputs are complete images; targets are image labels, and intermediate units are hidden feature maps, not positions, dynamically written memory addresses, tokens, bytes, or causal sequence states. Neither a query-last setting nor an intervention on remote source content is evaluated.

## Evidence and theory

- Fig. 2 reports MNIST error 0.56→0.51% for softmax and 0.48→0.39% for SVM; Table 1 reports greedy layerwise pretraining 0.43% versus DSN 0.39%. Both final train errors can be 0.03% while test errors differ (Fig. 2c), so the benefit is not just lower training error. Small-data curves are suggestive, not a modern LM result.
- Table 2 CIFAR-10: NIN versus DSN 10.41→9.69% without augmentation and 8.81→7.97% with augmentation. Table 3 CIFAR-100 35.68→34.57%. Table 5 ImageNet validation top-1/top-5: eight layers 40.7/18.2→39.6/17.8%, eleven layers 34.5/13.9→33.7/13.1%. Section 3.1 says the 11-layer CNN needed an 8-layer pretraining stage and 95 epochs versus DSN 58; that is a practical recipe comparison, **not** equal optimization work or a factor-isolated causal speedup. Matching network complexity approximately is not matching auxiliary backward FLOPs or head parameters. Batch 128, momentum .9; no actual wall time/FLOP ledger supplied.
- §2.3 and appendix: the argument assumes a shared optimum for output and companion objectives, local strong convexity, bounded unbiased stochastic gradients, projection, suitable learning rates and initialization in the local region. Lemma 2 combines curvature and obtains a bound scaling as `4G²/((λ1+λ2)² T)`; lemma 3 handles `1/t` under further approximations. The theorem compares **upper bounds**, not a general actual-convergence or downstream-generalization guarantee. The identity-extension argument in Lemma 1 presumes the relevant network layers can realize identity; it does not establish a common optimum for arbitrary architectures. Thresholded auxiliary objectives need not satisfy the asserted strict curvature everywhere.

## Independent options and discriminators

A non-retrieval alternative is a temporally scheduled intermediate predictive head, but its targets and causal visibility must be legal for the deployed task. Compare matched final-only, equal-extra-compute generic head, and targeted companion objectives; remove heads at deployment and measure both late native output dependence on altered content and clean-task cost. A gain solely in a supervised probe, or gains disappearing under matched compute/reverse schedules, would weaken the proposed mechanism. Nothing here establishes that all memory fails, that byte interfaces are innocent, or that more discriminative intermediate features preserve arbitrary future queries.
