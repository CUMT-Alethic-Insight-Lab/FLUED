## Problem and solution

Output accuracy after stitching can look good even when front and end models use different cues, or the bridge learns a new shortcut. **One sentence:** train the bridge on inputs optimized to be nearly indistinguishable to the front at the stitch, while requiring consistency with the standalone end model, to probe compatibility of front-model input preimages as well as forward transfer. Borrowed techniques are model metamers/input inversion, feature distillation, affine connectors and adversarial training.

§2.3/A define approximate identically represented inputs by small relative feature error, generated from uniform noise by 200 input-optimization steps. For a given original image, inversion uses front-model representations; SLM additionally uses end-model output distributions, while FuLA uses end-model internal features up to the penultimate layer. Hard-label matching instead only sees front representations and labels and is **not** invariance-enabling. These are substantially different permissions; the optimized image may be unnatural and near equality does not prove exact identity, semantic preservation, completeness of preimages, or uniqueness. The comparison assumes a unique optimal forward alignment on the test distribution, a substantive assumption rather than established fact.

## Evidence, countercontrols, cost

- §3/Figs. 2–5: standard stitching appears similar at deep layers even for pixel-biased front models; proposed variants reveal stronger incompatibility and often greatly reduce excessive agreement from newly injected class pixels. This is a controlled output/intervention observation on CIFAR-10 and related vision classifiers, not proof of shared original computation or a universal semantic metric. Robust and non-robust models show additional incompatibilities; robust-to-robust is more compatible under their criterion.
- Identity/cross-seed, self-supervised, cross-bias and cross-task models, pixel randomization and varying cue availability provide positive/negative controls. Yet Appendix F.2 reports a failed identity sanity check for ViT-Base SLM on ImageNet-100; F.3 notes residual shortcut use for SLM, and F.1 notes ImageNet IRI semantic divergence. Different objectives trade agreement against leakage; no overall winner. Treat these exceptions as part of the finding.
- §3 and B: ResNet-18/CIFAR-10 uses biased 1×1 convolution bridges, frozen model weights, Moore–Penrose direct-match initialization from 500 training samples; three random front initializations with the **same** end instance. Standard and IRI bridges share initialization, but standard bridge training updates batch-normalization statistics and uses augmentation; IRI training freezes statistics and skips augmentation. Authors say removing augmentation in standard training minimally affected trends; no analogous numerical BN-equalization result was located. Bridges train 30 epochs low resolution/12 high, Adam .001, weight decay 1e-4. Input inversions add 200 optimization steps per image and per front/stitch location; full wall-time and FLOP ledger unspecified. Backbone models usually train 200 epochs; SimCLR backbone plus classifier gets 200+200.
- Input contents are images and invariant-class examples, not byte ranges or stored sequence addresses. Inversion is an analysis-only optimization with privileged front activations; no causal legal streaming access is established. Output agreement is a normalized probability-distribution agreement, not accuracy nor direct semantic truth.

## Independent non-revisit discriminator

Compare a frozen-source/frozen-receiver restricted connector against self, cross-seed, random-source and cue-disjoint controls. Add legal counterfactual input families and receiver-specific activations; separate train/test examples and match bridge/inversion/tuning cost and BN handling. If identity fails or approximately invariant inputs change the receiver for reasons unrelated to actual natural distribution, avoid treating either positive or negative stitch score as semantic identity. Alternatively improve native receiver/readout without any retrieval system; use task output and content interventions to decide whether it helps.
