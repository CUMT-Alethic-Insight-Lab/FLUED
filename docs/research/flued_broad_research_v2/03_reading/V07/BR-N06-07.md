# BR-N06-07 — LoLCATs: On Low-Rank Linearizing of Large Language Models

**Problem / one-sentence solution.** Convert a pretrained softmax language model to a lower-cache sequential model without expensive full-model retraining: learn query/key feature maps to match attention outputs, then fit low-rank adapters to recover next-token quality (§3.1).

**Objective and permissions.** Stage 1 uses frozen teacher projections/backbone and minimizes head/layer output mean-squared error on natural sequences; only added feature maps train. Teacher softmax outputs propagate to later layers, so same-input local match suppresses natural chain errors; stage 2 replaces attention in the actual whole stack and fits rank-8 LoRA on Q/K/V/O with next-token cross-entropy (§3.1; PDF pp.4–5). Stage 1 teacher forward and hidden-state extraction are additional costs. Cleaned Alpaca 50k samples train both stages. Appendix A.1 specifies AdamW, sequence length 1024, global batch 8, learning rates 1e-2 then 1e-4, two epochs per stage, early stopping and selection of best validation-perplexity checkpoint; patience is not specified. The main implementation uses 64-token terraced rather than strictly per-position sliding windows. For 70B/405B the no-transfer baseline jointly trains feature maps, mixing term and LoRA; LoLCATs freezes maps/mixing during LoRA. At 405B stage 1 adds attention-map cross-entropy to output MSE, uses blocks of nine and rank-4 LoRA (Appendix A.2–A.3).

**Units and deployment.** Causal language tokens are input and prediction units. Token q addresses past k/v; remote older tokens aggregate into per-head feature-map numerator and denominator states, while the most recent 64 tokens retain exact KV in the hybrid, with a learned mixing/normalization term (§3.3.1, Eq.6). These are bounded-window plus recurrent states, not a stand-alone memory lookup benchmark. Final loss/task performance measures deployed predictions; feature reconstruction by an offline teacher is diagnostic and not proof that individual content is naturally used.

**Controls, results and budget.** Table 2, HTML §3.2: Llama 3 8B Hedgehog no-transfer starts PPL 2448.01 and reaches 9.02 after 4 epochs LoRA; transfer starts 60.86 and reaches 3.90 after 2 epochs LoRA, with 2 earlier transfer epochs. This is an equal total-epoch/token **strategy** contrast (~40M tokens), not a 2×2 equal-dose effect of transfer or LoRA: the second-stage dose changes. Mistral Hedgehog corresponding numbers 561.47→4.87 versus 18.94→3.04. Initial attention transfer alone leaves large error; earlier simple variants lag original/full-parameter alternatives on 5/6 tasks, up to 42.4 points on MMLU (§3.2). The paper reports 73.1 vs 74.2 average LM-Eval and throughput measured at 128 prompt/4096 generation on one H100, not universal speed (§4). Blockwise teacher-forced transfer trades layer granularity for checkpoint/disk and compute; they estimate >200 TB to cache 50M tokens layerwise for 405B (§3.3.2). Longer-context preservation and alternative recurrent rules are explicitly left open (§5 limitations).

**Theory vs use / next test.** Kernel factorization makes recurrence possible for the defined feature map; it does not prove finite-width equivalence to softmax or universal retention. F.01–F.02/F.05–F.09/F.16; C.18 and E.20. Cross independent transfer and LoRA doses with unchanged-host/sham continuation, equal token and FLOP/teacher ledgers, same held-out long-context tasks, and compare teacher-fed local with fully autoregressive stack error. This distinguishes initialization, stage-specific optimization, cache companion and representational limitations. Appendix B.5.2/Table 21 gives an important task-specific negative/positive: at 8192 tokens, Alpaca-only linearization obtains 0% exact passkey retrieval in every placement decile, whereas training on 10K generated passkey examples yields 100%; extrapolation to 10240 fails for the linearized model. This is a trained task-format intervention, not universal retention. B.7.1's LoRA experiment optimizes local attention MSE rather than the usual end-to-end cross-entropy, so do not conflate the two.
