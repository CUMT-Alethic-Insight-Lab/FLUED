**Problem and one-sentence solution.** How much of a cross-task transfer gap is task specificity versus the difficulty of relearning downstream layers? Copy lower layers from either a source- or target-task pretrained ImageNet classifier, randomize and retrain the top, and contrast frozen with fine-tuned versions (§2, Fig.1).

**Technique and learning.** Supervised transfer learning, with Caffe AlexNet-style eight weight layers, is the measurement tool; labeled ImageNet classification supplies both base and target losses. SGD momentum 0.9, batch 256, learning rate 0.01 reduced tenfold every 100k, 450k iterations; each full training run ~9.5 K20-GPU days (Appendix A). Four random task splits, both directions (§4.1). Source and target each see ~645k labeled images of distinct 500-class subsets; dissimilar split has 551 man-made and 449 natural classes (Appendix C).

**Units, permissions and treatments.** Inputs are full images; internal content is convolutional activations, output a 500-way class prediction. There is no sequential query, addressable persistent memory or source reread in this test. `baseB` is an intact fully trained target network. `BnB` copies and freezes the bottom n layers from baseB but **randomly initializes and retrains every upper layer on B**; `AnB` does the same using the bottom of baseA and upper layers trained on B. `BnB+`/`AnB+` additionally fine-tune the copied bottom; random-source controls freeze randomly initialized bottom layers (§2, Fig.1; §4.3). Thus BnB is same-task reset/relearn, not intact replay or a self-stitch connector.

**Findings and scope.** §4.1/Fig.2 reports BnB loss at middle layers, especially 4–5, but near baseline at 1–2 and 6–7; BnB+ largely removes the gap. AnB versus BnB adds task-transfer and different-source-history effects; it does not cleanly isolate a universal coadaptation penalty across different hosts. Fine-tuned AnB+ improves mean generalization ~1.6 percentage points across layers 1–7 over baseB, versus equal-length BnB+ control; baseB is 62.5% top-1 on the random 500-class task. §4.3/Fig.3: deeper random frozen features approach chance; §4.2–4.3 report ~8 versus ~25 percentage-point final-layer drops for similar versus dissimilar splits. Confounds include 449/551 classes and task difficulty, not just semantic distance. Appendix B reports non-convergence for several small-data runs: random-feature failure is not a theorem.

**Inference and next discriminator.** Relevant F.03–F.07, F.13–F.16 and E.20. Use intact exact replay, identity-inserted self-stitch, BnB-style upper reset, then cross-host transfer with identical initialization/data and matched continuation. Compare several seeds and budgets: optimization failure, task specificity, random-top damage and cross-host coordinate mismatch are competing explanations.
