## Problem, mechanism and supervision

**Problem:** large unbounded dot-product logits can saturate attention, potentially impairing low-resource machine translation. **Solution:** normalize each query and key vector to unit length within its attention head, then multiply cosine logits by a learned scalar temperature instead of dividing by sqrt(head dimension) (§2–3 Eq. 2–5). Borrowed cosine normalization changes address scoring; values remain the native contextual vectors, not newly compressed memory. In bilingual encoder-decoder translation the source sentence is visible to cross-attention, encoder self-attention is bidirectional and decoder generates target autoregressively; target translations supervise cross-entropy rather than a retrieval target. No unknown-future-query long-delay state claim follows.

Scale starts at log2(L²−L), L the 97.5th percentile of training source/target sequence lengths, selected empirically rather than a proven optimum (Appendix B). Without learnable scaling, cosine scores lie in [−1,1], so especially many candidates force diffuse softmax; Appendix C Table 6 (p.4253), visually verified: removing g gives 24.53 BLEU versus the full model's 33.24 (Table 2); removing LayerNorm gives 31.56, FixNorm 32.63, FixNorm+PreNorm 32.20, and normalizing V as well 32.34. These are ablations within one en→vi setting, not a complete warmup/temperature factorial. LayerNorm and ScaleNorm differ in where/what dimensions they normalize and whether V is included (§2); do not conflate with per-head Q/K normalization.

## Comparator, findings and limitations

Five low-resource pairs: TED ar→en, sk→en, gl→en, en→he and IWSLT15 en→vi; BPE, FixNorm/PreNorm, validation checkpoint selection, 8,000-step linear warmup (§4). Combined QKNorm+LayerNorm vs published ScaleNorm+ordinary attention yields reported mean +0.928 BLEU (Table 2), but this is *not* a clean isolated QKNorm effect: layer normalization and warmup differ. Scoring/tokenization confounds are explicitly discussed; published en→vi baseline 32.79 versus their reproduced 32.48 Moses/32.41 SacreBLEU; their QKNorm SacreBLEU 33.18. A separate illustrative en→de direction is not claimed: §3's illustrative experiment is IWSLT16 **de→en**, 19.4 versus 24.33. Results do not quantify exact recall, conditional use, or learned-temperature effects at long contexts. Statistical bootstrap p<.01 reported for Table 2; no independent rerun.

## Directions, rival explanations, minimal test

C.09–C.13 and C.17: separating address geometry and scale matters, but reduced saturation is the authors' conjecture, not proof. Other candidates include LayerNorm choice, improved warmup, parameterization, or different convergence. Factorial original LayerNorm/ScaleNorm × ordinary/QK normalization × fixed/learned scale × warmup with same architecture, seed/data/compute, followed by held-out exact and semantic query shifts; measure logits, concentration, native values and final correct-content dependence separately. Visually checked Table 4 (p.4252) gives 32.40/33.16/33.24/32.42/32.30 BLEU for 2/4/8/16/32 heads. Table 5's sequence-length percentile choices 75/90/92.5/95/97.5/99/max yield 32.58/32.89/32.64/33.13/33.24/32.64/33.10; this is a limited en→vi sweep, not a generally validated initialization rule. Report uncertainty and selection conditions before generalizing.
