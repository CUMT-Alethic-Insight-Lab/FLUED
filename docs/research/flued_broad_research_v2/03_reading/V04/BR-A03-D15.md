## Problem and mechanism

**Problem:** standard softmax attention and its stability/output behavior have multiple coupled effects, while gating variants are often conflated with expert routing. **One-sentence solution:** train attention with a query-dependent, head-specific sigmoid multiplier on each head's post-attention output. Borrowed neural gate is a learned nonlinear filter, not a cache address or retrieval system (§2.2 Eq. 5). Alternative positions gate K, Q, V, and final output; headwise versus elementwise, shared versus distinct heads, additive/multiplicative and activation functions are explicitly compared (Table 1).

Causal decoder language modeling predicts next tokens; the gate derives from the current position's prenormalized hidden state, and modulates returned attention output after softmax, before output projection. Earlier positions' K/V are written to ordinary growing cache; gate is neither an independently stored semantic address nor a selective write. Value-side gate depends on each source position, in contrast to current-query-side output gate. Full model residual and FFN paths remain available (§§2, 4.2). It does not establish causal downstream consumption of a particular fact.

## Training, controls, observations

15B total/2.54B active MoE GQA and 1.7B dense models; up to 3.5T training tokens, length 4096, AdamW; 400B-token ablations §3.1–3.2. For MoE, matched/more-parameter comparators increase KV heads, Q heads or experts; Table 1 baseline PPL 6.026, output elementwise gate 5.761 (+201M), headwise 5.792 (+1.6M), value 5.820 (+25M), Q gate 5.981, K gate 6.016, final-output gate 6.017. Equal total parameters in dense controls require shrinking FFN, not identical capacity allocation (Table 2). 48-layer 1T baseline diverges at LR 8e-3; gated 7.078 PPL; sandwich normalization restores convergence in a distinct 400B comparison. Switch-V 1-of-1 = 5.808 PPL against 6.026 baseline, so gating alone can help without selecting multiple experts (Appendix A.1 Table 6).

Table 3 RMSNorm post-attention 5.847, parameter-free SiLU 5.975, additive identity 5.882: nonlinearity helps but does not isolate a unique cause. Table 4 mean output gate .116 versus input-independent .335, non-sparse sigmoid .653; first-token attention .048 versus baseline .467. Soft sigmoid near zero is threshold sparsity, not literal conditional skipping. Value gating substantially reduces large activations yet leaves first-token attention .297, separating two observations. Appendix A.2 compares against mean-gate scaling; A.5 clipping at 100 or 300 fails to stabilize LR 8e-3. Long context needs 80B extra 32k-token continuation before YaRN; after extension RULER 32k 72.88 vs 37.94, 128k 58.82 vs 31.65 (Table 5). Observational sink association is not proof of why extrapolation improves; authors explicitly lack a rigorous mechanism (§limitations). Latency claim <2% depends on their implementation; parameter/training and extension cost are separate.
