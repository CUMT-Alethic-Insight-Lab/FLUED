# BIW-0093 — Breaking the Softmax Bottleneck

**Reading: FULL**, [arXiv 1711.03953v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.03953v3), 9 February 2018; the PDF states published at ICLR 2018. **Problem → solution.** A linear word-vector softmax cannot represent all high-rank context-conditioned distributions with a low-dimensional head; mix several separately normalized softmax distributions with context-dependent weights. Borrowed technique: latent discrete mixture of experts, trained end-to-end by next-token likelihood, with exact probability summation (§2.4), not supervised segmentation or memory retrieval.

**Theory and proof.** For finite contexts and strictly positive true probabilities let A be the context×vocabulary log-probability matrix. All logit matrices realizing the same distributions are `A + b 1ᵀ`: softmax odds prove this, and the rank-one difference bounds rank changes by one. For arbitrary realizable context-vector/word-vector functions (the paper assumes a universal approximator), exact representation holds iff d is at least the minimum rank over these row shifts. Hence `d < rank(A) − 1` rules out exact representation, regardless of backbone expressiveness (App. A); true natural-language A being high rank is a hypothesis, not established ground truth. A probability-space mixture applies a log-sum-exp and can produce higher-rank log-probability matrices; mixing contexts *before* one softmax retains the factorized bottleneck. Higher rank is a representational possibility, not a theorem of generalization, learned retention, or correct content access.

**Comparators and costs.** PTB without fine-tuning/dynamic evaluation: MoS test PPL 55.97, parameter-matched MoC 57.55, conventional AWD-LSTM 58.95 (Table 5). Table 7: 15 mixtures 55.97 and rank 9981; 20 mixtures 56.17 despite same displayed rank. Dynamic-evaluation headline 47.69 includes test-time adaptation, not comparable to the unadapted result. On 1B Word 113M MoS versus 119M softmax: 37.10 vs 42.77 PPL; network shapes differ. Important negative control: text8 90M/5M/5M characters, tiny alphabet with embedding dimension exceeding alphabet size, parameter-matched models both 1.49 BPC or MoS 1.50 (App. C.2, Table 12). This is not a proof every 256-byte output head is sufficient, nor that byte models do not lose content: model dimensionality, restricted alphabet, task, and state remain relevant. Table 13 reports training slowdown e.g. PTB K=15 1.9× same batch / 2.8× best one-GPU; WT2 2.5× / 6.4× / 2.9× best three-GPU. Equal parameter count is not equal compute, activation memory, or optimization.

**Transfer.** Output categorical units are mostly words, separate from context-state and address. A stronger head can improve output fit without repairing the backbone and may conceal weak content. Directions I.12/I.16/A.05. Independent non-revisit options: head-only calibration, stronger linear/rank-controlled heads and conditional computation, versus independently changing state/optimization. A matched FLOP head factorial with frozen and jointly trained backbone, along with source-swaps and UTF-8-stratified byte loss, can distinguish readout capacity from actual retention/use.
