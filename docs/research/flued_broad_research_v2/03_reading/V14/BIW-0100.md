**Problem → solution.** Training one model for several byte-level BPE vocabularies could permit compact deployment at varying granularities without retaining multiple specialists. Ordered merge prefixes and shared row slices provide nested vocabularies; train with randomly drawn cap, optional cap token and output-vocabulary mask, then physically slice unused embedding/head rows. This borrows nested/Matryoshka parameterization and stochastic tokenization, but the units are BPE **tokens derived from bytes**, not a native byte output model. Each cap's deterministic tokenization changes both input and target; optimize next-token likelihood, score decoded target UTF-8 bytes with bits per byte (BPB). Input/output weights are untied, unlike tying variants.

**Protocol as reported (§§II–IV).** Caps 2,048/8,192/32,768 sampled with probabilities .2/.3/.5. XS/S bodies 3.1M/10.6M; paper says 30 models, 200M tokens each, batch 32×1024, FineWeb-Edu and held-out document decontamination. Paired seeds share initialization/data order, document bootstrap intervals. Fixed-token windows cover unequal source bytes, and per-cap exposure is unequal. Predeclared 3% early stop fired for the first two shared-model seeds at S and halted larger-tier continuation; XS factorial continued, and XS specialists after stop plus alternative-mixture and near-byte single-seed sensitivity experiments were outside preregistration (Fig. 2). The precise full preregistration/deviation list is unavailable, so no assertion of exact compliance or full run accounting.

**Results and controls.** Table I S shared vs specialists: 32k +3.640% BPB (document-bootstrap interval [+3.419,+3.861], margin 1%); 8k +2.958% ([+2.846,+3.071], margin 2%). Table II three-seed XS factorial: cap token without restriction +0.071–0.125% (intervals cross zero), output restriction without token +0.472–1.192% (worse). Interaction negative in each cell, but its interval excludes zero only at 32k: do not infer a uniformly significant interaction. Table III fine versus coarse mode is 12.5–15.4 percentage points less degraded by typo noise in these two tiers; blind multi-cap training yields 16.1 points vs full treatment 15.4 at XS. This supports an observed robustness benefit without the label/mask, not a proven augmentation mechanism or better clean orthographic accuracy: shared trails specialists by 8.3/11.3 points in those tests. BPE-dropout loses 1.40% at native cap vs 3.44% for multi-cap, whereas cap 2048 BPB is 1.9271 vs 1.4609 (§V.D); not equal native deployment objective.

**Deployment and uncertainty.** Across 76 checks the sliced and restricted-full logits were bit-identical on compared rows; Table IV 71.58→24.39 MB and 24.7→33.1 ms for full 32k vs sliced 2k on one fixed 10KB string. The 66% weight reduction is not unchanged latency at same raw input: 33.1/24.7 is ~34% slower, with 0.3556 vs 0.2236 tokens/byte; the paper's unchanged-latency claim compares slicing with the *same-cap restricted model*, not 32k against 2k. Prefill timing not end-to-end serving or total memory. Tying sensitivity reportedly improves every cap with 42% fewer parameters; no full factorial. Authors' data-dilution hypothesis predicts a smaller gap if per-cap tokens are fixed at higher total compute, not experimentally proved; model scale, exact original byte exposure and tokenizer variance remain limitations.

**Directions and independent alternatives.** I.13–I.17/I.24–I.27/F.17–F.18. Single fixed-cap or byte output, BPE dropout and head calibration are independent non-revisit routes. Per-cap raw-byte and total-FLOP matched specialist/shared tests, a tied factorial, equal-source-context controls and more seeds could change the exposure-vs-interference interpretation. Neither robustness nor this negative result resolves exact long-range content use.
