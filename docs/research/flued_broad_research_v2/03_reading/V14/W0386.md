**Problem → solution.** Chinese text lacks explicit word boundaries; learn a generative segmental character model from unsegmented sentences and decode the most probable boundary sequence. Borrowed techniques: recurrent sequence modeling, latent-variable marginalization and dynamic programming (forward sum versus Viterbi max). A context LSTM encodes past characters; a segment LSTM generates each successive character plus end-of-segment; all valid segmentations up to max length K contribute to unsupervised negative log-likelihood (§§2.2–2.4). Inference applies max-product recursion and traceback, not a learned pooling/compression operation. Finite K makes DP linear in sentence length for fixed K; runtime as K varies and decoder cost are not quantified. No proof is supplied for the paper's global normalization claim; it also requires an eventually terminating process.

**Units, permissions and supervision.** Inputs and generated targets are Chinese characters, not UTF-8 bytes. Segment scores condition on previous raw characters rather than inaccessible future characters; Viterbi segmentation of an observed sentence is offline and does not demonstrate deployed streaming-boundary latency. Unsupervised does not mean no prior resources: pretrained character embeddings from Chinese Gigaword and preprocessing replacing English/numbers/punctuation; both benchmark train and test raw text are used for training, with test annotations used for scoring (§3.1). Distinguish this transductive protocol from held-out generalization. The † results add 1,024 **segmented** sentences; * adds hand-coded postprocessing. Supplement splits particles with listed exceptions and merges dates. Neither enriched result is purely unsupervised.

**Findings.** Table 1: SLM-2 F1 PKU/MSR/AS/CityU 80.2/78.5/79.4/78.2; SLM-3 79.8/79.4/80.3/80.5; competing NPY-2 reports 80.2 on MSR and 82.4 on CityU. No universal superiority. SLM-4 PKU rises 79.2→81.9 with hand rules and 87.5 with 1,024 gold sentences; adding rules to gold decreases three datasets (Table 2). Larger K reduces insertion errors but increases deletion errors (Table 3). Baseline nVBE train-set and joint model initialization differ (footnote), so comparison is not perfectly controlled. Setup reports batch 256, initial SGD 400 steps then Adam, K=2–4; full compute not established.

**Directions and alternatives.** I.05–I.09/A.17–A.18. Non-revisit alternatives: direct causal byte prediction, fixed blocks, or jointly trained representation aggregation under length constraints. A matched byte/character causal implementation, separate boundary and pooling ablations and content-swapped long-range tasks could change whether induced boundaries help; this segmentation F1 does not measure memory content retention or native retrieval.
