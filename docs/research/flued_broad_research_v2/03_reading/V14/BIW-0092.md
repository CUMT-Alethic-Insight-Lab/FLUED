# BIW-0092 — nGPT

**Reading: FULL**, [arXiv 2410.01131v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.01131v1), 1 October 2024 preprint; this reading makes no claim about a later conference edition. [PDF](sources/BIW-0092.pdf), [text](sources/BIW-0092.txt); SHA-256 `34ca3431fcf3195276372661a935b95ce6583bdb85ba9a48660f6b7a90d68d0f`. Read all main sections and A.1–A.9; Fig. 1–3 and ablation tables are interpreted as reported, not redigitized curves.

**Problem → solution.** Uncontrolled vector norms and optimizer scale can make transformer training inefficient; normalize embedding/weight vectors and hidden states to a unit hypersphere while learning per-coordinate residual step sizes and nonlinear/logit scales. This borrows spherical representation learning and Riemannian retraction. Training remains causal next-token cross-entropy on OpenWebText, with a 32k LLaMA-2 tokenizer (§2, App. A.5); no byte-head experiment.

**What changes.** Input and output embeddings are separate unless explicitly tied (§2.1). Unit-normalized hidden and output vectors give cosine logits in [−1,1] before a learned per-vocabulary-element scale `s_z`; the latter is not just one global temperature. Q/K are normalized and separately scaled, attention scale adjusted; each attention/MLP residual step is a normalized interpolation with learned per-coordinate α; matrix vectors are normalized after optimizer steps. Adam replaces AdamW's weight decay 0.1 and 2,000-step warmup with zero decay and no warmup; initialization and effective learning rates of scales also change. Thus a comparison to the ordinary transformer is a bundled recipe, not a head-only intervention. Their optimization-on-a-sphere description is an analogy/parameterization, not proof that network blocks compute true task-loss gradients.

**Evidence and limits.** 468M/1.026B models, 64 A100s, batch 512, tuned initial learning rates. Authors report 4×/10×/20× fewer training tokens to comparable loss at 1K/4K/8K contexts (Fig. 2); 1B/4K reaches a comparable validation loss at 20K versus 200K steps (Fig. 1). Per-step time was ~80% higher at 4K and ~60% higher at 8K (App. A.4), so these are not direct equal-wall-clock speed ratios. App. A.8 isolates settings *within* nGPT: replacing per-token output-scale vector with a scalar or removing QK normalization slightly worsens validation loss in the shown setup, and jointly simplifying several scales changes more. For example no QK norm: +0.12% validation loss and 54.71 vs 54.44 average accuracy (Table 6, 0.5B, 1K, ~52B tokens). This does not isolate normalized output head against a baseline byte model. One 1B/8K learning-rate sensitivity needed α rescaling (A.6); no universal stability claim.

**Inference boundary and options.** Q/K geometry is an addressing-related feature; output vocabulary geometry changes readout, but neither measures stored content or its causal use. Directions I.15–I.17 and C.10–C.12. Independent no-revisit directions include output-only normalized head/learned scalar or per-byte scales, Q/K normalization alone, and optimizer schedule matching. Equal-byte/exposure/FLOP ablations and content substitution could overturn a head-specific attribution; normalize UTF-8 lead/continuation/ASCII metrics separately.
