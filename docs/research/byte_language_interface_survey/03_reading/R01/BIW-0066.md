# BIW-0066 — CharacterBERT

**Source.** Hicham El Boukkouri, Olivier Ferret, Thomas Lavergne, Hiroshi Noji, Pierre Zweigenbaum, Junichi Tsujii, *CharacterBERT: Reconciling ELMo and BERT for Word-Level Open-Vocabulary Representations From Characters*, [arXiv 2010.10392v3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.10392v3), 31 October 2020, COLING 2020. Alias S01/H15. **Reading: FULL**: PDF pp.1–13, §§1–6, Tables 1–3, Figures 1–8 and Appendix A.1; Figures 5–8 visually inspected, including numerical quartiles in Figure 8. No code execution/weights verified.

## Interface and roles

This is a word-tokenized English encoder with byte-composed words, **not** a raw-stream byte encoder or byte generator. The preprocessing supplies whole tokens/words (uncased BERT-base regime); each token is UTF-8 encoded, including non-ASCII, and represented with a 263-entry alphabet (256 bytes and seven specials) and maximum local length 50 including specials (§3.1 fn6). Exact truncation semantics and normalization beyond uncased setting are not stated. Character-CNN borrowed from ELMo embeds each byte in 16 dimensions, applies seven 1-D convolution banks of widths 1–7, max-pools and concatenates, uses two highway layers and projects to 768; this creates one word-position embedding for the 12-layer bidirectional BERT encoder (§3.1, Figure 2). Position and segment embeddings remain.

MLM plus next sentence prediction: only top-100k frequent whole words are eligible targets and tokens outside that set are never masked (§3.2, §5.1.1 fn12). Thus input open vocabulary does not mean open-vocabulary MLM head. Outputs at evaluation are entity labels, relation or inference classes, or a continuous sentence similarity score. No autoregressive sampling, EOS or verifier. Baseline BERT shares wordpiece input matrix and MLM output (§5.1.1); CharacterBERT replaces input lookup with CNN and uses a separate temporary 100k-word classifier: no direct input-byte-table ↔ word-softmax tie is documented; cross-task head sharing is not established. The paper attributes slower pretraining partly to loss of the baseline tying optimization, not a controlled tying-only ablation.

## Atomic findings

1. **[BIP-0136](../../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0136)** — Problem: general-domain WordPiece over-fragments specialized terminology. Solution: retain externally supplied word units but compose each from UTF-8 bytes with an ELMo-style CNN. On 1M medical tokens, ~13% of occurrences are whole in a medical vocabulary yet split by the general vocabulary (Figure 1, §2); this statistic alone does not demonstrate downstream causation. Example `choledocholithiasis`: 2 medical vs 7 general pieces (Table 1).
2. **[BIP-0137](../../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0137)** — Problem: open inputs and open targets are easily conflated. Solution: fixed 100k frequent word classifier limits the MLM prediction problem while allowing byte-composed input representations. Rare words receive no direct masking targets, although can appear as context. Since word positions are presegmented and 50-local-unit capped, neither unrestricted arbitrary raw text handling nor unlimited rare word recovery follows.
3. **[BIP-0138](../../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0138)** — Under matched pretraining recipes, medical CharacterBERT's nine-model ensembles improve over medical BERT roughly 1.5 ChemProt F1, 2 DDI F1, 0.5 MEDNLI/i2b2 points (§5.4, Figure 5); visually checked Appendix Figure 8 ensemble medians are 75.40 vs 73.94 ChemProt, 82.74 vs 80.20 DDI, 85.97 vs 85.65 MedNLI accuracy, 89.75 vs 89.51 i2b2 F1. **ClinicalSTS reverses**: 85.62 vs 86.08 Pearson ensemble median, with unstable single-model results and only 600 training examples. Do not generalize the medical gains to every task. Noise Figure 7 evaluates deletion/addition/replacement/swap per token; at 40% test-only noise the paper describes ~5-point advantage (the plot axis is accuracy although §5.5 calls it F1). Gains combine representation, output objective, preprocessing and training costs; not a CNN-only causal ablation.

## Budgets and controls

General data: English Wikipedia 2.14B and OpenWebText 1.28B tokens; medical MIMIC-III 505M and PMC OA abstracts 522M tokens (Table 2). Matched architectures 109.5M BERT vs 104.6M CharacterBERT; 16 V100-SXM2-16GB GPUs, LAMB, 3519 updates batch 8192 length 128 then 782 updates batch 4096 length 512; word vs wordpiece positions do not ensure equal original-text context/exposure (§4.2). BERT pretraining 26.5h, CharacterBERT 55h; average fine-tuning 19% slower but DDI training 14% faster and inference slightly faster in this setup (§5.1). Ten fine-tuning seeds, 15 epochs, single V100, Adam, mean/std and leave-one-out nine-model voting/averaging ensembles (§4.3). No external teacher, equal-FLOP or generation/serving budget established.
