# Multilingual byte-language interfaces: what coverage, robustness, and evaluation actually establish

## 1. The evaluation problem is larger than the alphabet

A small, universal input alphabet removes an important failure mode: the inability to encode a new spelling because the learned vocabulary has no suitable entry. It does not determine how much original text fits in context, what linguistic regularities are easy to learn, whether generation respects Unicode, or whether an answer is correct. These are different empirical questions. Multilingual evaluation becomes informative when it measures the transitions between them rather than treating “tokenizer-free” as an outcome.

The field has pursued several routes. Character composition shares information across related word forms; raw UTF-8 models make spelling directly available without a lexical input vocabulary; byte-based subwords combine coverage with fewer model positions; alternate encodings change script-dependent representation length; learned grouping changes where expensive computation occurs; and character-aware adaptation tries to preserve pretrained subword knowledge while improving surface-form access. Their mechanisms and evaluation contracts differ. A bidirectional encoder recognizing an entity, an encoder–decoder translating a complete sentence, and a decoder predicting the next byte are not alternative measurements of one ability.

This chapter follows the source-specific problems and techniques in the [detailed inventory](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md), principally [C01](../02_inventory/TAXONOMY.md#C01), [C10](../02_inventory/TAXONOMY.md#C10), [C11](../02_inventory/TAXONOMY.md#C11), and [C12](../02_inventory/TAXONOMY.md#C12). BIW identifiers denote works; BIP identifiers denote traceable findings or questions, not independent successful experiments. The governing conclusion is neither byte superiority nor subword superiority: **coverage, position allocation, accessible orthography, learned competence, probability accounting, and practical cost need separate measurements**. This separation permits positive findings to remain useful while retaining counterexamples that explain where an interface's advantages stop.

## 2. Define the string before testing the model

### 2.1 Bytes, code points, graphemes, and subwords

UTF-8 encodes Unicode scalar values using one to four bytes. Its legal sequences exclude surrogate values, overlong encodings, and values above U+10FFFF; continuation-byte syntax alone is insufficient to establish legality. A byte stream ending partway through a scalar may be a valid *prefix* of UTF-8 without being a complete valid string. These are normative properties, not learned-model results ([BIW-0090](../02_inventory/WORK_CATALOG.md#BIW-0090); [BIP-0112](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0112); RFC 3629 [§§3–4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3629.html#section-3)).

A Unicode code point is not necessarily a user-perceived character. Combining marks, conjoining Hangul, Indic sequences, and emoji sequences make that distinction consequential for counting, offsets, edits, and streaming display. Unicode's extended grapheme clusters provide a versioned, programmatically defined approximation to user-perceived characters; they are not identical to glyphs, morphemes, or every language's preferred editing unit. UAX #29 explicitly allows documented tailoring ([BIW-0116](../02_inventory/WORK_CATALOG.md#BIW-0116); [BIP-0217](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0217); [revision 47, §§1–3/Table 1a](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr29/tr29-47.html#Grapheme_Cluster_Boundaries)).

Consequently, a benchmark asking for the “third character” must choose a unit. Python-string indexing, UTF-8 byte offsets, and extended-grapheme indexing can return different answers without any model error. A named-entity span beginning inside a cluster may be a valid byte interval but an unsuitable user-interface boundary. Conversely, exact reproduction of a digital identifier may require original bytes even where a grapheme-based comparison would accept equivalent-looking text.

### 2.2 Normalization is part of the task contract

NFC/NFD address canonical equivalence; NFKC/NFKD additionally fold compatibility distinctions. Neither reproduces every original byte sequence. Compatibility normalization may remove stylistic distinctions and, in mathematical contexts, semantically important ones ([BIW-0115](../02_inventory/WORK_CATALOG.md#BIW-0115); [BIP-0218](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0218); [UAX #15 revision 57 §§1.1–1.2](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr15/tr15-57.html#Introduction)).

Evaluation therefore needs at least two explicitly named targets when appropriate: exact original-string preservation and task-authorized equivalence. Case folding, accent stripping, whitespace cleanup, spelling correction, and compatibility normalization cannot silently move from preprocessing conveniences into claims of robustness. A spelling-restoration task may require retaining precisely what a semantic classifier is allowed to ignore. The metric should reward the intended behavior, not a pipeline's undocumented destruction of distinctions.

## 3. Multilingual parity: representability does not equal equal opportunity

### 3.1 Position disparities survive universal coverage

Petrov et al. compare lengths on parallel FLORES content rather than training a new language model. In their Table 5, Shan/English length is 3.94 for UTF-8 bytes and 1.42 for code points; Cantonese/English is 0.87 and 0.31 respectively. Both representations still have substantial maximum/minimum disparities. The observation establishes unequal position consumption for those translations, not an intrinsic measure of each language's complexity ([BIW-0033](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B14/W0361.md), v2 §§3–4/Table 5; [BIP-0061](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0061)).

Parallel translations help hold content approximately constant, but translation direction, orthographic conventions, topic, and rendering choices remain relevant. Unknown-token collapse is not efficiency: a system discarding difficult characters can appear exceptionally short. The study excludes an UNK-heavy tokenizer from favorable parity interpretation. For practical evaluation, report both position ratios and retained information; neither universal byte coverage nor a low length ratio establishes learned access to unfamiliar scripts.

### 3.2 Three different interventions on unequal length

**Change the base encoding.** Universal Byte-Level Encoding routes scalar values between disjoint UTF-8 and UTF-16LE alphabets before BPE. Its solution attacks encoding overhead, while the causal LM still predicts subwords. At 32K vocabulary the reported configuration shortens 189/204 language–script configurations, yet Tibetan and Shan provide counterexamples elsewhere in the grid. Its primary three-seed LM means are 1.4900 versus 1.4854 BPB for UBE and ordinary byte BPE, so shorter encoding does not establish a general quality gain. The separate equal-text comparison changes position cost and must not be merged with the equal-step result ([BIW-0031](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B14/W0359.md), v2 §3, Tables D.1/D.2, E.8, F.5; [BIP-0055](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0055), [BIP-0219](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0219)).

**Encode recurrent morph-like strings.** MYTE uses language-specific Morfessor resources and variable-length codes, followed by ByT5-style span-denoising models. Morfessor supplies statistical segmentation; code construction supplies compression; the Transformer learns conditional prediction over the resulting codes. This is neither raw UTF-8 nor jointly learned neural boundaries. The lexicons, frequencies, capitalization policy, and decomposed-normalization wording are material resources and transformations ([BIW-0104](../03_reading/R07/BIW-0104.md), v2 §3/Appendix A; [BIP-0221](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0221)).

MYTE Table 2 reduces seen low-resource non-Latin mean length from 305 UTF-8 units to 145 code units, versus English 131 to 109. Unseen-script Ol Chiki Santali remains 365 to 365; its English-relative ratio worsens from 2.80 to 3.35 because English compresses. This is a particularly clear counterexample: improving known-language efficiency can increase disparity for an unsupported script without making that script's absolute representation longer.

The downstream story is mixed. Main Table 4 reports ByT5→MyT5 QA 73.2→75.3 F1, NER 81.5→80.8, semantic parsing 25.1→19.6 exact match, and low-resource MT 20.1→20.4 chrF. **Appendix B Table 8 instead reports low-resource/overall MT means of 21.1/21.8 for MyT5 and 21.9/23.1 for ByT5, both favoring ByT5.** Its columns are AVG LR then AVG, with MyT5 first. The main/appendix aggregation relationship remains unresolved. Table 8 contains Maltese (`mt`) and Shona (`sn`), whereas C.2 names Marathi and omits Shona, listing fourteen languages against fifteen columns. These are distinct reported aggregates, not numbers to average into a preferred ranking ([original Table 8/C.2 page](../03_reading/R07/sources/BIW-0104-p17.png); [current adjudication](../05_review/CLAIM_RULES.md#5-myte-explicitly-anchor-both-model-row-and-aggregate-column)).

MYTE's reported loss denominator is parallel **English** UTF-8 length, with the source's +1 convention, not each target language's byte length. Its missing minus sign and unspecified log base leave a notation/scoring-contract question; they do not prove negative surprisal was computed. Equal 250K training steps also do not match original-text exposure, FLOPs, or morphology preprocessing. Original-byte reversibility after normalization is not established.

**Allocate different internal rates.** MAGNET learns script-specific byte boundaries using a small Transformer, Gumbel sampling, per-script binomial priors, segment means, repeated upsampling, and byte skips. This changes expensive internal positions rather than the external encoding. On nine languages across three script groups, XQUAD mean 44.62 for bytes is essentially matched by 44.61 at one rate setting, while aggressive compression gives 41.06 and larger subgroup losses: Hindi 29.18→21.23 and Telugu SIB 68.63→53.90. Its entire-sequence script selection assumes a single script; mixed-script online routing is not resolved ([BIW-0045](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B26/W0510.md), v2 §§2–5/Table 3/Appendix D; [BIP-0222](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0222), [BIP-0223](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0223)).

These interventions target different causes of length. They should be compared through task-specific quality–cost curves, including per-language losses, not treated as interchangeable fairness solutions.

### 3.3 Dialect evidence does not give a universal sign

The dialect study defines tokenization parity as a language/English length ratio and information parity as an English/language total-negative-log-likelihood ratio. Neither is task accuracy or fairness itself. Its formal Table 8 reports dialect-identification tokenization-parity correlations of +0.638 for Phi, +0.4836 for mBERT, and −0.380 for Llama; information-parity correlations are −0.413 for Phi and +0.268 for Llama. Topic classification and extractive QA have different patterns ([BIW-0134](../03_reading/R10/BIW-0134.md), EMNLP 2025 §§3–5/Appendix F Table 8; [BIP-0124](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0124)).

The later prose describing a positive Llama correlation follows information parity and does not unambiguously contradict the negative TP column. The correct account names each metric, rather than manufacturing a definite TP sign conflict ([original passage](../03_reading/R10/sources/BIW-0134.txt), lines 270–319; [latest correction](../05_review/CLAIM_RULES.md#7-new-r10-clarification-do-not-manufacture-a-dialect-tp-prose-contradiction)). The hypothesis that finer fragments help surface dialect discrimination remains a hypothesis: model pretraining, task data, full encoder fine-tuning, and quantized low-rank decoder adaptation are entangled. Likewise, Latin-looking displays of byte-mapped fragments are not proof of corrupted Unicode; the paper itself notes that decoding may reassemble them.

## 4. Morphology and scarce-data utility: meaningful gains without a universal ranking

Character-aware modeling originally addressed sparse word types, not Unicode validity. Kim et al.'s character CNN/highway/LSTM improves several next-word comparisons by sharing word-internal patterns, but retains closed word outputs. Arabic ties a morphology comparator, and a published Russian morphology model beats its character counterpart. Its filters generally do not align with valid morphemes: successful composition need not be explicit morphological segmentation ([BIW-0055](../03_reading/R02/BIW-0055.md), Tables 4–7; [BIP-0126](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0126)).

Fully character-level NMT then used CNN filters, max pooling, highway layers, and bidirectional recurrence to shorten the source while generating characters. In Lee et al., Russian transliteration to Latin is a deliberate additional sharing intervention. The multilingual model benefits several directions, but bilingual Finnish character→character BLEU 13.10 trails BPE→character 13.98; a long German numeral is mistranslated by both systems. Illustrative examples are not morphology or noise benchmarks ([BIW-0059](../03_reading/R03/BIW-0059.md), v3 §§4–5/Tables 5–7; [BIP-0088](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0088)).

ByT5 provides stronger task-specific evidence for bytes. Large-model comparisons report transliteration character error 10.5 versus mT5's 18.1, grapheme-to-phoneme error 1.8 versus 6.7, and morphology accuracy 89.7 versus 75.7. These are separately supervised tasks with different metrics, not three measures of general causal language modeling. Removing a large vocabulary also reallocates parameters into dense computation, and the training recipe uses different encoder/decoder allocation ([BIW-0024](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B08/W0351.md), formal Table 5/§3; [BIP-0220](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0220)).

XTREME-UP makes scarce in-language annotation part of the problem rather than privileging zero-shot evaluation. Its task averages favor ByT5 over mT5 on several user-facing technologies, including transliteration CER 14.6 versus 37.6 and semantic-parsing exact match 37.5 versus 21.8. Yet preliminary autocomplete contains Belarusian, Greek, Hebrew, and Uyghur reversals; OCR is the identical external baseline. The benchmark's estimated eight-hour annotation budgets exclude some development overhead, and the MT benchmark population versus baseline evaluation population is not completely reconciled. It does not establish byte wins in every covered language ([BIW-0105](../03_reading/R07/BIW-0105.md), v2 Table 4/Appendix B; [BIP-0227](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0227)).

Endangered-language byte NMT likewise combines interface and transfer. One-hot byte inputs, a shared multilingual Transformer, high-resource pretraining, and fine-tuning improve several Indigenous-language results, but multilingual training slightly hurts some high-resource directions. Its byte-permutation ensembles average inverse-aligned probabilities from independent models; their gains are not evidence of typo robustness, and an ordinary same-encoding ensemble control is absent ([BIW-0106](../03_reading/R07/BIW-0106.md), COLING 2022 §§3–5/Figures 5–6; [BIP-0224](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0224), [BIP-0225](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0225)).

Understanding-task evidence also reverses with supervision and tuning. The multidimensional evaluation finds mBERT strong in scarce-label settings and a longer-tuned TyDi baseline overtaking CANINE on average. Byte versus subword rankings vary by task and language. This makes annotation quantity and baseline optimization part of interface evaluation, not optional details ([BIW-0032](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B08/W0360.md), formal §§3–4/Appendix Tables 4–7; [BIP-0056](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0056), [BIP-0057](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0057)).

## 5. Robustness is a relation between a task and a corruption process

### 5.1 Finer inputs do not create the needed invariance

Belinkov and Bisk expose a foundational counterexample. Character-to-character, character-composed word, and BPE translators all degrade under synthetic and harvested natural edits. German clean→natural BLEU falls 34.79→14.02 for charCNN and 29.97→12.68 for char2char; full scrambling is devastating. The natural-noise condition applies lexicon-derived substitutions to clean text, not a naturally collected noisy parallel corpus ([BIW-0109](../03_reading/R07/BIW-0109.md), v2 §§3–4/Table 3; [BIP-0228](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0228)).

The paper offers two different solutions. Averaging characters within each source word imposes scrambling invariance, but sacrifices order distinctions and clean performance. Training one model on mixed random, keyboard, and natural noise learns broader coverage: German clean/random/keyboard/natural BLEU becomes 31.29/30.04/29.81/31.60. Natural-only training performs better on its own noise yet fails badly on unseen corruption types. The mixture trades clean and specialist performance for breadth; it is not an inference ensemble. External spelling correction helps German and worsens Czech. The lesson is mechanism-specific: representation access, imposed invariance, and learned noise coverage solve different problems.

ByT5's denoising-based robustness is a later positive result, especially for casing perturbations, but not a theorem about every byte architecture. TokSuite evaluates multiple-choice behavior under varied surface perturbations and finds substantial variation even among byte-related external models. Its Unicode-style results also show why normalization matters: NFKC can remove much of a perturbation before the network sees it, while failing exact preservation. Its four-tokenizer same-text control is separate from the main equal-token comparison, and the affected Figure 6 BPB computation remains unverified ([BIW-0051](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B22/W0518.md), v2 §§3–5/Appendices E–G; [BIP-0079](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0079), [BIP-0080](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0080), [claim rule §4](../05_review/CLAIM_RULES.md#4-toksuite-exclude-an-unverified-figure-inference-not-the-whole-study)).

CharacterBERT and Char2Subword show useful hybrid alternatives. CharacterBERT composes presegmented words from UTF-8 bytes with CNN/highway layers and improves several medical tasks and test-noise results, but ClinicalSTS reverses and its MLM targets remain a fixed frequent-word list. Char2Subword combines embedding-geometry imitation, character perturbations, frozen-host MLM adaptation, and optional lexical fallback; its error analysis also identifies ambiguity when augmentation erases distinctions shared across languages. Neither is a native byte-output LM ([BIW-0066](../03_reading/R01/BIW-0066.md), v3 §5/Figures 5–8; [BIW-0122](../03_reading/R06/BIW-0122.md), v3 §§3–5; [BIP-0138](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0138), [BIP-0248](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0248)).

### 5.2 Coverage and legal output are separate robustness tests

The tokenizer-imbalance study independently varies tokenizer-training proportions and model-training sampling. Its byte-fallback control achieves zero unknowns without reliably preventing translation collapse; results differ by direction and language. Adding held-out characters rescues one Chinese setting but not Tamil. Alphabet coverage is therefore not a substitute for learning enough useful representations from the actual model-training mixture ([BIW-0107](../03_reading/R07/BIW-0107.md), AMTA 2022 §§3–4/Figures 7, 10/Table 3; [BIP-0226](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0226)).

Output validity requires another instrument. *Beyond Perplexity* tests a 355M, 8K-BPE-plus-byte-fallback model—not a pure-byte model—and reports common-character partial progress 96.21% but strict validity 50.47%; rare and unseen groups show similar large gaps. Intended-character accuracy is a further outcome. The diagnostic varies supplied prefix offsets, so byte length and remaining completion difficulty must be separated from frequency ([BIW-0044](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B22/W0509.md), v2 §§3–5/Appendices E–H; [BIP-0063](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0063)–[0065](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0065)).

Strict raw-stream validation should precede repair. ByT5's documented `errors='ignore'` decoding can drop invalid bytes; its paper reports not observing invalid sequences, not a constrained generator proving impossibility. A repaired string can pass a text metric while hiding raw output failure. Conversely, buffering an unfinished scalar during streaming is not itself an error. Report malformed completion, incomplete termination, repaired display, intended scalar, and task correctness separately.

## 6. Spelling access, character skills, and numeracy

SpellingBee asks whether subword embeddings retain recoverable orthography. A separately trained Transformer probe spells held-out vocabulary items from frozen token embeddings; random-vector controls fail exact match, and similarity/lemma filtering substantially reduces the trained models' scores. GPT-2-medium exact spelling falls from 32.9% without filtering to 16.5% after the strongest filter. This establishes recoverability by a learned decoder, not the host LM's ability to execute a spelling instruction ([BIW-0110](../03_reading/R10/BIW-0110.md), NAACL 2022 §§2–3/Table 1; [BIP-0229](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0229)).

Its separate intervention is equally important: spelling-pretrained embeddings reach 93.5% probe exact match, yet masked-LM losses differ by less than 0.01 after 1,000 updates in the reported training run. Readability by a probe does not establish utility for that objective ([BIP-0230](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0230), §5/Figure 2).

The Strawberry study instead adds an explicit character Transformer and block-causal cross-attention to token states, while retaining subword outputs. Its supervised synthetic transformations learn earlier with the character channel. The reported onset threshold is accuracy above **0.5%**, not 50% mastery. Wikipedia-input editing results remain only a few percent exact match at the plotted endpoint. This is evidence that access can improve task-supervised learning, not that all natural LM competence follows from exposing spellings ([BIW-0113](../03_reading/R10/BIW-0113.md), EMNLP 2025 §§3–4/Figures 3–7/Table 2; [BIP-0233](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0233)).

CharBench separates frequency counting, unique counting, and first/last position. Across seven prompted subword LMs, its averages are 77.34%, 47.96%, 43.64%, and 32.37%. Token-related correlations differ by model and task: GPT-4o positional accuracy is strongly associated with target-token length, but that feature does not dominate every model/task. Word length, answer magnitude, and segmentation covary; malformed-output exclusions and guessing floors are not sufficiently documented to infer them. English string questions are not multilingual grapheme competence ([BIW-0114](../03_reading/R07/BIW-0114.md), proceedings Tables 1–3 and separately scoped v3 appendices; [BIP-0234](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0234)).

Numeracy adds another layer. Digit access does not supply carry propagation, place-value alignment, or a numerical algorithm. The selected-primary record for *Tokenization counts* motivates right-to-left grouping and rewriting decimal strings in subword models, but its later controls were not fully read; no quantitative result or native-byte superiority is inferred here ([BIW-0111](../02_inventory/WORK_CATALOG.md#BIW-0111); [BIP-0231](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0231), designated A/P opening/Figure 1 scope). A field protocol should test transcription separately from arithmetic, vary carry-chain length and grouping alignment, and include non-ASCII decimal digits and locale-specific separators under explicit numerical equivalences. Current evidence for non-ASCII numeracy is thin, not evidence that bytes solve or fail it.

## 7. Exact copy and long dependencies need their own tests

Low next-byte loss does not establish exact long copying. A single substituted accent, omitted combining mark, or wrong digit can invalidate an identifier while barely moving average loss. Attention concentrated on a source phrase is also not a generated-copy measurement: the byte-scaling study's short ASCII attention analysis is such a diagnostic, not whole-string exact match ([BIW-0028](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B14/W0356.md), v1 Appendix E.1; [BIP-0053](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0053)).

*Repeat After Me* supplies a sharper capacity distinction. For a uniform length-L string over D symbols, a deterministic single-read finite-state system with state set S has whole-string success at most |S|/D^L. This counting argument concerns synthetic symbols, bounded distinguishable state, and exact whole-string recovery, not every natural-language sequence. External memory, growing state, or rereading changes the premise. Query-first filtering can also be much easier than answering a query revealed after ingestion ([BIW-0021](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B04/W0061.md), v2 §2.3/Appendix D/§3.5; [BIP-0235](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0235)).

Architectural access must be distinguished from trained success. In Hourglass without fine-resolution Transformer layers, the shortened branch lacks current-group history, but the shifted-input residual still carries the immediately preceding symbol. Appendix A.1 demonstrates a particular two-position-back copying restriction, not blanket within-group independence. A fine-resolution attention layer removes that restriction in the construction, without guaranteeing learned arbitrary copying ([BIW-0072](../03_reading/R03/BIW-0072.md), v2 §2.2/Appendix A.1; [BIP-0104](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0104); [corrected wording](../05_review/CLAIM_RULES.md#6-hourglass-restrict-the-independence-claim-to-the-information-path)).

Full-resolution alternatives matter too. Transformer-XL retains byte positions and reuses stopped-gradient segment activations; Sparse Transformer retains positions but changes attention edges. Sparse Transformer's fixed pattern gives 0.99 BPB versus dense 1.00, whereas its strided pattern gives 1.13 despite a smaller reported Time/Iter. These enwik8 results show that longer computational reach and cheaper connectivity are not interchangeable with effective dependencies or multilingual copy ([BIW-0119](../03_reading/R09/BIW-0119.md), formal §3/Table 2; [BIW-0118](../03_reading/R09/BIW-0118.md), v1 §7.2/Table 2; [BIP-0236](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0236), [BIP-0238](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0238)).

## 8. Fair scoring starts with the probability event

For matching scored events with negative log probability in nats, byte-normalized accounting is

\[
S=\frac{-\sum_i\ln q(e_i\mid c_i)}{B\ln 2}
 =\frac{\overline{\mathrm{CE}}_{\mathrm{nats}}N}{B\ln2},
\]

where N counts the actual labels in the mean and B counts matching original bytes. PAD, prompts, EOS, normalized targets, masked spans, and overlapping evaluation windows affect this correspondence. Sum losses and byte totals before aggregation when reporting a corpus-weighted result. Dividing cross-entropy by log₂ vocabulary size is not a general BPB conversion ([scoring rules](../05_review/CLAIM_RULES.md#22-valid-byte-normalized-accounting); [BIP-0286](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0286)).

The denominator does not change the event. A canonical subword-path score normalized by original bytes is a legitimate, transparently labeled quantity, but is not automatically the probability of all token sequences decoding to that text. Marginal-likelihood evaluation sums compatible segmentation paths; importance sampling adds support, estimator-variance, and nonlinear-log qualifications. Cao and Rimell's study uses word-normalized document perplexity and disables Transformer-XL recurrence for differently tokenized batches; it is not an exact native-byte scoring experiment ([BIW-0080](../03_reading/R04/BIW-0080.md), EMNLP 2021 §§2–4; [BIP-0109](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0109)).

An unfinished raw-text prefix introduces a further distinction. Encoding that prefix as tokens can condition on a narrower event than observing those bytes, even under an ideal trained token process. Prefix-cover algorithms address this mismatch by summing compatible histories. Their exactness requires the stated lossless/canonical-support process; masking noncanonical neural token mass and renormalizing changes the original model. Completed-text marginalization, arbitrary-prefix conditionals, and boundary-marked histories cannot be collapsed into one “exact byte probability” ([BIW-0132](../03_reading/R04/BIW-0132.md), v2 §§2–3/Appendices G–H; [BIW-0088](../03_reading/R04/BIW-0088.md), v2 §4/Appendix A.6; [BIP-0274](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0274), [BIP-0111](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0111)).

For multilingual comparison, ordinary BPB additionally rewards or penalizes the physical encoding's length. Parallel-sentence total bits and an explicitly English-normalized quantity answer different questions from target-byte BPB. Report them separately, alongside task scores. A low loss on gold answers does not establish successful generation; a denoising or composite future-window objective is not ordinary left-to-right likelihood without recovering the appropriate numerator.

## 9. Resource matching changes the scientific question

A fair comparison needs multiple controlled views, because equal text, equal positions, equal total parameters, and equal FLOPs cannot generally all hold simultaneously.

ByT5 and mT5 train with the same input-position budget, yet mC4 averages about 4.1 UTF-8 bytes per subword, varying roughly 2.5–9 by language. ByT5 therefore receives substantially less original text under that recipe. Its tiny vocabulary also allows more dense parameters in the same total-size class. These differences are part of the tested systems, not isolated effects of the alphabet ([ByT5 formal §§3/7/Tables 1, 9](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B08/W0351.md); [BIP-0040](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0040)).

The byte-scaling study makes the distinction explicit: its fitted byte models lead on the reported parameter frontier, while subwords lead on the training-FLOP frontier. The backbone is a **3:1 Gated DeltaNet/Gated Softmax Attention hybrid**, not a dense-attention-only baseline; “flat” means no explicit local–global–local hierarchy. Its fitted parameter count includes output parameters but excludes input/hash tables, and its compute formula omits several operations. Thus this is a real budget-dependent reversal under the paper's convention, not a whole-resident-memory or measured wall-time result ([BIW-0028](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B14/W0356.md), v1 §2/Figure 1/Appendices A–C; [BIP-0050](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0050); [adopted correction](../02_inventory/AUTHOR_HANDOFF.md#other-adopted-corrections)).

Data packing matters after sampling. *Language-Specific Effects* reports tokenizer-dependent removal of 30.4–43.5% of sampled original text and language-dependent repetition effects. “Same mixture” does not establish equal retained exposure. Its downstream tasks near chance also cannot support strong claims of capability equivalence ([BIW-0050](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B22/W0516.md), v1 Appendix A.4/§§5–7; [BIP-0075](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0075), [BIP-0076](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0076)).

Compression is not a sufficient quality proxy either: PathPiece can minimize token count while underperforming BPE on the tested English multiple-choice average. Vocabulary construction, pretokenization, and inference segmentation must be separated ([BIW-0131](../03_reading/R08/BIW-0131.md), EMNLP 2024 §§4–6/Appendix G; [BIP-0268](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0268), [BIP-0269](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0269)).

Serving needs real task measurements. ByT5 Table 10's 6.4–9.5× slowdown is **XNLI**, whose sentence-pair input has a short label output, not NER; it cannot establish a long-output cause. A separate V100 study finds ByT5 slightly faster than mT5 on English XNLI but much slower on NER. Local Byte Fusion can train faster per fixed updates yet infer slower; Lao→English BPE 9.2 exceeds dense bytes 5.9 and both fusion arms 6.4/6.5 ([BIW-0032](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B08/W0360.md), Appendix Table 3; [BIW-0048](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B26/W0514.md), v3 Table 3/Appendix B; [BIP-0041](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0041), [BIP-0072](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0072)). These are conditional counterexamples, not a cross-paper speed leaderboard.

## 10. A field evaluation protocol

The following protocol is a synthesis recommendation, not a benchmark already executed by the cited works. It accommodates raw-byte, code-point, character, subword, and hybrid systems without requiring any particular hierarchy or local project.

### 10.1 Freeze the interface and data contract

For every arm, publish input serialization, internal position/update units, output symbols, supervision/corruption units, normalization, unknown handling, reversible domain, and termination policy. State full-source encoder access, causal target access, suffix-conditioned formats, offline segmentation hints, and external script labels. Distinguish ordinary fixed-weight evaluation, persistent hidden-state reuse, and test-time weight updates. Record exact source/model/tokenizer versions and sharing only on axes actually documented.

Keep original bytes and a reversible offset map to each permitted processed view. Test round trips on representative valid text, combining sequences, non-BMP scalars, whitespace, control characters, and mixed scripts. Invalid byte inputs belong in a separately declared stress domain; a scalar-input system need not pretend to model arbitrary binary data. Publish rejected examples and transformation counts by language.

### 10.2 Sample linguistic variation rather than only language names

Use both human-reviewed parallel content and independently sourced natural documents. Stratify by resource level, script, morphology, whitespace convention, encoding length, domain, and dialect; language and script must not be synonyms. Include scripts absent from tokenizer training and, separately, absent from model training. Add naturally mixed-script and code-switched examples, then matched controlled insertions to diagnose boundaries. Keep translation direction and alternatives visible when interpreting length parity.

Separate independent training seeds from evaluation-language cells and repeated sentences. Pair comparisons on the same examples; uncertainty estimates should respect document, language, and training-run structure. Report macro language averages, corpus-weighted totals, subgroup distributions, and worst-group changes. Predeclare acceptable task degradation at a target cost rather than letting a favorable mean erase subgroup harm.

### 10.3 Use a task matrix with distinct outcome instruments

| Evaluation family | Primary outcome | Necessary diagnostic/control |
|---|---|---|
| Encoding and coverage | Exact encode/decode round trip; rejected/unknown fraction | Original and processed lengths in bytes, scalars, graphemes, and model positions |
| Unicode continuation | Complete strict UTF-8 validity | Valid unfinished prefix, bytes remaining, raw versus repaired output, intended scalar |
| Surface operations | Whole-string exact match and unit-specific edit distance | Copy, insertion, deletion, replacement, case and diacritic operations; explicit index unit |
| Morphology/translation | Task accuracy, chrF or other declared task measure | Rare forms, held-out lemmas/paradigms, language-specific validation, clean controls |
| Noise robustness | Absolute clean/noisy task scores and paired change | Natural edits versus synthetic noise, severity and affected-unit counts, unseen noise types |
| Numeracy | Numerical answer correctness plus exact transcription | Carry length, grouping, numeral script, separators, answer-format validity |
| Long dependencies | Generated whole-span/whole-document correctness | Distance in original text, distractors, query timing, repeated keys, source-absent baseline |
| Language modeling | Named-event log loss with declared denominator | Canonical path/marginal/prefix distinction; EOS, overlap, PAD, prompt and normalization accounting |
| Serving | End-to-end latency, throughput, memory at matched quality | Hardware, precision, batch/concurrency, prompt/output text, preprocessing and all auxiliary models |

For spelling probes, keep recoverability distinct from the host's generated behavior. For multiple choice, state whether scoring is label likelihood, complete option likelihood, normalized path score, or generated selection; randomize labels and include chance/majority baselines. Publish parser failures instead of excluding malformed answers silently.

### 10.4 Make robustness falsifiable

Apply the same underlying corruption to paired inputs before each interface's preprocessing. Record changed words, code points, graphemes, and bytes; equal byte-edit rates need not be equal linguistic severity. Separate semantically preserving perturbations from transformations that change the answer. Use clean, in-distribution noisy, unseen-noise, and adversarially selected conditions. A normalizer or spellchecker is an explicit additional component with its own cost and failure cases.

Report accuracy retention together with clean ability. Include a task-appropriate constant/majority baseline and an ordinary subword model with matched noise augmentation. For finite-vocabulary or hybrid outputs, test unseen input forms and unseen *required outputs* separately. Analyze where normalization helps classification but breaks exact copy, numerals, names, or mathematical distinctions.

### 10.5 Stress long dependencies independently of local prediction

Vary source length, dependency distance, target length, and distractor count separately. Test query-before and query-after ingestion, complete and partial-prefix prompts, and positions near internal group/window boundaries. Include random strings for capacity stress and natural multilingual text for realistic redundancy. Require generated outputs, not teacher-forced accuracy alone.

Use exact original-byte copying where fidelity is the task and an additional canonical/grapheme-aware score where warranted. Replace the relevant source span with a matched alternative and check whether the output follows it; remove it to establish the task's no-source floor. This tests dependence on the supplied information without presupposing an external memory mechanism. Record truncation, rereading, external storage, and persistent-state bytes as resources.

### 10.6 Publish separate exposure, compute, and deployment comparisons

First match retained original text and original-text context to study representation/learning differences. Next match end-to-end training compute, allowing text exposure or model size to vary, to study efficiency. Finally compare measured serving at matched task quality, prompt content, and output requirements. Each view should state what was allowed to change.

Count unique and repeated retained text per language, update count, positions, dense and lexical parameters, training-only heads, tokenizer/morphology induction, teachers, drafters, and hyperparameter search. Unknown costs remain unknown rather than zero. Match corruption/dropout semantics as well as their nominal probabilities: the embeddingless-byte NMT study shows that token-dropout controls can reverse an apparent representation advantage ([BIW-0068](../03_reading/R01/BIW-0068.md), NAACL 2021 §6/Tables 3–4; [BIP-0139](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0139)).

At inference, include prefill, output decoding, Unicode buffering, repair, external models, and search. Report original bytes/s and completed scalars or graphemes/s alongside request latency; time to first byte can differ from time to first complete scalar or stable displayed unit. Specify whether end-of-string, maximum-length truncation, or token healing changes the workload. A generation algorithm must name its target distribution and any constraints; higher acceptance or parallel prediction alone does not prove either exactness or speed.

## 11. Open questions and synthesis

The strongest evidence supports conditional propositions: fine interfaces help selected spelling, morphology, scarce-data, and perturbation tasks; byte completeness removes unknown-symbol dependence; alternate encodings and learned rates can reduce position disparities; and explicit character access can accelerate supervised transformations. Equally substantive negatives show that coverage does not ensure robustness, compression does not ensure task quality, and low local loss does not establish valid or exact long output.

Important gaps remain in grapheme-preserving long generation, non-ASCII arithmetic, unseen-script output learning, general prefix-computable mixed-script routing, and matched-quality batched serving with first-complete-scalar and tail-latency measurements. Most character-skill studies do not supply controlled native-byte training comparisons; several multilingual studies do not jointly match retained exposure, capacity, compute, and supervision. These are missing comparisons, not demonstrated failures.

A useful field agenda is therefore to measure where each interface loses information, spends computation, and gains usable task access. Encoders, translation systems, and causal generators should meet the contracts appropriate to their roles. The resulting evidence can guide raw-byte, code-point, subword, or hybrid design without making one project, one compression mechanism, or one granularity the presumed destination of the literature.
