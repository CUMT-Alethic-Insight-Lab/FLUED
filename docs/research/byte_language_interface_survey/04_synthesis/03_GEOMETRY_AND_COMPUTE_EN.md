# Geometry, optimization, and the real cost of byte-language interfaces

## 1. A smaller alphabet changes the allocation of work, not just the vocabulary table

The computational attraction of a byte interface is easy to state: a language model can replace a large learned lexical alphabet with a small, fixed set of byte values. The consequence is less simple. It exchanges vocabulary-dependent parameters and classification work for more positions, more fine-grained prediction events, and, under ordinary autoregressive decoding, more sequential decisions. Whether that exchange is favorable depends on where the expensive computation occurs, how representations are supervised, and what is being held constant. Weight tying and output normalization belong inside this accounting: they constrain the relationship between representation and prediction, but they do not determine the frequency or cost of contextual processing.

This chapter develops three connected arguments. First, sharing must be specified as a relationship between named parameter sets rather than a model-wide property. Second, a small output alphabet changes the relevance of large-vocabulary regularization and rank arguments without settling the empirical choice between tied and untied interfaces. Third, quality, training operations, text exposure, persistent state, and serving latency define different frontiers. A method can improve one and lose on another. These are not exceptional qualifications: the selected literature contains concrete reversals on each axis.

The underlying [detailed inventory](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md) records source-specific problems, solutions, actual techniques, controls, and negative findings; its BIP identifiers are not counts of independent experiments. Here the principal connections are [sharing and geometry](../02_inventory/TAXONOMY.md#C07), [optimization](../02_inventory/TAXONOMY.md#C08), and [budget and implementation evidence](../02_inventory/TAXONOMY.md#C12). Generic optimization and word/subword theory below are labeled **background**. They explain possible mechanisms, not matched-byte causal findings.

### Four interfaces before one efficiency claim

A byte input does not require byte-sized internal positions, byte outputs, or byte-only supervision. The following distinctions organize the comparisons that follow.

| Family and carrying example | Input | Expensive/internal positions | Output | Supervision and visibility |

## 2. Weight sharing: identify the axis before explaining the benefit

### 2.1 The historical argument was largely about word tables

In **background** work, Press and Wolf's *Using the Output Embedding to Improve Language Models* ties a word input table to the transpose of the word classifier. Their motivation combines parameter economy with different update patterns: a lookup row receives an input-path contribution when its symbol occurs, while a full softmax ordinarily contributes gradients to every output row. Sharing adds the two paths. Their PTB large model moves from 66M parameters and 78.4 test perplexity to 51M and 74.3; this is a useful result, but not an equal-parameter byte experiment. In low-dropout settings, a regularized hidden-to-embedding projection helps some configurations; it does not help their large dropout-regularized model. Tying also worsens their skip-gram similarity results. The paper therefore supports task- and recipe-dependent reuse, not an intrinsic identity between optimal input and output geometry. [BIW-0035 card](../03_reading/R08/BIW-0035.md); [1608.05859v3, §§2–4, Tables 2, 5–8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.05859v3); [BIP-0184–0186](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0184).

Inan, Khosravi, and Socher separate two interventions: reusing the word table and adding a similarity-derived soft-target loss. Their augmented target comes from the model's own embedding geometry, not an independently pretrained teacher. The factorial PTB comparison at 650 units gives baseline/augmented-loss/reused-embedding/combined perplexities of 77.7/74.7/73.9/73.2. Their theoretical argument obtains a shared column space up to a basis transformation under restrictive conditions; literal equality of classifier and embedding transpose remains an imposed architectural choice. Both parameter count and some retraining protocols differ across arms. This is **background** evidence for distinguishing supervision from reuse, not a theorem that byte inputs and outputs should share vectors. [BIW-0036 card](../03_reading/R08/BIW-0036.md); [1611.01462v3, §§3–6 and Appendices A–B](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.01462v3); [BIP-0187–0189](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0187).

Translation introduces a different axis: encoder input, decoder input, and decoder output can share a joint subword vocabulary. Press and Wolf report substantial three-way table savings, but these do not imply shared encoder/decoder blocks. Nor does a common vocabulary require a common matrix. This distinction matters especially for byte encoder–decoders, where the alphabet is naturally common but the representational roles remain different.

ELMo's inspected scope is §§1–3.4/Table 1; later experiments and supplement are not fully audited.

This register defeats two tempting generalizations. Byte systems are not uniformly untied: MEGABYTE explicitly specifies local lexical tying. Conversely, a shared input alphabet or shared backbone does not establish lexical tying: LCA's common readout and Reformer's Q/K projection answer different questions. None of these architecture observations alone estimates the causal effect of changing the sharing switch. That estimate would need a controlled intervention within the same model, resource convention, and tuning protocol.

### 2.3 Sharing changes gradients as well as storage

For a shared table E, the ordinary chain rule gives an input-path plus output-path gradient. Those paths need not have balanced norms, equal curvature, or equal influence after optimizer preconditioning. The **background** study *Weight Tying Biases Token Embeddings Towards the Output Space* reports stronger alignment with untied output spaces and an early output-gradient norm share around 70% in its examined token-model runs. Its intervention multiplying input gradients by five changes alignment only modestly and worsens WikiText-2 perplexity from 35.71 to 36.64, while downstream tasks move in different directions. Some observational model pairs differ in data or scale, and conditions use single runs. The result motivates measuring path-specific dynamics; it does not show that untying improves byte modeling, or that balancing gradients must improve performance. [BIW-0037/W0366 card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B14/W0366.md); [2603.26663v1, §§3–6 and Appendix E Table 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26663v1); [BIP-0190–0191](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0190).

## 3. Small-vocabulary geometry: smaller tables do not settle the readout problem

### 3.1 Parameter savings become a different kind of argument

**Analyst accounting.** At common embedding/readout width d and vocabulary V, replacing two independent V-by-d tables by one saves Vd parameters, excluding biases and any bridging projection. With V=256 and d=2048, that is 524,288 parameters; it is not the tens or hundreds of millions saved by a large subword table. The arithmetic does not make tying irrelevant—regularization and gradient coupling remain—but it weakens an argument based only on lexical-table storage in a billion-parameter byte model.

ByT5 illustrates redistribution rather than a free reduction in total capacity. Its reported Small model has roughly 0.3% vocabulary-related parameters, versus 85% for mT5 Small; the released model families also allocate more layers to the encoder. Holding total size approximately constant therefore buys a very different amount of contextual machinery. Quality differences cannot be assigned solely to the alphabet or to sharing. [BIW-0024/W0351 card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B08/W0351.md); [TACL version, §§3 and 6, Tables 1 and 7–8](https://aclanthology.org/2022.tacl-1.17.pdf); [BIP-0037–0039](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0037).

Nor does character composition necessarily remove vocabulary cost. Kim et al.'s character-aware LM replaces independent word inputs with character CNNs, max pooling, and highway layers, but predicts a closed word vocabulary at word-time positions. Its large PTB model trains at approximately 1,500 word tokens/s versus 3,000 for the word-lookup model; precomputing word compositions can recover scoring speed at additional memory cost. This historical antecedent demonstrates that smaller input tables can coexist with substantial output and composition costs. [BIW-0055 card](../03_reading/R02/BIW-0055.md); [1508.06615v4, Model, Tables 3 and 7, Further Observations](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.06615v4); [BIP-0126–0127](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0126).

### 3.2 A head's formula matters more than its name

**Background geometry.** For class vector w_i and hidden state h, distinguish:

- ordinary dot product: z_i = w_iᵀh;
- row-normalized readout: z_i = w_iᵀh/||w_i||;
- fully normalized cosine with scale: z_i = s·w_iᵀh/(||w_i||||h||);
- distance-based readout, up to a class-independent term: z_i = w_iᵀh − ||w_i||²/2.

Row-normalized readout retains ||h|| as an example-dependent inverse temperature. Fully normalized cosine removes that channel unless a scale restores it. Class-dependent scales add a different freedom from a single shared temperature. Equal argmaxes under some transformations do not imply equal probabilities, gradients, calibration, or speculative-acceptance behavior.

*Normalization of Input-output Shared Embeddings in Text Generation Models* compares these distinctions in subword translation. Its method named “cosine” normalizes output rows, not h; other arms preserve raw input vectors while transforming output vectors. Consequently, shared base parameters need not mean identical effective input/output vectors. On WMT16 En→De, Transformer-big baseline 29.3 BLEU becomes 29.9 with normalized input/output vectors and 29.7 with the row-normalized readout; architecture and task are not byte LMs, and no byte-specific norm mechanism is isolated. [BIW-0091 card](../03_reading/R08/BIW-0091.md); [2001.07885v2, §2.2, Eq. 28, Tables 1–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.07885v2); [BIP-0194–0196](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0194).

A simple **analyst bound** explains why this distinction matters even for a small alphabet. If every unscaled logit lies in [−1,1], the largest possible single-class probability is at most e²/(e²+V−1). For V=256 this is approximately 2.8%. Learned scaling or an unnormalized hidden norm changes that bound. It is a property of the constrained logits, not a measured failure of any cited byte model. The inventory's nGPT extraction describes normalized vectors with trainable class scales; that source is PARTIAL background and provides no isolated byte-head result. [BIW-0092/BIP-0197](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0197).

### 3.3 Rank arguments also change when V is small

A large-vocabulary softmax bottleneck cannot simply be carried into a setting with hidden width at least the number of classes. For a full-row-rank V-by-d classifier with d≥V, arbitrary logits are algebraically attainable by some h; whether a trained causal network produces those h is another question. This observation neither proves good optimization nor makes tying harmless. It identifies why the dimension-based obstruction must be checked before invoking it. The inventory's mixture-of-softmax source is PARTIAL word-LM background, not evidence of a rank bottleneck in a 256-way byte head. [BIW-0093/BIP-0199](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0199).

Likewise, the selected pseudo-inverse-tying extraction requires a V-by-d matrix Z satisfying ZᵀZ=I_d. That exact construction requires V≥d. It cannot be imported unchanged into V=256, d>256. This is a dimensional feasibility check on the inspected equations, not a performance verdict on pseudo-inverse methods. [BIW-0038/BIP-0192, PARTIAL v2 §4, Eqs. 4–13](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0192).

### 3.4 The strongest byte-specific lesson is an optimization control

Shaham and Levy's *Neural Machine Translation without Embeddings* replaces learned byte tables with fixed one-hot coordinates and separate learned encoder-input, decoder-input, and output scales. It is a byte-input/byte-output **sequence-to-sequence** experiment: the encoder sees the full source, and the decoder is causal over targets. Common coordinates are not shared trainable matrices. Its d=512 coordinate softmax leaves special/unused output-coordinate handling insufficiently explicit for asserting a precisely 256-way normalized byte law.

With the initial recipe, removing embeddings improves 19 of 20 translation directions. But elementwise input dropout completely erases a one-hot symbol when its active coordinate is dropped, whereas it only removes some coordinates from a dense embedding. Giving the learned-embedding baselines explicit decoder token dropout changes the comparison: fixed bytes become nearly tied in en→xx and average 0.45 BLEU worse in xx→en. Thus an apparent representational gain substantially depended on how the training recipe corrupted teacher-forced history. It supports neither automatic untied superiority nor automatic embedding elimination. [BIW-0068 card](../03_reading/R01/BIW-0068.md); [NAACL 2021, §§3–6, Tables 2–4](https://aclanthology.org/2021.naacl-main.17.pdf); [BIP-0102](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0102), [BIP-0139](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0139).

## 4. Auxiliary supervision and useful internal representations

### 4.1 Better training signals need not imply a different generation law

Al-Rfou et al.'s 64-layer byte Transformer demonstrates how much optimization machinery can matter without shortening the sequence. It trains at every causal position, adds independent intermediate-layer classifiers, removes those layer losses on a schedule, and adds a weighted two-step-ahead prediction target. Only the immediate next-byte classifier remains at inference. Text8 development loss rises from 1.062 to 1.158 without intermediate losses and to 2.482 without all-position supervision under the reused recipe; removing the extra future target yields 1.068. These are informative ablations, not equally retuned proofs of universal necessity. Training parameters are 235M versus 219M at inference: discarded supervision is not cost-free training. [BIW-0064 card](../03_reading/R09/BIW-0064.md); [1808.04444v2, §§2–3, Table 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.04444v2); [BIP-0096–0098](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0096).

The independent future classifiers encourage predictive features; they do not define a coherent joint sampler for several future bytes. AU-Net supplies a different mechanism: shifted coarse representations must support the following regex-defined segment, inducing longer predictive horizons through the ordinary byte loss rather than an explicit auxiliary multi-token objective. Its stage widths, hand-specified splits, and expansion projections matter independently. Appendix C's simple versus multi-linear expansion changes HellaSwag from 60.6 to 66.0 in the reported three-stage setting. This is architecture-specific evidence about expansion, not proof that auxiliary supervision is unnecessary generally. Online prefix-stability of the published regex remains unverified. [BIW-0077 card](../03_reading/R10/BIW-0077.md); [2506.14761v1, §§2–3, Appendix C Tables 4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.14761v1); [BIP-0108](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0108).

The byte-simulation study makes the supervision question more explicit. Adding input n-gram lookup capacity improves byte loss, especially at smaller scale; a temporary four-byte grouping stage can leave a later benefit after returning to ordinary byte training. But that stage also removes three quarters of targets and changes context and grouping. A temporary next-subword head performs worse near the end of the 1.7B run, while its smaller-scale gap largely closes. These findings argue against treating all linguistic auxiliaries as interchangeable. They do not isolate “more text throughput” as the sole cause, nor establish that multi-byte prediction generally fails. [BIW-0108 card](../03_reading/R08/BIW-0108.md); [2604.27263v2, §§5.1–5.6, Figures 1–6 and Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.27263v2); [BIP-0115–0120](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0115).

### 4.2 A representation can exist without being useful to the task

There are several distinct problems often compressed into “latent collapse.” MANTa's denoising-only failure is an allocation failure: without its L/4 block bottleneck, it often learns approximately one block per byte. H-Net addresses hard-routing instability using smoothing, straight-through gradients, and a rate objective. Neither observation establishes a collapsed stochastic posterior. MANTa's membership approximation and capacity cap also differ from H-Net's selected contextual end states. [BIW-0006 card](../03_reading/R01/BIW-0006.md), [2212.07284v1, §§3 and 6.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.07284v1), [BIP-0154](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0154); [BIW-0011/W0011 card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B05/W0011.md), [2507.07955v2, §2 and §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07955v2), [BIP-0024–0025](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0024).

**Background** sentence VAEs pose another question: a stochastic posterior may approach the prior while an autoregressive decoder models text without using the latent. The selected PARTIAL inventory extracts describe KL annealing and word dropout, restricted decoder receptive fields, and extra inference-network updates. They identify candidate mechanisms and costs, not full-read byte evidence. A nonzero posterior KL is not itself improved likelihood, and deterministic residual bypass should not be diagnosed using that quantity. For deterministic byte systems, a more relevant test is whether changing or withholding the coarse state alters predictions under a matched intervention. [BIW-0094–0096/BIP-0202–0205](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0202).

Compressive Transformer offers a concrete consumer-facing alternative to reconstructing every hidden coordinate: train a compression function to preserve attention outputs, stopping gradients into the source hidden states and attention parameters for that auxiliary objective. Its enwik8 table favors convolution plus attention reconstruction over several alternatives, but rows separately select compression rates, and the backpropagation-through-time control changes unroll length and batch. This supports the distinction between reconstructing states and preserving their use by attention, not a universally optimal retention loss. More compressed memory is also nonmonotonic: 3,072 slots gives 0.97 while 4,096 gives 1.00 in the reported appendix. [BIW-0018/W0023 card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B24/W0023.md); [1911.05507v1, §3.2, Table 5 and Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/1911.05507v1); [BIP-0035–0036](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0035).

A complementary negative comes from subword models: *Models in a Spelling Bee* makes spellings highly decodable from initialized embeddings, yet masked-LM losses become separated by less than 0.01 after 1,000 updates in its training intervention. Probe recoverability and training utility are different outcomes. The result concerns that initialization and masked-subword objective, not a universal rejection of spelling-aware models. [BIW-0110 card](../03_reading/R10/BIW-0110.md); [NAACL 2022, §5/Figure 2](https://aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.373.pdf); [BIP-0230](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0230).

### 4.3 Gradient interventions are diagnostics, not a default cure

**Background** PCGrad projects conflicting task gradients; gradient-similarity weighting gates an auxiliary contribution according to local agreement with the main gradient. Their actual experiments include nonlanguage tasks, and their assumptions do not provide arbitrary-optimizer, finite-budget guarantees. Negative cosine alone does not establish negative transfer. The auxiliary-weighting paper even reports conditions where gating a useful teacher underperforms direct addition. Computing separate gradients and teachers has its own memory and training cost. These methods belong in the technique inventory as possible ways of investigating competing byte/rate/reconstruction objectives, not as already validated repairs for byte architectures. [BIW-0022/W0328 card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B16/W0328.md), [2001.06782v4, §2 and Appendix J](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.06782v4); [BIW-0023/W0331 card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B16/W0331.md), [1812.02224v2, §§3–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.02224v2); [BIP-0200–0201](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0200).

## 5. Architecture and compute scaling: where byte positions are paid for

### 5.1 Full resolution is a meaningful baseline, not a straw man

A deep byte Transformer can retain one state per byte and still learn useful long-range features. The 64-layer model in BIW-0064 uses a fixed 512-symbol causal window, two heads, width 512, and a 256-class next-byte head. Its intermediate classifiers and future-offset heads are training devices; the deployed model is still a dense full-resolution causal stack. On enwik8 it reports 1.06 bits/byte, versus 1.11 for its smaller 12-layer Transformer and 1.03 for a later 88M Transformer-XL configuration, with different training budgets. The historical value is a control: learned grouping is not a prerequisite for byte likelihood, and recurrent state is not the only way to use byte context. The practical cost is explicit: evaluation recomputes a 512-byte window for every predicted byte, and no cached serving throughput is reported. [BIW-0064](../03_reading/R09/BIW-0064.md); [BIP-0095 and BIP-0099](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0099).

Sparse attention changes a different factor. Sparse Transformer retains every byte position and next-byte output but replaces dense edges with fixed local/strided or block-summary patterns. In its enwik8 comparison, dense/fixed/strided configurations report 1.00/0.99/1.13 BPB and relative Time/Iter 1.31/0.55/0.35. The strided pattern is a useful negative: fewer interactions and a lower reported iteration quantity did not preserve language quality. The table does not state a time unit or provide a modern batched decode benchmark. Its asymptotic interaction count is not an end-to-end serving rate. [BIW-0118](../03_reading/R09/BIW-0118.md); [1904.10509v1, §§3–7, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.10509v1); [BIP-0236–0237](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0236).

Reformer combines three independent interventions: locality-sensitive hashing to approximate attention connectivity, reversible residual blocks to reconstruct depth-dependent training activations, and position-wise feed-forward chunking to lower peak intermediate memory. It keeps every sequence position. Shared Q/K projections serve the hashing mechanism; they do not tie a lexical input matrix to an output matrix. Reversibility reduces training activation storage by recomputation, but does not eliminate parameters, optimizer state, or inference KV. Feed-forward chunking partitions execution, not text. Enwik8 and synthetic copy results show that hash rounds affect quality and attention-evaluation cost; they do not establish matched-quality generation latency, exact nearest-neighbor recovery, or UTF-8 completion speed. [BIW-0139](../03_reading/FIELD_ADDENDUM/arxiv_2001.04451v2.md); [2001.04451v2, §§2–5 and Figure 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2001.04451v2); [BIP-0290–0292](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0290).

The common lesson is that full-resolution execution has at least three separable costs: the number of positions, the number of pairwise connections, and the amount of depth-dependent activation or cache state. A method may reduce one while retaining the others. Calling all three “sequence length” obscures the relevant mechanism.

### 5.2 Persistent hierarchy and transient within-byte work are different savings

ACT supplies a historical alternative to shortening: repeat a shared recurrent transition within each observed byte, then propagate weighted intermediate states. A learned halting signal trades task loss against a ponder penalty. Its byte-Wikipedia experiment reports similar training-set errors with and without ACT, not held-out improvement. Differentiation ignores halting-count discontinuities; punctuation-associated ponder is a qualitative trace, and transition count is not measured latency. Computation depth within a byte is thus separate from fewer global updates across bytes. [BIW-0138](../03_reading/FIELD_ADDENDUM/arxiv_1603.08983v6.md); [1603.08983v6, §§2.2 and 3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/1603.08983v6); [BIP-0287–0289](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0287).

MEGABYTE uses fixed P-byte patches. A larger causal global Transformer runs once over patches, while a smaller local Transformer autoregresses within each patch. If T is the byte length, the paper's full-attention cost is expressed as O(T²/P² + TP), with a favorable theoretical scaling when P grows with T. The local model remains serial within each patch, and the two causal shifts are essential: a global patch cannot see its current bytes, while local prediction sees only earlier bytes in its patch. The local input and local output matrix are explicitly the same paper-level embedding matrix. This is not a claim that all byte models should tie, and implementation aliasing was not verified.

The model's quality and speed comparisons show why budgets must be named. On five text datasets trained for 80B bytes, the PG19 byte BPB is 1.000 for MEGABYTE versus 1.057 for a byte Transformer and 1.104 for PerceiverAR, but parameter counts and contexts differ. For 8,192-byte generation, a 1.3B global plus 218M local model takes 93 seconds against 132 seconds for a 350M Transformer in the cited setting. That is roughly 29.5% less elapsed time, or a 1.42× rate; it should not be paraphrased as a 40% time reduction. The more expensive sliding/strided evaluation variants improve scoring numbers at additional passes and are not ordinary single-pass serving. Hardware, cache and batch conditions for the headline timing are not fully specified. [BIW-0008](../03_reading/R03/BIW-0008.md); [2305.07185v2, §§2–8, Tables 2, 6–8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.07185v2); [BIP-0013–0015, 0169–0170, 0284](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0013).

BLT takes variable entropy-selected patches rather than fixed patches, but the entropy selector is a separately trained causal byte model. The main model has a local encoder and decoder, a patch representation, and a global causal Transformer; the boundary policy is not free. In the reported construction, the entropy model has a 100M-parameter, 14-layer, 512-width, 512-byte-window configuration, trained on the same distribution but not jointly optimized with the main model. Its pretraining, per-byte inference, threshold tuning, and deployment scheduling costs are not fully placed into the main FLOP formula. BLT's equal-byte and approximate equal-FLOP comparisons therefore test a specified model-plus-policy convention, not a free learned segmentation operator. Its training and inference patch rates also need separate accounting. [BIW-0010](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B05/W0010.md); [BIP-0019–0022](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0021).

Dynamic Chunking and MANTa show two other ways of paying for hierarchy. Dynamic Chunking selects contextual states with a straight-through route and exponential moving average, while its multi-stage variant reuses selected higher-level computation. MANTa predicts soft frontier probabilities, approximates byte-to-block assignments, applies order-sensitive depthwise convolution and max pooling, then enforces an L/4 block cap. MANTa's cap is consequential: without it, denoising can produce approximately one block per byte, which defeats the intended computation saving. Its 16-V100 timing of 2.61 seconds/step is faster than a 57M byte T5 at 9.06 seconds/step but slower than a 60M subword T5 at 1.13 seconds/step. This is training update time, not decode latency; the cited variants also have different schedules and budgets. [BIW-0011](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B05/W0011.md), [BIW-0006](../03_reading/R01/BIW-0006.md); [BIP-0028, 0153–0154](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0154).

Scratchpad Patching makes the distinction especially clear. It temporarily recomputes a partial patch state when an auxiliary byte model's entropy threshold fires, broadcasts that state, and overwrites scratchpad entries rather than adding each one to the persistent trunk cache. The reported cache reduction and FLOP estimates therefore coexist with transient working memory and additional auxiliary-head computation. At fixed p=16, scratchpads raise the reported NLU average from 48.0 to 54.2, near the flat byte model's 54.1, but shrink the FLOP-saving factor relative to that model from 5.7× to 2.9×. Persistent trunk cache reduction can remain large while computation increases. H-Net can regress in the separate matched-training-FLOP comparison. [BIW-0076](../03_reading/R05/BIW-0076.md); [2605.09630v1, §§2–5 and Appendix E](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09630v1); [BIP-0105–0107](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0105).

### 5.3 Fixed state, memory recurrence, and latent arrays answer other questions

MambaByte advances one byte at a time through a fixed-size selective state-space recurrence. The current byte controls state update and readout; there is no persistent addressable raw-byte store. A state-space memory can make byte updates cheap, but cannot be interpreted as lossless source retention merely because it conditions on the prefix. Its reported byte BPB comparisons use approximately similar per-byte FLOPs but different training-byte totals and approximate formulas that omit some lookup and system costs. Its top-3 subword draft/byte verification speedup includes a resident drafter and does not establish exact target-law preservation. [BIW-0025](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B14/W0352.md); [BIP-0044–0045](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0044).

Transformer-XL reuses stopped-gradient past hidden states through a layerwise memory and relative-position attention. It stores sequences of hidden states rather than a learned grouping or an unbounded fixed vector. In the enwik8 discussion, a full-window recomputation baseline is reported as 363× slower at attention length 800 and 1,874× at 3,800. Those ratios compare reuse with an unusually expensive uncached evaluator, not modern batched serving with a matched KV implementation. The cache affects forward computation but is not trained through across segment boundaries. [BIW-0119](../03_reading/R09/BIW-0119.md); [P19-1285 and arXiv 1901.02860v3, §§3–4](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1285.pdf); [BIP-0238](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0238).

Perceiver IO maps either 2,048 bytes or 512 SentencePiece tokens into 256 learned latent vectors, processes the fixed latent array, and queries it with output vectors. The fixed workspace can reduce the dominant deep-processing sequence, but its input cross-attention and output queries remain costs. Byte Perceiver's 81.0 GLUE score at approximately 113B FLOPs is close to a token BERT's 81.1 at 109B, while its total parameters are larger; the comparison is an encoder masked-task result, not a causal language-generation result. Varying latent count and width changes both, so the bottleneck result is not an isolated latent-size effect. [BIW-0117](../03_reading/R01/BIW-0117.md); [2107.14795v3, §§3–4 and Appendix F](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.14795v3); [BIP-0121–0123](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0121).

## 6. Training bytes, text exposure, and scaling laws

### 6.1 “Equal tokens” is not a byte budget

Equal model positions can expose very different amounts of original text. ByT5 and mT5 use 1,024 input positions and one million steps, but an mT5 subword averages about 4.1 UTF-8 bytes on the cited mixture. ByT5 therefore sees roughly a quarter as much text under that position budget. Table 10's task timings range from competitive grapheme-to-phoneme performance to 6.4–9.5× slower XNLI across reported sizes. XNLI has long sentence-pair inputs and short label outputs, so its ratio cannot be assigned to long-output decoding. Task, source length, output length, and encoder–decoder allocation all matter. [BIW-0024/W0351](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B08/W0351.md); [TACL 2022, §§3–6 and Tables 1, 9–10](https://aclanthology.org/2022.tacl-1.17.pdf); [BIP-0040–0041](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0040).

Conversely, equal original bytes do not imply equal model positions or compute. MEGABYTE trains its main comparisons on 80B bytes but uses different patch sizes, contexts, and total parameter counts. AU-Net reports byte and BPE arms at approximately token-equivalent data and estimated FLOPs, but its multi-stage variants vary parameter count and stage execution. Scratchpad Patching holds a large raw-byte budget and model scale approximately constant while comparing FLOP-controlled curves; the trigger and transient work are part of the system. Each is a valid answer to a different question.

The most direct compute-optimal byte study, *Compute Optimal Tokenization*, sweeps compression, model size, and text exposure under total training FLOPs. It reports 988 latent and 320 subword models from 50M to 6.7B parameters, 4B to 1.1T training bytes, and 5e18 to 2e21 FLOPs. At 1e20 FLOPs, its fitted English optimum is about 3.7 bytes per global patch and about 60 training bytes per global parameter under the study's convention. Those are conditional fitted associations: the model-parameter variable excludes some components in the first scaling law, while total-compute comparisons include local modules and embeddings. The entropy model's pretraining and scoring lifecycle is not itemized as a full system cost. [BIW-0047](../03_reading/R05/BIW-0047.md); [2605.01188v2, §§2–3 and Appendices A–F](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.01188v2); [BIP-0239–0241](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0239).

Loss is nonmonotonic in compression at a fixed training budget; the fitted optimum shifts from about 3.69 bytes/patch at 1e20 FLOPs to 3.33 at 2e21. At approximately 2.1e9 inference FLOPs/byte, a 3.3B model with average patch length 4 scores HellaSwag 74.1%, versus 68.2% for a 6.7B model with length 8. The latter is competitive on ARC-Easy, while length 4 does better on ARC-Challenge. These operation-based comparisons separate task-specific deployment choice from training-optimal compression; they are not measured latency frontiers.

BIW-0028/W0356 supplies a sharp reversal on a 3:1 Gated DeltaNet/Gated Softmax Attention hybrid, not an all-full-attention backbone. Bytes favor the fitted same-parameter frontier, while subwords favor the tested training-FLOP frontier. The byte recipe also changes convolution width, adds hashed n-grams, and begins with four-byte bag prediction. Its fitted parameter count includes output but excludes input/hash tables; its operation budget excludes lookup/hash, averaging, loss, and optimizer work. The result establishes a conditional frontier, not a resident-budget or wall-time ranking. [W0356 card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B14/W0356.md); [2610.05978v1, §§2–4 and Appendices A–C](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.05978v1); [BIP-0050](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0050).

### 6.2 Data reuse and preprocessing are part of the experiment

Packing can change realized exposure even with identical corpus sampling. The multilingual tokenizer study BIW-0050 reports discarding 30.4–43.5% of sampled text, with tokenizer- and language-dependent retention. Its repeated versus single-pass conditions can change language-specific rankings: the lowest reported rank correlation is −.11 for Chinese. Thus fixed tokens and a common source mixture need not equalize retained bytes or repetition. This study mainly compares static tokenizer choices, not a matched-byte architecture intervention. [BIW-0050/W0516](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B22/W0516.md); [2610.12144v1, Appendix A.4 and Appendices B/D](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12144v1); [BIP-0075–0076](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0075).

TokSuite's common-text control illustrates the limit. It reconstructs the same text stream for four causal Llama-style tokenizer arms, but token counts and steps differ substantially—about 104.86B for Llama, 114.89B for Qwen, 100.23B for Gemma, and 169.80B for Comma in the reported table. It is an exposure control, not an equal-step or equal-FLOP control, and it excludes the byte arm. Its Figure 6 BPB transformation remains open: the paper's printed CE/log2|V| wording is not a valid general conversion to bits per original byte, while the actual run-to-figure transformation was not verified. The option-ranking and four-tokenizer controls should not be discarded, but Figure 6 cannot certify equal byte-normalized training sufficiency. [BIW-0051/W0518](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B22/W0518.md); [BIP-0077–0078, 0285–0286](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0286).

A valid byte-normalized score still requires the same scored events and matching original-byte denominator. For a token-path loss, the safe expression is

`bits per original byte = sum_i[-ln q(e_i | c_i)] / (B ln 2)`.

The effective event count, EOS and prompt conventions, padding/masking, normalization, overlap, and denominator B must be specified. This can report a transparent canonical token-path score; it does not turn a token-path likelihood into a marginal over all tokenizations, nor a marked byte-plus-EOT path into an unmarked byte-prefix marginal. TokSuite's open Figure 6 computation and the teacher-to-byte conversion in BIW-0103 are different examples of this general accounting problem.

## 7. Teacher, distillation, and auxiliary lifecycle costs

### 7.1 A byte deployment can be teacher-dependent

CANINE-S makes the training/deployment distinction explicit: its code-point encoder uses masked subword identities during pretraining, then discards that head. CANINE-C instead predicts bucketed codepoint targets; both use next-sentence prediction. Input vocabulary independence is therefore different from independence of lexical supervision. Hash tables, optional n-grams, and a discarded classifier still affect training capacity and cost. [BIW-0004](../03_reading/R01/BIW-0004.md); [2103.06874v4, §3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.06874v4); [BIP-0005](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0005).

Bolmo retains an original subword suffix lookup and adds local byte modules and a fused byte/boundary head. Stage 1 freezes the global model, uses oracle token boundaries, and aligns representations after four frozen global layers; stage 2 unfreezes all weights and removes distillation. Its conversion exposure is approximately 43.1B plus 172.9B bytes, additional to inherited pretraining. Omitting stage 1 is not catastrophic: extending the stage-2-only run by 6.5B tokens approximately matches global FLOPs and narrows the gap, especially at larger scale. This supports a curriculum trade-off, not a universal requirement for alignment. Frozen weights still require backward-to-input computation through the aligned layers; trainable parameter count is not the training-work budget. [BIW-0097](../03_reading/R06/BIW-0097.md); [2512.15586v2, §§3.2 and 6.2, Tables 7–8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.15586v2); [BIP-0206–0209](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0206).

*Breaking the Token Ceiling* distills Llama-3-8B into causal token, byte, and byte-plus-EOT students. Its approximate byte mapping filters teacher token alternatives at a within-token prefix and drops termination mass; the marked mapping retains EOT. Continuing terminated alternatives under an unmarked prefix would require further teacher conditioning. The marked-history conversion is not thereby an exact unmarked-prefix marginal. Saved logits also cost storage: one accounting retains top-600 token logits and indices versus about 260 logits per byte, without a complete teacher inference/pretraining/I/O budget.

At the approximately 1.8e21-FLOP reference, all four byte arms have lower validation BPB yet worse downstream performance than token-supervised training. The celebrated four-percentage-point asymptotic advantage is an extrapolation sensitive to validation set and learning rate, not the observed maximum-checkpoint gap of 44.6 versus 44.4. Markers add about 30.94% FLOPs per source token in its accounting. The experiment separates smaller resident lexical tables from cheaper learning, better downstream quality, and cheaper inference. [BIW-0103](../03_reading/R06/BIW-0103.md); [2609.12303v1, §§3–6 and Appendices A, D–G](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.12303v1); [BIP-0113–0114](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0113).

### 7.2 Where policy costs occur

BLT's independent entropy model is needed for deployment patch decisions; amortized preprocessing at training does not remove that online work. In Bolmo's alternative compression-policy experiments, a 370M auxiliary LM trained on 74.3B tokens supplies entropy/cross-entropy merges during training, not inference. Scratchpad Patching jointly trains an auxiliary two-layer Transformer and byte head, stopping its gradient at encoder output, and runs entropy-based triggers online. In its H-Net variant auxiliary layers are shared with router computation; lexical input/output and auxiliary/decoder readout tying remain unspecified. MANTa needs no external teacher but does pay for frontier estimation, membership approximation, and order-sensitive aggregation. These are distinct lifecycle placements, not a binary cheap/expensive split. [BIW-0010](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B05/W0010.md), [BIW-0097, §5.1](../03_reading/R06/BIW-0097.md), [BIW-0076, Appendix B.3](../03_reading/R05/BIW-0076.md), [BIW-0006](../03_reading/R01/BIW-0006.md).

## 8. Serving latency, cache, and decoding

### 8.1 Acceptance and head expressivity can point in the wrong direction

LCA's B200 batch-1 threshold policy accepts a left-to-right prefix of parallel future-byte candidates. Lowering its threshold from .9 to .7 raises acceptance from 50.1% to 56.7% and throughput by about 10%, while lowering mean task score by about three points. With threshold .75, increasing candidate count from three to seven reduces DailySum throughput from 210.42 to 164.18 bytes/s. Separate external-verifier experiments report 2.1–2.3× speedups at seven candidates, but explicitly drop KV caching and require a resident same-size verifier. Its shared unembedding saves a particular readout duplication; it does not make a second decoder or verifier free. [BIW-0015](../03_reading/R05/BIW-0015.md); [2608.15454v2, Figures 4 and 8, Appendix B Tables 3–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.15454v2); [BIP-0033–0034](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0033).

MTPC supplies a normalized dependent draft through a probabilistic circuit, rather than independent offset heads. On EvaByte with an eight-byte window on L40S, its sampling table reports factorized/CP-rank32/CP-rank128 acceptance of 5.19/5.88/6.02 but throughput of 176.3/191.0/182.3 bytes/s. At sixteen bytes, HMM accepts more than a binary tree yet runs slower: 6.93 versus 6.76 accepted, 168.0 versus 199.2 bytes/s. Greedy decoding can instead favor the factorized head. Its sixteen-byte rank-32 tree projection has about 730M parameters versus 21M for the factorized projection. A small byte alphabet does not guarantee a small structured multi-byte head. These are batch-1, prefill-excluded measurements; EOS is suppressed, the memory setup includes cache-on BF16 evaluation, and inherited byteification costs are additional. [BIW-0079](../03_reading/R05/BIW-0079.md); [2511.11346v2, §4, Appendix C Table 2 and Appendix K Tables 8/11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.11346v2); [BIP-0171–0173](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0173).

The circuit's normalization and ordinary Algorithm 3 residual correction do not certify the optimized shared-state Algorithm 2. The latter's description of discarding a verifier token does not clearly distinguish an all-accepted bonus from a post-rejection correction or retain correction state. A literal discard-and-ordinary-restart interpretation need not preserve the stochastic target law. This is an unresolved source-level guarantee, not a measured software defect, and does not invalidate its timing observations. [Latest claim rule, §3](../05_review/CLAIM_RULES.md#3-mtpc-exact-source-level-qualification-not-a-software-verdict).

### 8.2 Source compression does not remove serial output

MrT5's encoder deletion reduces a selected single-sequence timing from 44.29 to 29.99 ms at 50% target deletion. On TyDiQA GoldP, EM/F1 changes from 69.90/79.58 to 68.27/77.73 with a reported 33.31% runtime reduction; spelling and word-finding scores also fall. Its bidirectional source encoder sees a supplied answer-bearing paragraph, and its theoretical cost separates encoder, decoder, and cross-attention. Long autoregressive outputs dilute encoder-only savings. [BIW-0046/W0511](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B22/W0511.md); [2410.20771v3, §§5–6 and Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.20771v3); [BIP-0068–0069](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0069).

MEGABYTE amortizes expensive global updates but still autoregresses locally. MambaByte's measured 2.6× speculative speedup uses an extra 110M subword drafter on A100, three-token proposals, and top-3 verification; the acceptance rule does not guarantee unchanged greedy output or sampling law. These mechanisms save different work. Neither training bytes/s, an oracle accepted-position simulation, nor the number of trunk states determines user-visible generation latency. [BIW-0025](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B14/W0352.md); [2401.13660v3, Appendix F and HTML Table 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.13660v3); [BIP-0045](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0045).

### 8.3 State persistence and evaluation permissions

Fixed-window recomputation, Transformer-XL stopped-gradient hidden-state reuse, a state-space recurrence, and scratchpad refresh are not interchangeable caches. Test-time weight-updating dynamic evaluation is different again: it changes the learner rather than only the retained forward state. The Al-Rfou comparison includes a dynamically evaluated mLSTM but does not dynamically update its own Transformer. Thus model quality and evaluation permissions must be paired. [BIW-0064, Tables 1 and 3](../03_reading/R09/BIW-0064.md), [BIW-0119](../03_reading/R09/BIW-0119.md).

Hardware execution can reverse operation-based expectations. H-Net reports that its dynamic implementation can train about twice as slowly as an isotropic baseline despite favorable theoretical FLOPs, with variable-length batching and out-of-memory risks. Bolmo 7B's batch-1 H100 comparison with source Olmo 3 7B reports roughly 125 versus 150 bytes/s at the same compression, and approximately 1 versus .8 seconds for 72K-byte prefill. Bolmo overtakes near 6.6 bytes/patch, with quality trade-offs. These are useful operating points, not batch-serving universals. [BIW-0011, §4](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B05/W0011.md), [BIW-0097, Figure 7/§6.3](../03_reading/R06/BIW-0097.md).

## 9. A comparison contract, not a single leaderboard

A compact result should report six connected objects:

| Object | Minimum information |
|---|---|
| Sharing and geometry | Named matrix pair, dimensions, bias, normalization and scale; paper/config/code/runtime evidence level |
| Four interfaces | Input, internal, output, supervision; source/target visibility and any external boundary information |
| Training exposure | Retained and unique/repeated original text, contexts, positions, scored events and sampling mixture |
| Training work | Total/trunk/training-only parameters, operation convention, teacher/preprocessing, optimizer/activation memory and measured time |
| Serving | Prefill/output bytes, hardware/precision/batch, persistent cache, workspace and resident draft/verifier; measured latency rather than proxy |
| Quality and probability | Task/population, matched quality where applicable, byte-chain/path/marginal/denoising event, EOS and original-byte denominator |

This separates the question “does this representation learn?” from “does this system save resources at the required quality?” A mechanism-isolating ablation and a deployment-frontier comparison are complementary, not substitutes. The [sharing register](../02_inventory/TAXONOMY.md#version--and-axis-specific-sharing-register-current) and [budget taxonomy](../02_inventory/TAXONOMY.md#C12) preserve those distinctions.

## 10. Open problems and conclusions

The selected evidence does not choose a universal byte architecture. A tiny alphabet reduces table pressure while moving work into positions, contextual layers, local composition, and output decisions. Sharing may regularize or couple incompatible update paths; the documented byte systems contain both tied and untied specifications, not a controlled finding of automatic superiority either way. Better auxiliary loss or recoverable spelling does not alone establish useful latent dependence. Sparse edges, hierarchy, fixed state, and reversible training each remove different costs.

The clearest remaining needs are matched small-alphabet sharing/readout experiments; ablations connecting latent intervention to downstream utility; whole-lifecycle teacher accounting; and measured matched-quality batched serving. First-complete-scalar latency, grapheme-preserving generation, non-ASCII numeracy, and long natural multilingual exact copy remain thin evidence rather than established failures. Runtime aliases, the MTPC optimized-wrapper guarantee, and TokSuite's actual Figure 6 transformation likewise remain unverified. These limits identify concrete work without converting a promising mechanism into an unsupported universal claim.
