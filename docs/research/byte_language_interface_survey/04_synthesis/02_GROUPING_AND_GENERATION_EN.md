# Learning groups and generating text: compression signals, causal availability, and output dependence

## 1. The central distinction: shortening computation is not shortening the probability chain

Byte-level modeling separates a compact alphabet from a potentially expensive sequence. A UTF-8 byte model can use a small categorical output while taking several prediction steps to complete a non-ASCII scalar, and many more to complete a word. Grouping attempts to move substantial computation away from individual bytes. It does not, by itself, specify what a group contains, when that content becomes available, or how the next group is generated. Those are three additional design decisions.

The literature examined here supports a useful organizing argument: **compression policy, information aggregation, and output factorization are separable mechanisms whose interactions determine both quality and practical speed**. Charformer learns mixtures of candidate blocks but retains a fixed final reduction rate. Dynamic Token Pooling (DTP) explicitly averages spans. H-Net selects already-contextualized boundary states. MEGABYTE uses fixed patches with an autoregressive local decoder. Scratchpad Patching refreshes a partial patch without increasing its persistent patch count. Multi-byte prediction may instead leave the input computation essentially unchanged and alter the output proposal. Calling these all “learned tokenization” obscures their most important differences.

The unit distinctions matter historically as well as technically. DTP's reported language experiments use cleaned **character** streams, not general UTF-8. CANINE is a **Unicode code-point encoder**, not a raw-byte generator. Subword-Delimited Downsampling (SDD), despite its character terminology, explicitly uses **UTF-8 bytes** in its translation experiments. GPT-2's byte-based BPE operates and predicts at **merged-subword** positions; its 256-byte base does not make it next-byte modeling. The designated narrow GPT-2 check supports that interface history only, not a matched raw-byte performance frontier [BIW-0141, BIP-0295/0296; report p.4 §§2.2–2.3; PARTIAL by design](../03_reading/FIELD_ADDENDUM/gpt2_official_report_2019.md).

Four interfaces should therefore be read separately:

| Family and carrying example | Input | Expensive/internal positions | Output | Supervision and visibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Code-point encoder: CANINE | Unicode code points | Stride-reduced hidden states, restored task positions | Task labels/span endpoints; pretraining bucket or subword labels | Masked/full-source encoding, not online text generation |
| Byte encoder–decoder: Charformer/MANTa | Corrupted UTF-8 bytes and sentinels | Soft-composed shortened encoder states | Autoregressive bytes/sentinels | Span reconstruction with complete corrupted-source access |
| Character causal hierarchy: DTP | Restricted corpus characters | Pooled variable-length segments | Next character | Causal character LM plus optional boundary supervision/surrogates |
| Byte causal hierarchy: MEGABYTE/BLT/H-Net | Bytes | Fixed patches, attention-pooled patches, or selected contextual states | Next byte | Causal byte likelihood; boundary signals differ |
| Parallel byte proposal: LCA/MtPC | Byte-model prefix | Segment-conditioned head or circuit over future positions | Multiple candidate bytes | Auxiliary/composite prediction plus a separately specified emission policy |
| Nonbyte output comparator: Block Diffusion | Tokenizer tokens | Clean past blocks and noisy current block | Tokenizer tokens | Block denoising bound, not ordinary byte-chain cross-entropy |

These descriptions are grounded in the linked method cards below. They are not a common-budget leaderboard. A shorter internal sequence can coexist with serial byte emission; a parallel training loss can coexist with serial sampling; a larger accepted draft can coexist with slower generation.

## 3. Where does compression learning come from?

### 3.1 Rules and external segmentation are substantive baselines

A fixed stride offers predictable tensor shapes, simple scheduling, and a known compute rate. It also places an identical string at different offsets and can divide useful local structures. MEGABYTE addresses this with contextual patch embedding and a local decoder rather than learning boundaries. SpaceByte uses a more text-sensitive but still nonlearned policy: after consuming a byte that is neither an ASCII letter/digit nor a UTF-8 continuation byte, it can trigger global processing, with additional conditions for consecutive spacelike bytes and BOS. Its selected vectors already contain local causal context; the model does not mean-pool completed words. For a multibyte character, a trigger can occur after the **first byte**, not after a complete character [BIW-0009 / BIP-0016; v3 §2/Appendix C, carrying card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B05/W0009.md).

This rule is a strong control, not an obsolete straw model. At the study's independently optimized architectures under a training budget of 10¹⁹ FLOPs, SpaceByte/SentencePiece/fixed-interval variants score 1.009/0.989/1.112 BPB on PG-19, but 0.748/0.768/0.804 on arXiv. The ranking changes by domain; these are selected frontier points, not equal-parameter systems differing only in a boundary bit. The reported preliminary Chinese weakness further limits a whitespace-oriented generalization (v3 §§4–7, Table 1). DTP likewise finds whitespace marginally best on English text8 but not Finnish, where unigram-guided pooling is better at a different realized shortening rate [BIW-0007 / BIP-0008; Table 1, card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B05/W0007.md).

External subword segmentation is another legitimate design, provided it is named. SDD uses SentencePiece boundaries to pool source bytes and trains a target local LSTM to emit bytes until an end-of-group marker. Its fixed, buffered-fixed, word-delimited, and subword-delimited comparisons probe position alignment and block length as well as lexical grouping. Six-direction average BLEU rises from 16.56 for fixed groups to 19.46 for buffered-fixed and 19.98 for SDD, but English→Turkish and biomedical translation provide counterexamples to blanket superiority. At WMT14 scale, the two-step decoder itself limits quality: standard subword scores 28.62 BLEU, subword plus local LSTM 27.49, and SDD 27.27 [BIW-0075 / BIP-0168; §§3–8, Tables 3–6, Appendix C Table 15, card](../03_reading/R03/BIW-0075.md). The lesson is not that linguistic grouping is useless, but that boundary quality cannot compensate automatically for a weaker reconstruction interface.

### 3.2 Soft composition is not a posterior over hard partitions

Charformer's Gradient-Based Subword Tokenization (GBST) enumerates candidate widths, mean-pools blocks, scores them, repeats each score/representation over its contributing positions, and mixes widths with a softmax. A final fixed-stride mean sets the actual reduction. A position can prefer a different width from its neighbor; the result need not describe a coherent hard partition. A width-5 convolution introduces order-sensitive local context before otherwise order-insensitive means. Optional global score calibration is appropriate to an encoder but is not prefix-local; it was disabled in multilingual experiments [BIW-0005, v3 §§2.1.1–2.1.5; card](../03_reading/R01/BIW-0005.md).

MANTa makes the boundary variable more explicit. A local Transformer predicts frontier probabilities; cumulative Bernoulli counts define a Poisson-binomial block-index distribution, approximated by a truncated Gaussian. Weighted embeddings pass through depthwise convolution and blockwise max pooling before projection. This is a differentiable approximation to membership, not an exact likelihood sum over discrete segmentations. Its output capacity is capped at at most one quarter of input length. Without that bottleneck the authors report degeneration toward one block per byte; denoising alone does not reliably discover an economical grouping. Extra block representations can be truncated, so a short sequence is not evidence of lossless content retention [BIW-0006 / BIP-0007,0154; v1 §§3.1,6.1/Appendix D, card](../03_reading/R01/BIW-0006.md).

Both methods learn byte composition from span reconstruction with access to the entire corrupted source. Their autoregressive target decoders do not turn their **encoder grouping** into an online causal mechanism. MANTa's 2.61-second training step versus 9.06 for its byte-T5 comparator and 1.13 for subword T5—16 V100s, batch 1,024, 1,024-byte versus 256-subword inputs—shows useful encoder savings and a remaining subword gap, not accelerated byte generation (Table 5).

### 3.3 Hard boundaries: surrogate gradients, rate control, and sampled policies

HM-RNN supplies an early example of boundaries as computation clocks. A lower-level boundary and previous same-level boundary select UPDATE, COPY, or FLUSH; COPY preserves hidden/cell states exactly, while FLUSH transmits/resets information with top-down context. A hard-sigmoid straight-through estimator supplies a biased derivative through the binary decision. Dataset-specific slope annealing helps some settings but was not used identically across PTB, text8, and enwik8. Boundary pictures near spaces do not establish recovered words: a later segmental study finds very low HM-LSTM word F1 in a different space-stripped task [BIW-0016 / BIP-0145/0146; v7 §3/Table 1; card](../03_reading/R03/BIW-0016.md).

DTP brings related questions into a local–global–local Transformer. Its jointly learned arm combines hard Gumbel-sigmoid choices with a differentiable assignment surrogate and a binomial boundary-count prior. The unigram-supervised arm instead imitates offline segmentation; the entropy arm uses local peaks of the **main model's** conditional next-character entropy, not a separately trained teacher. This distinction matters: an external segmenter, the current model's distribution, and task-loss gradients impose different targets and costs. DTP's Gumbel arm is reported less stable, while simple whitespace remains competitive in English [BIW-0007, §§3–5, carrying card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B05/W0007.md).

H-Net uses thresholded cosine change between projected adjacent contextual states, selecting states for a slower backbone. EMA-based dechunking smooths high-level output; straight-through treatment lets boundary confidence receive language-loss gradients. Removing smoothing destabilizes compression rates. Its ratio objective couples realized hard selection fraction with mean boundary confidence, but is not a strict per-example quota. The two can disagree; auxiliary balance is an optimization device, not proof of exact rate or semantic segmentation [BIW-0011 / BIP-0023–0026; v2 §2 Eqs.1–10/§3.3 Figure 7, card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B05/W0011.md).

The reinforcement-learning alternative samples Bernoulli actions and optimizes their score-function gradient rather than differentiating through selection. Its formal objective is the expected conditional log-likelihood over boundary paths, a Jensen lower bound on a boundary-marginalized likelihood. Practical variance control is decisive: an early-exit byte classifier supplies a tokenization-independent difficulty baseline, future rewards are discounted, and batch-relative advantages are detached. Discounting deliberately introduces bias; the practical estimator should not inherit an unqualified “unbiased REINFORCE” label. At approximately matched rates around 0.2, the 147M FineWeb experiment reports 1.280 BPB for its w=1 policy versus 1.299 BPE-guided and 1.386 H-Net; uniform routing still wins the ARC-Easy **gold-continuation BPB** cell. These are likelihood measurements, not answer accuracy or serving speed [BIW-0078 / BIP-0164/0165; v4 §§2.4–2.7/Table 1/Appendix A,D, card](../03_reading/R09/BIW-0078.md).

### 3.4 Entropy, confidence, and utility answer different questions

BLT's patcher is a separate causal byte LM, typically 100M parameters with a 512-byte window. Its default rule thresholds predictive entropy; an alternative thresholds an entropy difference. The patcher is trained separately rather than through the main model's task loss. Entropy estimates uncertainty over all next-byte possibilities before the actual byte is known. SOMBRERO instead regresses boundary probability toward `1 − stop_gradient P(observed next byte | prefix)`: a target-conditioned error signal available during training, while the deployed router remains prefix-only. These are neither identical statistics nor identical optimization procedures [BIW-0010, conference §§2,4/Appendix O, card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B05/W0010.md); [BIW-0012 / BIP-0156; v1 §3, card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B24/W0012.md).

SOMBRERO's controlled ladder is especially informative. At roughly 5× target compression, 1.95 GFLOPs/byte and 839B training bytes, H-Net's 0.6701 BPB becomes 0.6571 with byte-level smoothing; sigmoid routing gives 0.6578, and adding confidence supervision gives 0.6568. Most improvement in that ladder is not attributable to the new target alone. At larger scale, stronger surprise enrichment coexists with essentially unchanged BPB and a lower downstream average. Steering a diagnostic is not necessarily improving the task (Table 1/Appendix D).

Utility-derived patching asks a different question: how much does fresh global computation reduce relevant loss? The BLT-1B study fits a past-two-byte lookup from differences between **whole boundary layouts**, not clean single-boundary interventions. A smaller-model variant uses single-added-boundary scoring. Both may inspect future training labels to construct targets without giving the deployed lookup future access. At roughly 10% rate, entropy-plus-dependence raises forced-true-prefix computed-value accuracy from 19.0% to 67.1%; dependence alone scores 67.8% there but collapses final-answer accuracy to 4.8% versus entropy's 51.4%. Free GSM8K solving remains around 2% or below [BIW-0029 / BIP-0054; v1 §§3–10, card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B05/W0357.md).

The related novelty study predicts future repetition from past-byte contexts. It improves selected forced-prefix math targets but worsens overall BPB and transfers poorly to code; switching inference boundaries can destroy a model trained under the original rule. These two papers share author, pipeline, and evaluation resources and are not independent replications [BIW-0030 / BIP-0162/0163; v1 §§3–6/Table 5, card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B14/W0358.md). Together they establish conditional separation between uncertainty and computational value, not a universally superior patcher.

## 4. What is actually compressed?

A boundary identifies a location; it does not identify the information operator. DTP averages contextual states. MEGABYTE concatenates fixed-position byte embeddings, preserving their ordered coordinates at the patch input. BLT uses byte states as keys/values and a pooled patch query for cross-attention; hashed byte n-grams add local memorization. H-Net and SpaceByte select contextual endpoint states. A selected state can already encode earlier bytes because its encoder is causal and contextual; conversely, membership in a mean or attention pool does not imply recoverability of each member.

The DTP/H-Net comparison shows why aggregation should be tested rather than inferred. DTP's mean pooling beats endpoint sampling in its character experiments. H-Net's Appendix E.1 finds direct selection roughly comparable with mean, max, and cross-attention aggregation under its learned boundaries. Different encoders, tasks, and gradient paths make these compatible findings, not a contradiction or a universal verdict on pooling [BIP-0010](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0010), [BIP-0026](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0026).

The distinction extends beyond patch models. CANINE contextualizes code points locally, downsamples with stride-4 convolution, and restores task positions with repeated states, fine representations, convolution, and attention. It can consume vocabulary-independent inputs while receiving subword supervision during pretraining. This is a bidirectional encoder and does not establish causal byte output [BIW-0004, v4 §§3.1–3.2; card](../03_reading/R01/BIW-0004.md). ELMo is an earlier character-composed **word-position/word-output** mechanism: a CNN/highway network composes each word and directional LSTMs contextualize word positions. Its designated partial reading establishes this architecture, not exact byte serialization or a native byte LM [BIW-0140 / BIP-0293/0294; v2 §§1–3.4/Table 1; PARTIAL by design](../03_reading/FIELD_ADDENDUM/arxiv_1802.05365v2.md).

Continuous patch states also differ from emitted discrete codes. The segmental neural LM sums over character-segment paths while mixing a learned lexicon with an autoregressive spelling LSTM. Its semi-Markov assumption makes history depend on previous characters rather than latent segmentation, enabling dynamic programming. An expected powered-length penalty and lexical memory provide explicit inductive bias. Whole-sentence MAP segmentation uses the observed sentence, whereas the generative segment conditional uses the preceding character history. Good marginal likelihood and word-boundary F1 are separate results; its reported segmentation evaluation includes the union of splits, and unseen character types are filtered [BIW-0042 / BIP-0149; official §§2–4,7/Appendix F, card](../03_reading/R03/BIW-0042.md).

Nor is every adaptive method compression. ACT repeats recurrent transitions **within one byte**, penalizing ponder cost without shortening the byte sequence; its Wikipedia errors are training-set-only and broadly similar to the non-ACT baseline [BIW-0138 / BIP-0287–0289; v6 §2/§3.5, card](../03_reading/FIELD_ADDENDUM/arxiv_1603.08983v6.md). Reformer retains positions while changing attention connectivity, reconstructing activations through reversible residual blocks, and chunking position-wise feed-forward execution. Such execution chunks are not learned text segments [BIW-0139 / BIP-0290–0292; v2 §§2–3, card](../03_reading/FIELD_ADDENDUM/arxiv_2001.04451v2.md). These alternatives prevent the field's compute problem from being reduced to boundary discovery alone.

## 5. Causal availability is an information-path property

### 5.1 Completed groups and fine-resolution paths

For next-byte prediction at position t, every feature used must be computable from the permitted observed prefix. Attention masks are only one part of that requirement. Convolution, pooling, score calibration, boundary annotations, residuals, and expansion can each alter visibility.

MEGABYTE uses two distinct shifts: the global patch input moves back one complete patch, while local inputs move back one byte. The current patch's global conditioning therefore comes from preceding patches, and its local autoregression sees previously emitted bytes of the current patch. DTP similarly makes a completed segment available only when completed; earlier positions use the preceding segment result and their fine-resolution residual [BIW-0008, v2 §2.2 Eqs.2–6; card](../03_reading/R03/BIW-0008.md); [BIW-0007, §§3.1–3.3; card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B05/W0007.md).

Hourglass requires an additional k−1 shift before shortening beyond the ordinary one-step input shift. For attention pooling, all source operands—including keys and values—must obey that alignment. Its fine-resolution path is important for expressivity, but the precise restriction matters: **without fine-resolution Transformer layers, the shortened branch lacks current-group history, while the shifted-input residual can still carry the immediately previous symbol**. Appendix A.1 demonstrates a two-position-back copying restriction; it does not establish blanket within-group independence. A fine-resolution attention layer removes that restriction in the construction, not a guarantee of learned arbitrary-task success [BIW-0072 / BIP-0103/0104; v2 §2.2/Appendix A.1; corrected interpretation](../05_review/CLAIM_RULES.md#6-hourglass-restrict-the-independence-claim-to-the-information-path), [card](../03_reading/R03/BIW-0072.md).

The causal-GBST study provides an empirical failure rather than merely a missing proof. Convolution and overlapping candidate n-grams expose future symbols in its tested decoder construction. On independent 100-symbol random sequences, some early positions approach perfect accuracy despite 1% chance. Replacing convolution alone leaves smaller leaks; removing or masking crossing n-grams repairs the tested paths. Yet a larger-padding alternative, also leak-free, nearly fails translation entirely, and repaired GBST remains below the strongest byte/convolutional baselines. Removing future access can change the effective prediction problem enough to impair optimization [BIW-0074 / BIP-0166/0167; v1 §§2–3/Tables 1–3, card](../03_reading/R03/BIW-0074.md). Its diagnostic uses synthetic symbols; its translation experiments use UTF-8 bytes.

### 5.2 Offline labels versus inference-time side information

A boundary target computed using future **training** text is not automatically leakage. A prefix-only policy can learn from offline subword ends, observed next-byte confidence, or future loss differences, just as a language model learns from future labels. The deployment question is whether the actual action uses only available information.

By contrast, adding offline boundaries to a held-out full string can expose information unavailable online. Byte-simulation experiments supply BPE starts/ends and repeated subword RoPE positions; the claim that “starts are causal” requires prefix stability of the actual tokenizer. A one-byte shift of offline BPE end flags does not prove that stability either. A generic ranked-merge counterexample can refute universal prefix stability, but does not measure leakage in a particular released tokenizer [BIW-0108 / BIP-0117/0118,0270; v2 §5.3–5.4, card](../03_reading/R08/BIW-0108.md); [BIW-0078, §4.3/Appendix D.1, card](../03_reading/R09/BIW-0078.md).

AU-Net selects contextual segment-end states under an externally specified regex, contracts them into larger multiword stages, and expands a state across the **following** segment. Its longer predictive horizon is induced by this timing under ordinary next-byte supervision, not by a separate multiword output loss. Its stated right-insertion-stability requirement is appropriate, but the complete regex's actual streaming behavior remains unverified; causal attention alone cannot certify it [BIW-0077 / BIP-0108; v1 §§2.1–2.2/Appendix B, card](../03_reading/R10/BIW-0077.md).

### 5.3 Refresh need not mean another permanent patch

Scratchpad Patching addresses delayed global context directly. At a consumed prefix, it pools only the observed partial patch and runs the trunk without committing another persistent state. Training masks let a scratchpad attend to itself and prior committed patches, but not other scratchpads; old scratchpads can therefore be discarded rather than becoming a hidden recurrent chain. At p=16, average NLU rises from 48.0 to 54.2, while validation FLOP reduction relative to flat byte narrows from 5.7× to 2.9×. Quality is purchased with refresh work even when permanent patch count stays fixed. H-Net patchifier variants can regress under matched-training-FLOP comparisons, and dense refresh can be slightly worse on natural language [BIW-0076 / BIP-0105–0107; v1 §§3–5/Table 1/Appendix E, card](../03_reading/R05/BIW-0076.md). Persistent cache, transient workspace, and total compute must consequently be accounted for separately.

## 6. Patch latents do not determine the output joint

### 6.1 Autoregressive recovery: dependence without a giant block vocabulary

For a fixed k-byte block, a flat categorical classifier would require up to 256ᵏ outcomes. Independent byte heads avoid that explosion but cannot express dependencies between alternatives. MEGABYTE instead uses a small causal Transformer to parameterize

`p(block | past) = product_j p(byte_j | past blocks, earlier bytes in this block)`.

This is a dependent normalized byte joint under its local autoregressive construction, with serial local sampling. Global processing is amortized across the patch; the number of emitted byte decisions is not reduced. Patch-tail loss and offset effects reveal where the small local model assumes more responsibility. Optional cross-patch context and shifted scoring address different aspects of this limitation; the best quoted scoring configuration costs four basic scoring passes [BIW-0008 / BIP-0015,0169/0170; v2 §2.3–2.4/Table 8, card](../03_reading/R03/BIW-0008.md).

BLT gives its local decoder cross-attention to processed patch states and fine byte-encoder information, while SDD uses a recurrent byte upsampler. These are expressive recovery mechanisms, not proof that a patch is a self-contained lossless code. MEGABYTE's paper explicitly shares the local byte input embedding with its output projection; LCA instead shares one unembedding **between two heads**. Neither fact determines BLT/H-Net input–output tying, which remains unknown in the examined scope [BIP-0284](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0284), [BIP-0032](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0032).

### 6.2 More targets can improve states without defining a sampler

Deep self-attention work already combined all-position causal supervision, temporary intermediate-layer classifiers, and two-step-ahead auxiliary targets. Only the immediate next-byte classifier remained at inference. Its text8 ablations show a much larger deterioration without all-position supervision than without the extra future target. This historical example separates optimization density from an output-generation algorithm [BIW-0064 / BIP-0096–0098; v2 pp.2–4/Table 4, card](../03_reading/R09/BIW-0064.md).

LCA's dynamic multi-byte decoder makes the distinction particularly concrete. The normal next-byte head uses byte residuals; the MBP Transformer receives repeated **previous-segment** latent information, with segment-aware attention. Earlier MBP slots are not previously sampled byte identities. Therefore the parallel candidates are conditional predictions from shared past information, not a locally autoregressive joint merely because their hidden slots attend causally. Its weighted next-byte/MBP/rate losses jointly train approximately 373M-parameter systems on 50B FineWeb-Edu bytes [BIW-0015 / BIP-0032; v2 §§2–4, card](../03_reading/R05/BIW-0015.md).

LCA's confidence policy accepts the first next-byte output and then the left-to-right candidate prefix above a threshold. That is approximate bulk emission, not target-law-preserving sampling. Lowering the threshold raises acceptance and throughput but reduces mean task quality; increasing candidates from three to seven can even reduce DailySum throughput from 210.42 to 164.18 bytes/s at τ=.75. Parameter matching does not equal training-FLOP matching, and accepted fraction does not measure coherence (Figure 4/Appendix B Table 3).

### 6.3 Normalized circuits and denoising blocks

MtPC addresses candidate dependence explicitly. A context representation parameterizes normalized categorical leaves, normalized nonnegative sums, and decomposable products. Mixture, HMM, and binary-tree structures introduce different dependence: a latent component can coordinate several byte positions; a tree can sample branches in parallel by level. Smoothness and decomposability enable tractable marginals and conditionals, allowing a dependent draft's prefix probabilities to be computed for verification. This is a concrete use of probabilistic-circuit structure, not simply additional parallel softmax heads [BIW-0079 / BIP-0171–0173; v2 §3/Appendix B, card](../03_reading/R05/BIW-0079.md).

Its overlapping-window, discounted training objective is composite likelihood, not a once-per-byte chain NLL. Expressiveness also has substantial cost: at a 16-byte window, the paper lists roughly 21M output-projection parameters for a factorized head versus about 730M for rank-32 BTree. At eight bytes on L40S, rank-32 CP reaches 191.0 bytes/s against factorized 176.3, but rank-128 accepts more and slows to 182.3. HMM can similarly accept more than BTree yet run slower. These batch-1 measurements exclude prefill and suppress EOS; they are not full request-latency or variable-termination measurements (Tables 1–2/Appendix K).

Fast BLT combines an autoregressive path with masked denoising over future byte blocks, while Block Diffusion is a **subword-token comparator** that denoises each current block conditional on clean completed blocks. Within-current-block bidirectional noisy attention is legal for its block-generative process; it is not left-to-right byte causality. Block Diffusion optimizes a sum of negative evidence lower bounds, an upper bound on NLL, and spends multiple network evaluations denoising. More parallel positions do not mean one-pass exact ancestral samples [BIW-0014 / BIP-0029; v1 §3, card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B14/W0014.md); [BIW-0089 / BIP-0183; v3 §§3–5/Appendix B, card](../03_reading/R05/BIW-0089.md).

## 7. Verification: which output is preserved?

Blockwise Parallel Decoding makes independent greedy proposals and verifies them under a causal scorer, accepting the longest matching prefix. This preserves that scorer's greedy sequence. Importantly, its inserted branches can transform the primary scorer itself, even when original weights are frozen; preservation is not necessarily of the unmodified pretrained baseline. Sequence-level beam distillation reduces variation in training targets and improves agreement, adding a teacher and another training stage. Top-k or forced-minimum-block acceptance relaxes exact equality and changes the policy. Its subword translation experiment reaches a 3.3× wall-time peak at k=8; k=10 accepts more but is slower [BIW-0082 / BIP-0177–0179; v1 §§3–7/Figure 4, card](../03_reading/R05/BIW-0082.md).

Stochastic speculative decoding needs a stronger correction. Writing p for the desired target distribution and q for the actual proposal, a draft x sampled from q is accepted with `min(1,p(x)/q(x))`. Upon rejection, sampling from normalized `(p−q)+` restores the missing mass. The identity is accepted mass `min(p,q)` plus residual mass `(p−q)+ = p`; the all-accepted branch may draw an ordinary target bonus. Temperature or top-p transforms belong **inside the distributions used by that ratio**, not outside an already proved identity. Leviathan et al. and Chen et al. independently develop this approach; their empirical systems use tokenizer tokens, not native byte interfaces [BIW-0083/0084 / BIP-0110; Algorithm 1/Appendix A.1 and Algorithm 2/Theorem 1, cards](../03_reading/R04/BIW-0083.md), [Chen card](../03_reading/R04/BIW-0084.md).

The target must always be named. Fast BLT's greedy verification preserves its corresponding autoregressive path, not necessarily the original BLT before denoising training. LCA self-verification targets its next-byte head; external verification targets a separately resident FlexiTokens model. LCA's 2.1–2.3× reported external speedups use experiments that explicitly **drop KV caching**, so they do not establish cached-serving gains [BIW-0015, §6.3/Appendix B](../03_reading/R05/BIW-0015.md). Fast BLT estimates weight-loading traffic from calls and parameter counts rather than measuring optimized wall time [BIW-0014, Eq.8/limitations](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B14/W0014.md).

**MtPC requires an additional qualification.** Its circuit supplies a normalized dependent draft, and Algorithm 3 specifies ordinary speculative residual correction. Optimized shared-state Algorithm 2 describes dropping the final verifier token after any proposal acceptance without distinguishing an all-accepted bonus from a post-rejection correction or specifying retained residual state. A literal discard-and-ordinary-restart interpretation need not preserve the stochastic target law. Exactness of that optimized wrapper remains unverified; this is not a demonstrated released-software bug and does not invalidate its reported throughput. The issue concerns correction/state composition, not the circuit's normalization [BIW-0079, v2 Appendix F pp.19–21, Algorithms 2–3; retained original extract](../03_reading/R05/sources/BIW-0079.txt), [current claim rule](../05_review/CLAIM_RULES.md#3-mtpc-exact-source-level-qualification-not-a-software-verdict).

## 8. Likelihood: changing the denominator does not change the event

For matching scored events, byte-normalized negative log probability is

`BPB_event = total_NLL_nats / (matching_original_bytes × ln 2)`.

A mean token cross-entropy additionally requires the effective scored token count. PAD, prompt exclusion, EOS, normalization, repeated windows, and auxiliary terms affect the numerator/denominator contract. A canonical token-path score can legitimately be normalized by original bytes when labeled as such; it does not become a marginal over all detokenizations.

Cao and Rimell estimate a sum over compatible tokenizations using a probabilistic-tokenizer proposal, importance weighting, and without-replacement stochastic beam search. Exact dynamic programming for the tokenizer proposal is not exact summation of a context-sensitive neural LM. The probability-space importance estimator and its logarithm have different bias properties; an n-best sum is a lower bound on the covered nonnegative path sum. Their reported perplexity uses whitespace words and does not fully specify a lossless raw-UTF-8 termination event [BIW-0080 / BIP-0109,0272/0273; official §§2–4, card](../03_reading/R04/BIW-0080.md).

An arbitrary raw-byte prefix asks a different question again. Exact Byte sums disjoint compatible token-prefix covers, including tokens that extend beyond the observed prefix, and obtains next-byte probabilities by ratios of prefix probabilities. Its exactness refers to a compatible lossless canonical-support process. An ordinary neural softmax can assign mass to noncanonical token sequences; masking and renormalizing them changes that original model. Its incremental reuse reduces redundant model calls but retains cover/vocabulary/cache work and does not create a native byte-trained network [BIW-0088 / BIP-0111,0182; v2 §4/Appendix A.6, card](../03_reading/R04/BIW-0088.md).

Boundary-marked distillation creates another distinct process. Breaking the Token Ceiling maps teacher-token probabilities into byte-plus-EOT events; a terminated alternative contributes EOT rather than being discarded and renormalized away. Conditional exactness given marked history is not an unmarked-prefix marginal, which must also account for continuations after hidden boundaries. The pseudocode does not show all required preprocessing, but caller inputs may already be expanded; that omission is not evidence that an implementation lacks EOT [BIW-0103 / BIP-0113; v1 §2.2/Appendix F, card](../03_reading/R06/BIW-0103.md), [current qualification](../02_inventory/AUTHOR_HANDOFF.md#3-confirmed-corrections-the-author-must-use).

TokSuite illustrates why these distinctions affect comparisons. Its causal arms use matching tokenizer-specific outputs, including a byte-tokenizer arm, not original ByT5 checkpoints. **V2 C.2 describes BPB as CE divided by log₂|V|, which is not a general bits-per-original-byte conversion. The actual run→log→Figure 6 transformation remains unknown. Figure 6 therefore cannot certify cross-tokenizer equal BPB or equal training sufficiency here.** This does not invalidate its separately defined option-ranking or four-tokenizer source-text control. The additional exact-byte option evaluation is Gemma-tokenizer-only on forty examples, not fourteen-way exact scoring [BIW-0051 / BIP-0286; PDF p.21 C.2; authoritative correction](../05_review/CLAIM_RULES.md#4-toksuite-exclude-an-unverified-figure-inference-not-the-whole-study).

## 9. The remaining training–generation gap

Several distinct gaps survive successful compression training. First, teacher-forced parallelism assumes observed preceding outputs; deployment must obtain them. Independent future heads, autoregressive local decoders, circuit drafts, and iterative denoisers pay different prices for that missing information. Second, offline grouping can be available during prefill but not at the emission frontier. Bolmo and the token-to-byte distillation study explicitly address this by jointly predicting byte and boundary; the latter also tests a future-byte proxy rather than reading a true future byte. Their early alignment stages, teacher-token outputs, and final byte outputs are different interfaces, not interchangeable evidence [BIW-0097, v2 §§3.1–3.2; card](../03_reading/R06/BIW-0097.md), [BIW-0098, v1 §§3–4; card](../03_reading/R06/BIW-0098.md).

Third, training savings need not become serving savings. H-Net's implementation can train about twice as slowly as an isotropic comparator despite favorable FLOP accounting, with variable-length batching and asynchronous sample updates remaining difficult. MEGABYTE's 8,192-byte generation example falls from 132 to 93 seconds—29.5% less elapsed time, or about 1.42× rate—but changes model size, original context, and quality, and does not supply a complete hardware/batch/cache contract. BLT's theoretical main-model FLOPs omit a separately itemized entropy-model forward and its training/preprocessing lifecycle. These are different, informative measurements, not entries in one controlled speed ranking [H-Net §4](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B05/W0011.md), [MEGABYTE §8.1/Table 6](../03_reading/R03/BIW-0008.md), [BLT Appendix H/§10](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B05/W0010.md).

A decisive comparison should consequently match or explicitly vary original-text exposure, original-byte context, realized group rate and length distribution, trunk/local/head parameters, teacher work, training FLOPs, and target quality. Serving should report device, precision, batch/concurrency, prompt/output bytes, prefill, persistent and transient memory, and the exact generation policy. Longer drafts, greater compression, or fewer persistent states are useful mechanisms to measure—not substitutes for those measurements.

The clearest open problems follow from the mechanisms rather than a preferred architecture. Can a causal router learn **incremental computational value** instead of an uncertainty proxy while retaining cheap deployment and stable rates? Can boundary and aggregation effects be separated under matched realized rates and representation capacity? Can large patches retain fresh global computation without excessive transient work? Can dependent parallel drafts stay small enough that improved acceptance pays for sampling and verification? Can partial prompts, group flushing, EOS, and incomplete UTF-8 prefixes be specified as carefully as training masks? Finally, multilingual mixed-script routing, long exact spelling/copy, first-complete-scalar latency, and matched-quality batched serving remain thinly evidenced. These are missing comparisons, not established universal failures.

The field's progress is therefore best understood as increasingly explicit control over **where expensive computation occurs and how output dependence is represented**. Differentiability is one tool; hard selection, rule-based clocks, dynamic programming, state-space/recurrent local processing, auxiliary supervision, probabilistic circuits, and rejection correction address different parts of the same systems problem. None alone resolves the full path from compressed training representations to faithful, efficient text generation.
