# Representation and history: how language models moved below the word

## 1. The field is about interfaces, not a single alphabet

Byte-level language modeling is best understood as a collection of decisions about where a system represents text, performs contextual computation, predicts events, and receives supervision. Moving below words can remove a training-derived lexical bottleneck, expose spelling regularities, or make one finite alphabet usable across scripts. None of those changes dictates the rest of the architecture. A model may compose bytes into externally segmented words, process every byte with a large network, allocate most computation to shorter latent sequences, or use bytes only as an intermediate language for transferring knowledge between tokenized models. These are different responses to different problems, not successive versions of one inevitable design.

This chapter follows those problem lineages: lexical coverage and parameter sharing; local composition and contextualization; long fine-grained sequences; learning useful internal units; and reusing pretrained systems across incompatible interfaces. Its organizing evidence is the stable [work catalog](../02_inventory/WORK_CATALOG.md), the granular [problem–solution–technique inventory](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md), and their [manual taxonomy](../02_inventory/TAXONOMY.md). The corpus contains 141 independent source entities and 296 traceable records, but the latter include mechanisms, controls, negative observations, and specifications—not 296 independent scientific problems or experiments. Ninety-two sources have scoped full-reading support. Historical character work and deliberately narrow ELMo/GPT-2 readings retain their own evidence limits rather than becoming raw-byte experiments by association ([counts](../02_inventory/COUNTS.md); [claim rules](../05_review/CLAIM_RULES.md)).

### 1.1 Which unit is actually being modeled?

A **byte** is an eight-bit storage unit. UTF-8 encodes a Unicode scalar value in one to four bytes; a byte prefix can therefore end inside a scalar. A **Unicode code point** is an index in the Unicode code space, not an encoded byte; valid UTF-8 excludes surrogate code points. A **grapheme cluster** approximates a user-perceived character and may contain multiple code points. The word *character* in older papers may instead mean an author-defined finite alphabet, sometimes after lowercasing, transliteration, or unknown-symbol substitution. **Subwords** are variable-length vocabulary entries; starting BPE from bytes makes coverage byte-based but does not make its model steps next-byte steps. These distinctions follow [RFC 3629 §§3–4, BIW-0090](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3629.html), [UAX #29 revision 47 §§1–3, BIW-0116](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr29/tr29-47.html), and the catalog's unit-specific entries.

Coverage and preservation also differ. An interface capable of encoding every byte string does not establish that its generator emits legal UTF-8, knows unseen scripts, or preserves original text after normalization. Canonical equivalence and compatibility normalization define still other targets: accepting normalized equivalents is not exact original-byte reconstruction ([UAX #15 revision 57 §§1.1–1.2, BIW-0115](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr15/tr15-57.html); [BIP-0218](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0218)). A serious comparison must identify four axes:

1. **Input units:** what is indexed before contextual processing?
2. **Internal positions:** what sequence or state receives the expensive computation?
3. **Output events:** bytes, characters, subwords, whole words, boundaries, or task labels?
4. **Supervision units:** which events, masks, teacher distributions, or alignments train the model?

## 2. Early recurrence: learning strings before eliminating vocabularies

The early recurrent literature supplied two enduring ideas: condition each next symbol on a learned state rather than a fixed-order context, and share statistical structure across symbol transitions. Sutskever, Martens, and Hinton's 2011 *Generating Text with Recurrent Neural Networks* used an input-conditioned, factorized multiplicative transition. The current character chooses a combination of shared low-rank transition components, allowing different symbols to transform the same history differently. Hessian-free optimization, Gauss–Newton curvature products, and structural damping addressed difficult recurrent gradients. This is a concrete architectural and optimization lineage, not an early learned tokenizer ([BIW-0052 card](../03_reading/R02/BIW-0052.md), official paper §§2–3, Eqs. 3–9; [BIP-0081–0082](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0081)).

Its alphabet was 85 permitted characters plus an unknown symbol, with corpus-specific transformations. Consequently, its bits per character are not likelihoods of arbitrary original UTF-8 text. The original Table 1 is also a useful corrective to triumphal histories: at roughly matched 100 MB training data, MRNN outperformed the sequence memoizer but lost to dictionary-disabled PAQ on all three reported corpora. Wikipedia scores were 1.60 versus PAQ's 1.51 bits per transformed character; the other two corpora had the same ordering. Learned distributed state was promising, but classical compression remained a serious comparator under that setup ([same card](../03_reading/R02/BIW-0052.md), §5/Table 1). Classical context mixing is only selectively covered here; this is not a complete history of compression.

Graves's *Generating Sequences With Recurrent Neural Networks* made the unit split especially visible. Its PTB experiments compared a 49-class character task with a 10,000-class word task, whereas its Wikipedia experiment consumed the 205 observed one-byte symbols, including components of multibyte encodings. Stacked LSTMs, skip connections, gradient clipping, and truncated backpropagation supported causal next-symbol learning. Forward state could persist substantially farther than the backward gradient horizon: the Wikipedia state continued across contiguous 100-byte training pieces, while gradients stopped at each piece ([BIW-0053 card](../03_reading/R02/BIW-0053.md), arXiv:1308.0850v5 §§2–3).

This work also distinguished *state adaptation* from *weight adaptation*. Its dynamic evaluation updated parameters on evaluation data; the large reduction from 1.67 static to 1.33 dynamic validation bits per byte on Wikipedia therefore changes the evaluation permissions, not merely the hidden-state cache. Neither its character/word comparisons nor the PAQ contest comparison equalized all parameters, data splits, or compute. The historical lesson is constructive: long state persistence, online learning, and input granularity can each improve a system, but they are separate interventions ([BIP-0083](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0083); source §3.2/Table 2).

Byte recurrence also became a representation-learning method. Radford, Jozefowicz, and Sutskever trained a 4,096-unit multiplicative LSTM on more than 38 billion Amazon-review bytes, then used frozen final cell states in supervised linear classifiers. Weight normalization, Adam, state persistence, and truncated gradients enabled a single-epoch run lasting about a month on four Pascal Titan X GPUs. Next-byte training produced useful sentiment features without sentiment labels in LM pretraining; downstream accuracy still used labeled readouts. Selected-unit interventions altered generated review sentiment, a stronger statement than simple feature correlation, but not a general disentanglement theorem ([BIW-0063 card](../03_reading/R02/BIW-0063.md), arXiv:1704.01444v2 §§3–4.5; [BIP-0091–0094](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0091)).

The transfer failures are part of the result. Its frozen features plateaued on large Yelp training sets, fell below an n-gram classifier there, and were less competitive on semantic relatedness and paraphrase. Domain mismatch, final-state bias toward document endings, and readout capacity were author hypotheses rather than isolated causes. Thus a universal input alphabet did not produce universal transfer. This early distinction—surface coverage versus functional reuse—returns in modern byteification.

## 3. Local composition: removing a lookup table without removing words

Character-aware word modeling developed alongside stream modeling because lexical parameter sharing and sequence length are different problems. Kim et al.'s *Character-Aware Neural Language Models* composed each already segmented input word using character embeddings, multi-width convolutions, max-over-time pooling, and highway layers. A word-time LSTM then predicted a fixed-vocabulary next word. CNN filters supplied reusable local spelling patterns; pooling formed one vector per word; highway gates regulated how much to transform or carry. External word boundaries and the fixed output vocabulary remained ([BIW-0055 card](../03_reading/R02/BIW-0055.md), arXiv:1508.06615v4 Model/Figure 1; [BIP-0085](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0085)).

The distinctions explain both benefits and limits. A composition function can produce a vector for an unseen spelling, but the reported target softmax still cannot name every unseen word; the experiments also applied UNK preprocessing. On PTB, the large character-input model achieved 78.9 word perplexity with about 19 million parameters versus 85.4 for the authors' roughly 20-million-parameter word-input baseline. That is evidence about input composition at word-time positions, not about raw-byte generation. Its approximately 60% parameter saving refers to a different published 52-million-parameter comparator, not that 20-million baseline ([same card](../03_reading/R02/BIW-0055.md), Tables 3–5).

The local-network ablation matters historically. On large PTB, no highway layer gave 84.6 perplexity, two highways 78.9, and an ordinary one-layer MLP 92.6. More nonlinear processing was not automatically better; the gated route was useful under that recipe. Composition also cost work: around 1,500 versus 3,000 word tokens per second in the reported character/lookup comparison. Caching word compositions could recover scoring speed at a memory cost. The problem had moved from storing unrelated lexical rows to repeatedly calculating lexical features, while the word-output expense remained ([BIP-0126–0127](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0126); source Table 7 and Further Observations).

ELMo extended this lineage from static composition to contextual transfer. A character-CNN/highway network creates word-position inputs, directional LSTMs contextualize them, and each downstream task learns a softmax-weighted mixture of layers plus a scale. The LM predicts words in the forward and backward directions; the combined downstream representation uses both contexts. Its directional LSTMs are separate, while representation parameters are shared *between directions* and softmax parameters are shared *between directions*. This is not a declaration that character input and word output are one tied matrix. These claims are restricted to the designated PARTIAL reading of arXiv:1802.05365v2 §§1–3.4/Table 1; exact UTF-8 serialization and the complete transfer evaluation were not examined ([BIW-0140 card](../03_reading/FIELD_ADDENDUM/arxiv_1802.05365v2.md); [BIP-0293–0294](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0293)).

CharacterBERT makes the hybrid even more explicit. It UTF-8-encodes each externally supplied word, uses a local byte CNN/max/highway composer, and runs BERT at word positions. Its MLM classifier predicts only the top 100,000 frequent words; words outside that set are never masking targets. Input composition is open-ended relative to a lexical lookup, yet capped local lengths and closed supervision remain. On the reported medical setup, pretraining took 55 hours versus BERT's 26.5, and ClinicalSTS reversed several favorable task trends. CharacterBERT therefore demonstrates a useful byte-composed *word encoder*, not a byte-stream generator or cost-free vocabulary removal ([BIW-0066 card](../03_reading/R01/BIW-0066.md), arXiv:2010.10392v3 §§3–5/Appendix A.1; [BIP-0136–0138](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0136)).

## 4. Whole-source processing and conditional generation

One of the earliest explicit multilingual UTF-8 interfaces was Gillick et al.'s *Multilingual Language Processing From Bytes*. A compact LSTM read 60-byte windows and emitted serialized span annotations: start, length, label, then STOP. This avoided a large cross-product classifier over every possible annotation triple. Byte dropout discouraged memorizing local strings; overlapping inference windows reduced segmentation-boundary problems. The approximately 900,000-parameter model shared recurrent parameters between reading and annotation phases and across languages within each task. Its outputs were annotation events—not generated text bytes—and the complete window was visible before any annotation ([BIW-0057 card](../03_reading/R02/BIW-0057.md), arXiv:1512.00103v2 §§3–6; [BIP-0129–0131](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0129)).

This choice illustrates a recurring trade: simplifying lexical preprocessing can complicate output structure and window handling. Invalid or incomplete annotations were ignored rather than excluded by a formal output grammar; overlapping windows roughly doubled long-text input processing and did not guarantee arbitrary-span coverage. Multilingual improvements were not uniform, and the authors had no annotated code-switching test. Byte-level representability enabled common processing, but did not establish every multilingual behavior.

Character translation attacked the sequence-length problem directly. Lee, Cho, and Hofmann's source encoder applied width-one-to-eight convolutions, stride-five max pooling, four highway layers, then a bidirectional GRU; an attention-based target GRU generated characters. Overlapping convolutional receptive fields could capture patterns across fixed pooling-cell boundaries. The pooling grid itself was not learned variable segmentation. Moreover, the finite source alphabets, Moses processing, and Russian ISO-9 transliteration make this a character antecedent rather than raw-byte evidence ([BIW-0059 card](../03_reading/R03/BIW-0059.md), arXiv:1610.03017v3 §4/Figure 1; [BIP-0086–0088](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0086)).

The multilingual character system improved all four reported test2 directions relative to its multilingual BPE-to-character comparator, but bilingual comparisons included Finnish and Russian reversals. Character and BPE length filters also retained different examples, and multilingual training added English target exposure. Those conditions identify the actual achievement: trainable local composition can support competitive full-source translation, not proof that one alphabet or pooling operator dominates independently of data and capacity.

ByteNet offered another route. Dilated convolutions expanded the receptive field while preserving resolution; causal target convolutions enabled parallel teacher-forced training without recurrent target transitions. Its translation experiment used finite English/German character alphabets, whereas its Hutter language-model experiment concerned byte-valued Wikipedia symbols. The claimed linear-time structure is a fixed-depth operation count, not parallel ancestral output emission or a measured long-context serving frontier. Attention-like saliency patterns are likewise not an implemented attention mechanism ([BIW-0058 card](../03_reading/R02/BIW-0058.md), arXiv:1610.10099v2 §§2–6; [BIP-0132](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0132)).

Later UTF-8 translation research revisited how much local structure must be supplied. Local Byte Fusion's convolutional arm mixes local byte patterns and restores the original byte length; its word-span arm restricts attention using externally provided spans. Neither necessarily shortens the sequence passed upward. This separates *feature fusion* from *position reduction*. Lao-to-English remained below the subword baseline, and a training-time advantage in one fixed-step comparison coexisted with slower inference and no equal-epoch advantage ([BIW-0048/W0514 card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B26/W0514.md), arXiv:2205.11490v3 §3/Table 3/Appendix B; [BIP-0070–0073](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0070)).

Subword-Delimited Downsampling takes the opposite stance: retain byte input/output but borrow SentencePiece boundaries for variable groups, CNN/max-pool those groups, and recover bytes with a local autoregressive LSTM and end marker. Its comparison of fixed, buffered-fixed, word-delimited, and subword-delimited groups exposed bundled effects of position, length, and lexical grouping. Average translation gains did not survive every language, out-of-domain test, or high-resource comparison; SDD still took 2.61 times the subword training time per iteration in its reported setup. The interface was byte-valued, but the grouping was explicitly hybrid ([BIW-0075 card](../03_reading/R03/BIW-0075.md), Findings EMNLP 2022 §§3–8/Tables 3–9/Appendices C–D; [BIP-0168](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0168)).

A particularly revealing control comes from *Neural Machine Translation without Embeddings*. Its UTF-8 model replaces learned lexical tables with fixed one-hot input/output coordinates while retaining learned Transformer projections and separate scalar scales. With the original recipe it appeared better than learned byte embeddings in 19 of 20 directions. But elementwise dropout on a one-hot decoder input can erase the entire token, unlike dropout on a dense embedding. After explicitly allowing token dropout in the controls, the advantage largely disappeared and fixed bytes were worse on average into English. The paper does not fully specify unused output-coordinate and special-symbol handling, so even “embedding-free byte softmax” needs its exact contract. This is evidence that changing representation also changes regularization—not evidence that learned byte embeddings are intrinsically unnecessary ([BIW-0068 card](../03_reading/R01/BIW-0068.md), NAACL 2021 §§2–6/Tables 2–4; [BIP-0102/0139](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0139)).

## 5. Full-resolution byte models are an independent historical route

The success of local pooling should not make hierarchy seem necessary. Al-Rfou et al.'s deep self-attention model retained one position per byte and used a 256-class next-byte head. Its optimization combined all-position causal targets, temporary intermediate-layer losses, and auxiliary two-step-ahead classifiers; only the immediate next-byte classifier remained at inference. Auxiliary future prediction trained representations but did not define a parallel multi-byte sampler. Text8's restricted 27-character alphabet and enwik8's raw-byte stream must remain separate despite both being called character modeling ([BIW-0064 card](../03_reading/R09/BIW-0064.md), arXiv:1808.04444v2 §§2–4; [BIP-0095–0099](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0095)).

Its 64-layer model reached 1.06 bits per byte on enwik8, with a costly protocol that recomputed a 512-byte window for every scored byte. The later *Bridging the Gap for Tokenizer-Free Language Models* scaled a plain 40-layer Transformer to about 836 million parameters. On processed lm1b it reached 0.874 BPB, re-expressed as 23.0 word perplexity using the same corpus's byte/word counts. This countered a simple claim that byte stepping intrinsically prevents competitive large-data likelihood, without establishing equal compute to the cited word systems. The corpus already contained tokenization and UNK substitutions, so the result is not open-vocabulary likelihood on untouched source documents ([BIW-0071 card](../03_reading/R09/BIW-0071.md), arXiv:1908.10322v1 §§2–3/Table 2; [BIP-0142–0143](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0142)).

Several efficiency lineages preserve positions instead of replacing them. Transformer-XL caches prior segment activations under stopped gradients and uses relative positions to distinguish distance across segment boundaries. Sparse Transformer restricts connectivity through fixed causal patterns. Reformer uses locality-sensitive hashing for approximate content-based connectivity, reversible residual blocks for activation reconstruction, and position-wise feed-forward chunking for smaller intermediate tensors. These modify accessible edges, forward history, or training memory—not learned text segmentation ([BIW-0119 card](../03_reading/R09/BIW-0119.md), ACL 2019 §3 and separately scoped arXiv-v3 appendices; [BIW-0118 card](../03_reading/R09/BIW-0118.md), arXiv:1904.10509v1 §§3–5; [BIW-0139 card](../03_reading/FIELD_ADDENDUM/arxiv_2001.04451v2.md), arXiv:2001.04451v2 §§2–3).

Their controls prevent a generic efficiency story. Sparse Transformer's dense/fixed/strided enwik8 comparison gave 1.00/0.99/1.13 BPB: the fastest reported iteration configuration did not preserve quality. Reformer could train large models for which equally large full-attention controls were unavailable. Transformer-XL's celebrated 1,874-fold comparison was against uncached full-window rescoring at long attention length, not modern matched-quality batched serving. Reversible training activations do not erase inference KV state, and execution chunks do not become linguistic units ([BIP-0236–0238](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0236), [BIP-0290–0292](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0290); source locations in the linked cards).

Adaptive Computation Time supplies a further orthogonal axis: compute *within* one byte. A halting unit weights repeated recurrent transitions at each external input position; a ponder penalty trades task loss against additional transitions. Its Wikipedia model consumes one byte and predicts the next, without shortening the stream. However, the reported byte errors are training-set errors and were fairly similar with and without ACT. Qualitative peaks at spaces and punctuation are not validated semantic boundaries. The gradient ignores stopping-count discontinuities, and ponder is not measured latency ([BIW-0138 card](../03_reading/FIELD_ADDENDUM/arxiv_1603.08983v6.md), arXiv:1603.08983v6 §§2.2/3.5; [BIP-0287–0289](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0287)). This route shows why uncertainty, computational difficulty, and segment length should not be collapsed into one quantity.

MambaByte returns to a recurrent alternative at full byte resolution: selective state-space transitions condition their state update and readout on the current input, while each layer retains a fixed-size state. There is no required patch sequence or attention cache over all past bytes. Its reported near-per-byte-FLOP comparisons with MEGABYTE were favorable on several corpora but used different training byte totals, so they do not isolate architecture under equal exposure. Its subword-drafter acceleration is a separate hybrid inference policy; the reported top-three verification rule does not establish exact target-law sampling. Full-resolution byte processing, fixed-state memory, and token-assisted generation can therefore coexist in one system without becoming learned segmentation ([BIW-0025/W0352 card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B14/W0352.md), arXiv:2401.13660v3 §§2–3/Appendix F; [BIP-0044–0046](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0044)).

## 6. Pretrained encoders and latent workspaces

ByT5 established a particularly simple alternative to learned tokenization: retain UTF-8 bytes, use a small lexical interface, and pretrain an encoder–decoder on corrupted-span reconstruction. Its encoder sees the entire corrupted source; its decoder autoregressively emits missing byte spans. Increasing average corruption span length to 20 bytes rather than mechanically copying a subword setting helped keep the task meaningful at the finer resolution. A roughly three-to-one encoder/decoder layer allocation placed more capacity on source processing. These are objective and compute-placement choices, not consequences of the byte alphabet alone ([BIW-0024/W0351 card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B08/W0351.md), TACL 2022 §§3/6; [BIP-0037–0043](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0037), [BIP-0246](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0246)).

ByT5's spelling-sensitive task strengths and noise results coexist with material costs. Equal 1,024-position inputs meant much less original text per byte-model example than per mT5 example. At the reported pretraining setup, ByT5 throughput was approximately three-quarters of mT5's. Task latency varied greatly: XNLI could be substantially slower despite short label outputs, so that timing cannot be attributed solely to long generation. The paper decoded with invalid bytes ignored, not a UTF-8-validity guarantee. These details explain why the contribution is broad byte-to-byte pretraining rather than universal byte superiority.

CANINE represents another solution to lexical coverage. It takes Unicode code points, embeds them through multiple learned hash tables, locally contextualizes them, and uses stride-four convolution before the deep Transformer. Sequence tasks restore fine positions through repeated coarse states, concatenated early representations, convolution, and final attention. CANINE-C predicts bucketed code-point labels under shuffled masked-target autoregression; CANINE-S uses temporary subword supervision. Neither is a left-to-right raw-byte generator. The input vocabulary can disappear at deployment while its training targets remain vocabulary-dependent ([BIW-0004 card](../03_reading/R01/BIW-0004.md), arXiv:2103.06874v4 §3/Figures 1–2; [BIP-0003–0005](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0003)).

Its n-gram rescue also reconnects the field to lexical memorization. CoNLL macro F1 rose from 74.0 for CANINE-C to 86.7 with hashed n-grams, against mBERT's 87.8; parameters increased from 127 to 167 million. The result argues against treating fully compositional input as automatically sufficient. The corrected headline comparisons refer to different arms and metrics: CANINE-S SELECTP improves by 2.8 points, while CANINE-C+n-grams MINSPAN improves by 5.7. They are not alternative reports of the same effect (source Tables 2/5 and [claim rules §2](../05_review/CLAIM_RULES.md)).

Perceiver IO makes the workspace independent of text partitions. Latent queries cross-attend to input bytes; a fixed latent array receives deep self-attention; output queries select task- or position-specific readouts. Its language experiment used byte or subword inputs, whole-word mask selection, and masked-symbol prediction from a fully corrupted-source encoding. Latent slots are not contiguous byte groups, and parallel output queries are not a causal byte decoder. At approximately matched reported operations, byte Perceiver achieved 81.0 GLUE versus byte BERT's 71.5, but used 201 versus 20 million parameters. Narrowing the number of slots while widening them could hurt despite similar FLOPs: compact workspaces are not interchangeable solely by operation count ([BIW-0117 card](../03_reading/R01/BIW-0117.md), arXiv:2107.14795v3 §3/§4.1/Appendix F Tables 11–15; [BIP-0121–0123](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0121)).

## 7. Learning local units: several mechanisms, several objectives

Charformer's Gradient-Based Subword Tokenization softly mixes candidate block sizes at each position before a final fixed-stride reduction. Candidate means, learned scores, repetition back to fine positions, and convolution supply local composition; they do not sample one coherent variable-length segmentation. MANTa instead predicts frontier probabilities and approximates cumulative byte-to-block assignments, using depthwise convolution and max pooling to retain order-sensitive features. Both train on byte denoising with a full corrupted source, not inherently prefix-causal grouping ([BIW-0005 card](../03_reading/R01/BIW-0005.md), arXiv:2106.12672v3 §2.1; [BIW-0006 card](../03_reading/R01/BIW-0006.md), arXiv:2212.07284v1 §3/Appendix D; [BIP-0006–0007](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0006)).

MANTa's capacity constraint is mechanistically important: without its block-count bottleneck, the model often settled near one block per byte. Task loss alone did not reliably request useful compression. Conversely, borrowing Charformer's encoder front end for generation required more than a causal attention mask. *Patching Leaks in the Charformer* diagnosed information flow through convolution and overlapping candidate windows, then removed or masked those paths. Extra padding could remove leakage yet nearly eliminate useful translation learning. Correct visibility and adequate predictive capacity were separate requirements ([BIW-0074 card](../03_reading/R03/BIW-0074.md), arXiv:2205.14086v1 §§2–3/Tables 1–3; [BIP-0154](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0154), [BIP-0166–0167](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0166)).

Earlier hierarchical multiscale RNNs learned boundary-controlled UPDATE, COPY, and FLUSH operations, with biased straight-through gradients. Their upper-state clock could slow without changing the next-symbol output. But plausible space-adjacent boundary pictures did not establish word discovery. Segmental neural LMs attacked that objective explicitly: mix a character spelling generator with lexical memory, marginalize permissible segmentations through dynamic programming, and regularize posterior lengths. Their character/phoneme experiments are antecedents, not raw-byte findings; full-sentence MAP segmentation also differs from online generation ([BIW-0016 card](../03_reading/R03/BIW-0016.md), arXiv:1609.01704v7 §3; [BIW-0042 card](../03_reading/R03/BIW-0042.md), ACL 2019 §§2–4/7; [BIP-0145–0149](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0145)).

Causal Transformer hierarchies then combined fine-scale output with coarse-scale context. Hourglass shortens and later restores the stream; its additional shift must cover all shortening operands. Without fine-resolution Transformer layers, the shortened branch lacks current-group history, although the one-step-shifted residual can still carry the immediately preceding symbol. The appendix demonstrates a specific copying restriction, not blanket within-group independence. MEGABYTE instead concatenates fixed byte patches for a global Transformer and autoregresses locally inside each patch; a patch shift and byte shift make the two conditional histories explicit ([BIW-0072 card](../03_reading/R03/BIW-0072.md), arXiv:2110.13711v2 §2/Appendix A.1, with [corrected rule §6](../05_review/CLAIM_RULES.md); [BIW-0008 card](../03_reading/R03/BIW-0008.md), arXiv:2305.07185v2 §2.2).

The alternatives remain materially different. BLT uses a separately trained causal entropy model to propose patches and cross-attention to aggregate byte states. H-Net selects contextual states rather than necessarily averaging a span, with smoothing and rate objectives stabilizing routing. MrT5 deletes contextualized encoder positions and continues from survivors; that is neither raw-byte pruning before contextualization nor guaranteed lossless merging. None of these mechanisms is identified merely by the phrase *latent bytes* ([BLT BIW-0010/W0010](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B05/W0010.md), ACL 2025 §§2–4/Appendix H; [H-Net BIW-0011/W0011](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B05/W0011.md), arXiv:2507.07955v2 §2; [MrT5 BIW-0046/W0511](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B22/W0511.md), arXiv:2410.20771v3 §§3/5). Their task quality, rates, teachers, and practical execution costs belong in distinct comparisons.

## 8. Mixed interfaces and pretrained adaptation

### 8.1 Hybridization can occur at any stage

GPT-2's report explains a historically influential compromise: begin with all 256 bytes, learn frequent adjacent merges, and run a causal Transformer over merged pieces. Category restrictions prevent spending lexical entries on too many word-plus-punctuation variants, with an exception for spaces. The reported vocabulary has 50,257 entries and the context is 1,024 model tokens, not bytes. This account is supported only by the designated PARTIAL extraction of report p.4 §§2.2–2.3 and adjacent §3.1; its preliminary raw-byte quality observation is not a specified matched-compute frontier ([BIW-0141 card](../03_reading/FIELD_ADDENDUM/gpt2_official_report_2019.md); [BIP-0295–0296](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0295)).

Other hybrids preserve a lexical output more radically. T-FREE hashes overlapping trigrams of rule-split units, sums sparse input features, and predicts the next unit's multi-hot pattern. A candidate dictionary then scores possible whole-unit outputs. The training BCE is not ordinary word-softmax likelihood, and hashing an unseen input word does not enable unrestricted novel-word generation. Binary trigram sets can also lose multiplicity: the card's repeated-character counterexample concerns representational noninjectivity under the stated rule, not a reproduced software failure ([BIW-0127 card](../03_reading/R10/BIW-0127.md), arXiv:2406.19223v2 §§3.1–3.2/Algorithms 1–5; [BIP-0261](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0261)). Hybridization is therefore not a binary label: it can involve preprocessing, lexical features, internal clocks, supervision, or output support.

### 8.2 Transferring representations is not transferring behavior

Char2Subword exposes the adaptation problem before modern causal byteification. Its character Transformer/max-pool composer learns to imitate existing mBERT vectors using cosine, L2, neighborhood-distance, and token-classification losses. Noisy spellings are paired with the same target vector. Dynamic MLM then trains the composer through a frozen host and fixed subword classifier; hybrid deployment retains old embeddings for recognized full words and composes unseen words. Static similarity is only one stage: contextual adaptation changed neighborhood fidelity, and a later re-approximation mattered. The hybrid retained lookup storage and extra composition work, rather than realizing full replacement savings ([BIW-0122 card](../03_reading/R06/BIW-0122.md), arXiv:2010.12730v3 §3/Tables 2–5; [BIP-0247–0250](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0247)).

Zero-Shot Tokenizer Transfer generalizes interface construction with a hypernetwork. For each target token, a small Transformer composes its decomposition under the original tokenizer and predicts input and, where needed, separate output embeddings. A frozen host supplies task gradients while the hypernetwork trains across sampled vocabularies; tables can later be generated once. This is predominantly subword-to-subword transfer, not raw-byte generation. “Zero-shot” means no adaptation on that target tokenizer, not no hypernetwork training. Its untied GPT-2 experiment diverged without an embedding anchor, while the tied case did not; that specific result is not a universal law about tying ([BIW-0126 card](../03_reading/R06/BIW-0126.md), arXiv:2405.07883v2 §§2–4/Appendix B; [BIP-0255–0257](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0255)).

Bolmo moves to byte-input/byte-boundary-output conversion while retaining a subword-suffix input table. Local recurrent modules interface with a pretrained Olmo backbone. Stage one aligns representations after four frozen host layers, imitates teacher boundaries, and matches patch-level probabilities alongside byte CE; stage two unfreezes the model. Passing gradients through frozen layers tests functional compatibility beyond matching static embeddings. Equal local/global widths avoid the hard rank ceiling of a narrow linear interface, but this algebraic consideration does not prove that rank explains every empirical difference ([BIW-0097 card](../03_reading/R06/BIW-0097.md), arXiv:2512.15586v2 §3/Appendix E; [BIP-0206–0208](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0206)).

Its staging result should be read alongside, not merged with, *Distilling Token-Trained Models into Byte-Level Models*. The latter first aligns byte-encoder states, then distills teacher-token distributions, then trains the router, before replacing the token head and adding a byte decoder. Stage one still emits teacher-vocabulary tokens. In its equal-20-billion-byte comparison, staged alignment markedly outperformed direct distillation. Bolmo's different stage-two-only ablation, approximately compensated in global FLOPs, was not catastrophic. Together they establish recipe-dependent alignment problems, not a universal prerequisite for every converted model ([BIW-0098 card](../03_reading/R06/BIW-0098.md), arXiv:2602.01007v1 §§4/6.1/Table 3; [BIW-0097 §6.2/Figure 6](../03_reading/R06/BIW-0097.md); [BIP-0210–0211](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0210)).

Conversion also changes when boundaries become available. A prefill router may compare two already observed byte states, but generation cannot inspect an ungenerated future byte. Bolmo and the latter distillation system use joint byte/boundary prediction; the latter also studies a second-byte proxy. These are generated decisions, not access to the true future. Their explicit treatment is a useful progression from simply transplanting a bidirectional encoder front end into a decoder ([BIP-0212](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0212); the two cards' routing sections).

### 8.3 Bytes as a supervision language

A common byte space can bridge unequal teacher/student vocabularies without becoming the deployed alphabet. *Cross-Tokenizer LLM Distillation through a Byte-Level Interface* uses approximate teacher byte-prefix probabilities and temporary position-specific student byte heads, then ordinarily removes the interface. Its parallel student heads do not condition on previously sampled within-token bytes, so they are not an exact sequential byte decoder. In a separate actual token-to-byte conversion, instruction following and MMLU deteriorated substantially. Transfer through a common alphabet therefore does not guarantee preservation of the host's behavior ([BIW-0099 card](../03_reading/R06/BIW-0099.md), arXiv:2604.07466v1 §3/Tables 1–3; [BIP-0213–0214](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0213)).

*Breaking the Token Ceiling* instead trains dense byte or byte-plus-end-of-token students with converted teacher targets. The end marker preserves a teacher token-termination event that a simple within-token renormalization discards. Exactness conditioned on marked token history is not the same as an unmarked byte-prefix marginal; Appendix F's preprocessing is not shown, rather than proven omitted. Its observed curves favor subwords at low compute, and lower validation BPB did not always mean better downstream scores. The advertised larger asymptotic marked-byte gain is an extrapolation sensitive to validation set and fit choices, not an observed universal advantage ([BIW-0103 card](../03_reading/R06/BIW-0103.md), arXiv:2609.12303v1 §§2/5–6/Appendices D/F; [BIP-0113–0114](../02_inventory/DETAILED_INVENTORY.md#BIP-0113); corrected [claim boundaries](../02_inventory/AUTHOR_HANDOFF.md#4-citation-boundaries-that-remain-open)).

## 10. What the lineage establishes—and what remains open

Three conclusions organize this history. First, finer input units redistribute work rather than simply remove it. Word-table parameters can become local composition, more model positions, additional decoding steps, teacher alignment, or candidate scoring. Second, representation and objectives are coupled: word-local inputs can retain closed word targets; byte denoising can use word masks; byte-output models can inherit subword boundaries and checkpoints. Third, compression is one important route among recurrence, sparse connectivity, latent workspaces, deeper plain models, and within-position computation—not a prerequisite established by the literature.

Budgets decide which conclusion a comparison supports. Equal parameters do not equal equal dense capacity when lexical tables differ; equal positions do not equal original-text exposure; equal training FLOPs do not equal latency. The recent flat byte study BIW-0028 is especially instructive: its corrected backbone is a 3:1 Gated DeltaNet/Gated Softmax Attention hybrid, not an all-attention Transformer, and its byte parameter frontier coexists with a subword advantage at the reported training-FLOP frontier. Its fitted parameter convention excludes input/hash tables, so that number is not full resident storage ([BIW-0028/W0356 card](../../flued_multigranularity_survey_20261108/03_reading/B14/W0356.md), arXiv:2610.05978v1 §2/Appendices A–C, as corrected in [AUTHOR_HANDOFF §3](../02_inventory/AUTHOR_HANDOFF.md#3-confirmed-corrections-the-author-must-use)).

Likelihood units require equal care. For matching scored events, negative log probability in nats divided by matching original bytes and ln 2 gives a byte-normalized score. Changing that denominator does not turn a canonical token path into a completed-string marginal, a masked conditional into a causal chain, or a marked boundary process into unmarked text probability. Task accuracy, probe decodability, legal Unicode, intended characters, and exact original strings remain different outcomes. Matrix sharing likewise needs named axes: ELMo's directional sharing, MEGABYTE's paper-specified local byte input/output tying, and Reformer's Q/K projection sharing establish different facts. Unspecified sharing remains unknown, not inherited from a model family ([claim rules §§2/9](../05_review/CLAIM_RULES.md)).

The open research questions are therefore specific. How can local composition preserve rare exact forms without spending an expensive update on every byte? Which objectives teach useful internal units without making human word boundaries the only target? Can pretrained behavior survive a changed input geometry, internal clock, and output law simultaneously? How should mixed-script systems balance original-text context when UTF-8 expansion differs? What is the matched-quality cost of preprocessing, prefill, batched generation, and producing the first complete Unicode scalar? This corpus provides strong mechanisms and conditional results, but thin evidence for general mixed-script causal routing, grapheme-preserving long exact copy, non-ASCII numeracy, and modern tail-latency frontiers. Those are unresolved comparisons, not established impossibility results.

The most productive historical reading is thus plural: character antecedents contributed composition, recurrence, gating, and contextual transfer; raw-byte work established compact shared alphabets and direct symbol likelihoods; encoder and seq2seq work made fine-grained pretraining practical; hierarchical models relocated expensive computation; and adaptation work made interface compatibility itself a learning problem. The field's central question is no longer merely whether a tokenizer can be removed. It is which contracts between text, representations, computation, supervision, and output should be learned, fixed, or transferred—and what evidence shows that the resulting system works under the intended task and budget.
