## 1. Probability and generation contracts used to classify the records

The following are **analyst definitions**, not new author-attributed results or techniques. They make the individual contracts comparable without declaring equivalent models or budgets.

1. **Byte chain:** for fixed original bytes `b[1:T]` and context `c`, `p(b|c)=product_t p(b_t|c,b_<t)`. Bits per byte is `-log2 p(b|c)/T` only after the probability object, document/EOS treatment, original-byte denominator, window resets, masking and excluded special symbols are specified. A teacher-forced causal chain can be evaluated in parallel while its ancestral generation remains sequential.
2. **Dependent patch joint:** `q(b_{t+1:t+k}|prefix)` may use an intra-patch AR chain (MEGABYTE), a latent mixture/probability circuit (MtPC), or a diffusion sampling process. These are not the same distribution merely because each returns k bytes. A single categorical distribution over every k-byte string has `256^k` outcomes; a product of k categoricals is normalized but imposes conditional independence. Hidden-state communication without observing sampled sibling bytes does not automatically remove that independence.
3. **Auxiliary multi-offset objective:** summing future-position CE or overlapping-window losses is not automatically the NLL of one globally consistent original-text probability law. MtPC explicitly calls its overlapping objective composite log-likelihood. A next-byte head can still define a separately scored AR chain; report which head/policy is evaluated.
4. **Greedy versus sampling:** longest-prefix equality to target argmax preserves that target's greedy output, not its full sampling law. Exact speculative sampling additionally requires the appropriate proposal/target probability accounting and rejection/correction step. Top-k or confidence-threshold acceptance is a third, approximate policy. “Same quality” on task averages is not a distribution identity.
5. **Verifier identity:** `original BLT`, `new BLT-D AR path`, `LCA next-byte head`, `external FxT`, and `EvaByte STP head` are different reference models. Preservation is always relative to the named target; training may already have changed it.
6. **Three token/string events:** a canonical token path likelihood; the marginal over token sequences decoding to a completed text; and a byte-prefix event that may end inside an unfinished token are distinct. EOS and whether noncanonical token sequences have positive probability matter. The EMNLP 2021 marginalization study and the Exact Byte paper's consistent-tokenizer construction must not be merged without checking assumptions.
7. **Streaming:** byte availability, first complete Unicode scalar, first user-perceived grapheme, committed output burst and final text are different timestamps. A valid unfinished UTF-8 prefix need not be a valid completed UTF-8 string. Buffering, replacement, dropping invalid bytes and constraining/renormalizing the model distribution have different evaluation effects. This inventory has no common measured streaming benchmark.

## 8. Outstanding evidence, not invented closure

Selected missing full readings are prioritized in [READING_HANDOFF.md](READING_HANDOFF.md). The largest gaps are exact LCA tensor/target dependencies and cache policy; MtPC sampler/probability proof and timing setup; deterministic-tokenizer idealization versus positive neural mass on noncanonical paths; MEGABYTE evaluation/strided-generation accounting; full streaming metrics at scalar/grapheme boundaries.
